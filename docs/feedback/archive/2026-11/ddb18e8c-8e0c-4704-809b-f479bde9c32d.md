**frank890417** · 2026-11-17 00:45

Thanks — four separate things here, and they do not all land in the same place, so let me answer them one at a time instead of filing the whole thing as one idea.

**1. "there could be more different pages for different topics, like geography, history" — these already exist, and that is our problem, not yours.**

They are live right now:

- https://taiwan.md/en/geography/
- https://taiwan.md/en/history/
- https://taiwan.md/en/culture/
- https://taiwan.md/en/explore/ (all categories in one place)

All of them return 200. You were on the English homepage and still came away thinking they were missing, which means the homepage is not doing its job of telling you they are there. I checked the rendered English homepage: it links to individual category pages inside article cards, but there is **no standalone category navigation block** on it. So the only way you meet a category is by happening to click an article that belongs to one.

That is a real finding and it is more useful than the feature request it arrived as. Keeping this issue open against that, not against "build topic pages".

**2. "the small tabs could be more sorted and maybe have a slider"** — this is about the same surface as (1). Routing it together, since both are about how the homepage presents the shape of the library.

**3. Traditional Chinese font quality.** Worth separating from the rest: this site does load a webfont for headings, so if the body text is rendering in a poor fallback on your machine, that is a real rendering gap and I would like to know your OS and browser. Font choices across twelve languages also touch how the site looks overall, which is a maintainer call rather than a routine one.

**4. "where every image source is found".** Articles do carry their image sources — most depth articles end with an **Image sources** section listing each file, its origin and its licence, and inline captions usually name the source too. If you were reading an article where that was missing, that is a bug in that specific article and worth a separate report with the URL.

So: (1) and (2) stay open here as a homepage navigation issue, (3) needs your browser details plus a maintainer decision, (4) needs an article URL if you hit one without sources.

Thank you for writing all of this down — the most useful line in it was the one that turned out to be about something we got wrong.
