# CPBL: Thirty-Seven Years, a League That Nearly Died Twice and Came Back

> **30-second overview:** The CPBL was founded on October 23, 1989, and began play on March 17, 1990; in the opening game, the Uni-President Lions defeated the Brother Elephants 4–3. After the Black Eagles incident broke in 1996, the Times Eagles, Mercuries Tigers, and Wei Chuan Dragons withdrew in succession. Following the Black Mi incident in 2008, the Chinatrust Whales disbanded, and the Black Elephants incident erupted in 2009. Both times the league shrank back to four teams. The Wei Chuan Dragons returned in 2019, the TSG Hawks joined in 2022, and starting in 2024 the Taipei Dome brought single-game crowds to 40,000; in 2025 the regular-season average attendance surpassed 10,000 for the first time.

## October 26, Top of the Ninth Inning

Top of the ninth, two outs. Lin Kai-wei induced Chen Chun-hsiu to hit a ground ball; the moment the first baseman caught it and stepped on the bag, 38,178 people at the Taipei Dome threw red confetti into the field together. The Wei Chuan Dragons shut out the CTBC Brothers 6–0 in Game 6 of the Taiwan Series, winning the series 4–2 to claim the franchise's 6th championship — and their 2nd since reorganizing in 2019.[^32]

Starting pitcher Cristopher Mercedes retired the first 10 batters in order, not allowing a hit until the 6th inning. He threw 100 pitches over 8 innings, allowing only 2 hits and no runs while striking out 6, earning both the Taiwan Series MVP and Game 6 MVP honors. The offense scored first in the bottom of the 3rd on Chang Cheng-yu's timely hit, then added 5 runs in the bottom of the 8th on 4 consecutive hits plus Chiang Shao-hong's two-run homer.[^32]

The series was originally 3–0 before the CTBC Brothers rallied to make it 3–2, with a two-day rain delay due to a typhoon in between. The Wei Chuan Dragons' previous championship was in 2023; before that, you have to go back to the four titles from 1990 to 1999 — after which the team announced its dissolution.[^32]

## March 17, 1990: The First Pitch

The Chinese Professional Baseball League did not appear out of nowhere.

On December 31, 1987, the "Professional Baseball Promotion Committee of the Republic of China" was established, facilitated by Brothers Hotel Chairman Hong Teng-sheng, with Baseball Association Chairwoman Tang Pan-pan serving concurrently as convener and Hong Teng-sheng as executive secretary. On January 18, 1988, the committee announced its goal of establishing a professional league by 1990, with at least four teams.[^1]

On October 23, 1989, the "Chinese Professional Baseball League" held its founding assembly at the Brothers Hotel. The first four enterprises to invest in professional baseball teams were Wei Chuan, Uni-President, Mercuries, and Brothers.[^1]

On March 17, 1990, the inaugural season opened. The opening game featured the Uni-President Lions against the Brothers Elephants, with Sadaharu Oh throwing the ceremonial first pitch, and Hongye Little League player Yu Hung-kai invited to participate in the opening ceremony. TTV broadcast live, and Japan's NHK also simulcast the first day's two games via satellite. Uni-President won 4–3, with Wang Chun-liang hitting the first home run in CPBL history in the top of the fourth inning. In the other game that same day, the Mercuries Tigers shut out the Wei Chuan Dragons 3–0.[^1][^2]

From that day forward, "the inaugural year of professional baseball" became a periodization term in Taiwan baseball history.

## Peak: Seven Teams and Ten Million Attendance

From the inaugural year through the eighth year, the CPBL's attendance, player compensation, and social status all rose together. By 1997 (CPBL Year 8), cumulative attendance since the inaugural year surpassed ten million, with old ballparks renovated and new ones built successively.[^1]

The number of teams also grew. Beyond the original four, China Times, Jungo Construction, Hsin Nong Group, and Hotai Group joined in succession, bringing the 1997 single-season maximum to seven teams. Together with the four teams of the neighboring Taiwan Major League, the total number of professional baseball teams in Taiwan once reached eleven.[^1][^3]

But during this same period two hidden dangers were also planted: Sampo attempted to join several times but never succeeded, and a dispute over television broadcast rights led Sampo and Era Television to join forces and establish a second league. Star players were poached with high salaries, and the two-league competition formally began.[^1][^3]

## First Near-Death Experience: The Black Eagles and Three Teams' Departure

In 1996, a scandal involving professional baseball players gambling and throwing games came to light, known as the Black Eagles incident; afterward, the CPBL faced successive waves of game-fixing cases. [^4] The Taiwan High Court's later ruling determined that the thrown games occurred in that year. [^5] Mid-way through the 1997 season, prosecutors and investigators conducted large-scale questioning and arrests; nearly all of the Times Eagles' local players were taken away, and the team had to finish the second half with players loaned by other league teams. [^6] Fans' distrust of the games was directly reflected in attendance figures.

Then came team departures. The Times Eagles disbanded in September 1998. In 1998 and 1999, the league switched to a single-season format, but box office numbers still failed to improve. After the 1999 season ended, the Mercuries Tigers and Wei Chuan Dragons announced their disbandment one after another, returning the league to a four-team scale; in 2000, average attendance was only 1,676 per game. [^1][^4][^6]

From seven teams to four took only two years.

## Merger and Revival

In 2001, the 34th Baseball World Cup was held in Taiwan. The Chinese Taipei team recruited 15 professional players, and led by Chang Chih-chia and Chen Chin-feng, defeated Japan 3–0 to take third place — Taiwan's third third-place finish in Baseball World Cup history. Playing at home reignited fan enthusiasm. [^1][^7]

On October 25, 2002, the CPBL and Taiwan Major League signed a merger agreement. On January 13, 2003, the two leagues officially merged under the witness of Sports Affairs Council Chairman Lin Te-fu and President Chen Shui-bian; the TML's original four teams were consolidated into the Chinatrust Whales and First Financial Holdings Agan, which joined the league, and the league was renamed the "Chinese Professional Baseball League." [^8][^9]

On March 1, 2003, the first opening game after the merger was played at Tianmu Baseball Stadium. [^1]

## Second Near-Death Experience: Black Bears, Black Whales, Black Media, Black Elephants

The shadow of game-fixing did not disappear with the merger.

In July 2005, the Criminal Investigation Bureau arrested a La New Bears catcher, a Chinatrust farm team coach, a former Sinon Bulls interpreter, and gambling ringleaders — ten people in total. Multiple players and coaches were detained or released on bail, and subsequently fired by their clubs. This became known as the Black Bears Incident.[^1][^10] In August 2007, it was revealed that some Chinatrust Whales players had been threatened and enticed by an elected representative with gangland ties. This became known as the Black Whales Incident.[^1]

In October 2008, the MediaTek Dragons (formerly the Chinatrust Cobras) were suspected by prosecutors and investigators of having been purchased by a behind-the-scenes financier and gambling ringleaders who directly manipulated games. The league suspended and expelled the team. This became known as the Black Media Incident.[^1] On November 11, one month later, the Chinatrust Whales announced their dissolution, and the league returned to four teams.[^11] The Black Media case lasted until the High Court's second-instance ruling in 2013, where the behind-the-scenes financier was convicted of fraud and sentenced to two years, while the club operator's breach of trust charge was not upheld due to insufficient evidence.[^12]

On October 26, 2009, the day after the Taiwan Series ended, prosecutors and investigators launched a large-scale search, and multiple Brother Elephants players were summoned for questioning. This became known as the Black Elephants Incident.[^1][^13] The investigation concluded in February 2010, with 24 people indicted on charges of fraud and gambling.[^14]

From 2005 to 2009, almost every year saw a team embroiled in gambling allegations.[^13] The result was a sharp decline in attendance: total 2010 attendance fell nearly 30% from the previous year. Teams considered disbanding, and many players chose to remain in amateur leagues or go overseas directly.[^1]

## Taipei Dome Era

In 2012, after 17 years of operating the Sinon Bulls, the Sinon Group could no longer bear the losses and decided to cease team operations. On December 17 of that year, they sold the team to the E-United Group for NT$130 million, renaming it the EDA Rhinos. [^1][^15] The 2013 World Baseball Classic craze and "Manny Mania" brought professional baseball's popularity back up. [^1][^4]

The Brother Elephants announced a sale the same year; in 2014, a CTBC Financial Holding subsidiary took over, renaming the team the CTBC Brothers. In 2016, Fubon purchased the EDA Rhinos for NT$300 million, renaming them the Fubon Guardians. [^1][^16]

The league also expanded. On September 12, 2017, the Board of Directors approved the expansion rules: new teams must prepare NT$580 million (NT$360 million deposit, NT$120 million franchise fee, NT$100 million local baseball development fund), play in the minor league first after joining, then move up to the major league. [^17][^18] The Wei Chuan Dragons returned in 2019 and moved up to the major league in 2021. The TSG Hawks became the sixth team in 2022 and moved up to the major league in 2024. [^1][^19]

On March 30, 2024, the CPBL held its first-ever regular-season game at the Taipei Dome, drawing 28,618 attendees and setting a new single-game record for domestic professional baseball. [^20] On August 3, a CTBC Brothers home game attracted 34,506 people, breaking the record again. [^21] On September 21 and 22, Chou Szu-chi's retirement games both sold out at 40,000 attendees. [^22]

Over the entire 2024 season, league regular-season attendance reached 2,766,386, averaging 7,684 per game, both record highs. [^1][^23] The 38 regular-season games at the Dome averaged 20,689 attendees, and the CTBC Brothers became the first team to average over 10,000 per home game. [^24][^22] The 2024 Taiwan Series drew a total of 132,625 across five games, with the CTBC Brothers winning 4–1 to capture their tenth franchise championship. [^25]

The 2025 All-Star Game at the Dome sold out both nights, totaling 80,000 attendees. [^26] That year's regular season averaged 10,373 per game, breaking 10,000 for the first time, with 3,734,429 total attendees. [^4] The Liberty Times estimated the six teams' combined revenue exceeded US$100 million, approximately NT$3.1 billion. [^27]

On October 26, 2026, the Wei Chuan Dragons defeated the CTBC Brothers 4–2 in the Taiwan Series to win their sixth franchise championship, with a cumulative 230,615 attendees across six games. [^28][^29] At this point, the CPBL was a six-team league, and President Tsai Chi-chang's term runs until around March 2027. [^30][^31]

## People Standing on This Line

The league's numbers fit on a single page, but every continuation and every disappearance is borne by concrete human beings.

**The person on the mound**. In the championship-clinching game, Mesa threw 100 pitches over 8 innings, retiring the first 10 batters he faced before Kao Yu-chieh broke up the no-hit bid with the game's first hit in the 6th inning. His teammates scored 5 runs in the bottom of the 8th, allowing him to exit with a 6–0 lead and earning him both the Series MVP and Game MVP honors. [^32]

**The people in the field**. The game-winning RBI in Game 6 came from Chang Cheng-yu's timely hit in the bottom of the 3rd inning; he was ultimately named the Wei Chuan Dragons' Outstanding Player. For the CTBC Brothers, the Outstanding Player was import pitcher Mario Sanchez, who started Game 1 and took the loss after 7 innings and 3 runs allowed, relieved for 4.1 scoreless innings in Game 4 to keep the team alive, then started again in Game 6 on just 4 days' rest, going 5 innings. The losing side had people burning just as brightly. [^32]

**The people in the stands**. That day, 38,178 people packed the Taipei Dome. The first regular-season game at the same venue in 2024 drew 28,618; in 2025, the regular-season average attendance topped 10,000 for the first time, and the six teams' total revenue was estimated by the _Liberty Times_ at over US$100 million. Translate those numbers into the scene on the ground, and you get the crowd in the outfield waving thundersticks—how cheerleader culture became a reason to attend is the subject of another article, "Lee Chu-chen" [李珠珢](/en/people/lee-ju-eun). [^20][^23][^27]

**The people not on the list**. After the 1996 Black Eagles incident, the players who were questioned, taken away, and fired mostly never returned to the diamond; when the Wei Chuan Dragons disbanded in 1999, the team had just won three straight championships. Behind every year written in the preceding sections of this article, there is a group of people forced out of the sport, and their names will never appear on the championship roster. [^1][^6]
