# Taiwan Baseball Culture

On August 25, 1968, in a small school in Yanping Township, Taitung, a group of slender indigenous children faced the star team from Japan's minor league. No one expected that this seemingly lopsided game would shock the world with a score of 7-0, nor did anyone expect it to be the beginning of Taiwan's baseball legend.

The victory of Hongye Minor League was a way for a small island to declare its existence to the world. In an era when Taiwan faced setbacks in international politics, baseball became an important vehicle through which Taiwanese people proved themselves and established their identity. From then on, this sport originating from the United States took root in this land, eventually becoming Taiwan's "national sport."

## Baseball Comes to Taiwan: From Colonial Legacy to Cultural Identity

The history of baseball in Taiwan begins during the Japanese colonial period. In 1906, the first baseball team in Taiwan was established at the Provincial Government School (the predecessor of Jianuo Middle School). The Japanese brought a set of baseball culture—rules, disciplined training, and the cultivation of spirit—all transplanted together.

Interestingly, baseball, which was initially introduced as part of colonial rule, later became an important medium for Taiwanese people to establish their own identity. This cultural transformation is not unique in Taiwan's history; foreign elements are often localized and eventually become a part of oneself.

The localization of baseball in Taiwan can be seen from the composition of its players. Indigenous players hold a special status in the history of Taiwanese baseball, with the Bunun children of Hongye Minor League being one of the most well-known examples.

## The Hongye Legend: The Beginning of an Era

### The Baseball Dream of Mountain Children

Hongye Elementary School, located in Yanping Township, Taitung County, is a typical mountain school. When the Hongye Minor League team was established in 1960, these Bunun children practiced with self-made wooden bats and collected baseballs in a rocky open area.

After Principal Lin Chu-peng took office in 1963, he noticed that students had low attendance and little interest even when they did come. He came up with an idea: to form a baseball team so the children would like coming to school to play ball while learning. This seemingly simple idea unexpectedly ushered in a golden age for Taiwanese baseball.

### The World-Shocking Victory

On August 25, 1968, a star Japanese minor league team visited Taiwan. This team consisted of elite players from the Kansai region and was expected to win easily. However, the children of Hongye displayed astonishing skill; not only did they achieve a decisive 7-0 victory in the first game, but they won again the very next day.

This result shocked all of Taiwan. Newspapers made major headlines, radios played it repeatedly, and suddenly all of Taiwan knew about a place called "Hongye" where children were bringing glory to Taiwan. Chiang Ching-kuo visited Hongye four times between 1975 and 1980, stating that "the whole world knows that Free China has Hongye Elementary School."

### The Golden Dragon Team's World Championship

The victory of Hongye inspired all of Taiwan. In 1969, Taiwan formed the "Golden Dragon Minor League Team" to participate in the World Minor Baseball Championship held in Williamport, USA. The Golden Dragon team advanced through successive rounds and won the championship, securing Taiwan its first world title.

The significance of this championship far exceeded sports itself. At a time when Taiwan had just withdrawn from the United Nations and faced difficult international circumstances, these children showed the world what Taiwan was like. Baseball became a way for Taiwan to speak on the international stage, extending far beyond mere athletics.

## The Golden Age of Minor League Baseball

The success of Hongye Minor League sparked a baseball fever across Taiwan. The government promoted minor league baseball (Minor, Youth, Junior), and various cities formed representative teams, while schools began systematically cultivating baseball players.

From the 1970s to the 1980s, Taiwan performed brilliantly in international youth baseball competitions, often winning first or second place. The outstanding performance of these young athletes brought honor to Taiwan and cultivated a large pool of baseball talent, which later formed the foundation for the establishment of the Chinese Professional Baseball League (CPBL).

The development of minor league baseball also fostered a unique Taiwanese baseball training system, with school teams at its core; many professional players in subsequent years started from this base.

## The Birth of Professional Baseball: A Historical Moment in 1990

### The Arrival of Pro Baseball

On October 23, 1989, the Chinese Professional Baseball League (CPBL) was officially established, with the four corporations—Wei-chuan, Uni-ty, San-song, and Brothers—becoming the founding teams. On March 17, 1990, the first game in CPBL history began, officially entering the "Year One of Pro Baseball."

The establishment of professional baseball marked an important milestone in Taiwan's transition from amateur to professional baseball. It was also a symbol of Taiwan's social and economic development—Taiwan finally possessed sufficient economic strength to support professional sports.

### Early Enthusiasm and Challenges

In the early days of CPBL, the enthusiasm of Taiwanese audiences was astonishing. Stadiums were often sold out, fan culture rapidly took shape, and each team had devoted supporters. The yellow whirlwind of Brothers and the green army of Uni-ty were household names in that era.

However, professional baseball also faced serious challenges. The match-fixing scandals and fake game incidents that erupted at the end of the 1990s severely damaged the image of CPBL, leading to a sharp decline in attendance and the dissolution of several teams. Taiwanese professional baseball entered its darkest period.

## Taiwan Baseball on the International Stage

### Glory in Asian Games and Olympics

In addition to professional baseball, amateur baseball in Taiwan also performed excellently in international competitions. In major events like the Asian Games and the Olympics, the Taiwanese national baseball team (under the name Chinese Taipei) frequently won medals, gaining international visibility for Taiwan.

Important tournaments such as the 2003 Asian Championships, the 2006 World Baseball Classic, and the 2008 Beijing Olympics all featured exciting performances from Taiwanese baseball players. These competitions were a place where the emotions of the Taiwanese people were invested; every major game garnered attention across Taiwan, transcending mere athletic competition.

### The Wang Chien-min Phenomenon

In 2005, when Wang Chien-min joined the New York Yankees, Taiwanese baseball found its place on the Major League Baseball stage. This right-handed pitcher from Tainan conquered the MLB with his two-seam fastballs and won 19 games in consecutive years, becoming a source of pride for the Taiwanese people.

Wang Chien-min's success boosted attention to baseball across Taiwan; his influence far exceeded his personal achievements. Whenever Wang Chien-min was on the mound as a starter, Taiwan experienced a "national obsession with baseball," proving the special status of baseball in the hearts of the Taiwanese people.

## The Cultural Significance of Baseball as a "National Sport"

### Why Baseball?

Starting in 2000, baseball was officially referred to as the "national sport" in Taiwan. Behind this title lies profound political and cultural connotations.

According to research by Professor Liu Chang-te from the Department of Journalism at National Taiwan University, referring to baseball as the "national sport" starting in 2000 carries significant political implications, including the establishment of national identity within Taiwan and a new imagination of nationhood.[^1]

Baseball was able to become the national sport because it embodied too many shared memories of the Taiwanese people: the glory of Hongye Minor League, the fervor of professional baseball, and the passion of international competitions. This sport witnessed Taiwan's journey from authoritarianism to democracy, and from isolation to openness.

### Baseball and Taiwanese Identity

In the political context of Taiwan, baseball provides a vehicle for identity that transcends party lines. Regardless of one's political stance, the Taiwanese people cheer for the national team's victories and take pride in Wang Chien-min's performance.

This identity built through sports resonates more deeply with ordinary people than political slogans. When we root for the Taiwanese team, we are simultaneously confirming our own identity.

## Modern Challenges and New Hopes

### The Rebirth and Development of Professional Baseball

After the low point caused by match-fixing scandals, Taiwanese professional baseball spent several years rebuilding trust. The league reformed its discipline, strengthened anti-gambling mechanisms, and improved game quality. New teams joined, stadium facilities were upgraded, and fan culture became more mature.

In recent years, many Taiwanese players have made their way to Japanese professional leagues and Major League Baseball, not only raising their own standards but also building a higher international reputation for Taiwanese baseball. The overseas endeavors of players like Chen Wei-yin, Lin Tzu-wei, and Wang Po-rong continue the tradition started by Wang Chien-min.

### Challenges in Grassroots Baseball

However, Taiwanese baseball also faces severe challenges. The impact of the declining birthrate, the diversification of sports, and changing parental attitudes have led to a shortage of players at the grassroots level. Many traditional strong baseball schools can no longer field complete teams.

Furthermore, the overly elite development model is also being questioned. Does professionalization too early stifle children's other developmental possibilities? Finding a balance between cultivating top players and popularizing sports is an issue that Taiwanese baseball must face.

### The Shohei Ohtani Effect and New Paradigms

In recent years, the success of Japanese player Shohei Ohtani in Major League Baseball has set a new paradigm for Asian baseball. His "two-way system" represents a mindset unconstrained by frameworks, technically overturning past training concepts.

For Taiwanese baseball, Shohei Ohtani's success provides a new direction for thought: are we too fixated on traditional training models? Can we incorporate more innovative elements while retaining the characteristics of Asian players?

## Women's Baseball and Diversified Development

Women's baseball in Taiwan has performed brilliantly on the international stage. The Taiwanese women's national baseball team has achieved repeated successes in tournaments like the Women's Baseball World Cup, representing another line of development for Taiwanese baseball.

The development of women's baseball has its own specific challenges: resource allocation, exposure, and career paths are less than those for men's baseball. Yet, people continue to dedicate themselves to this field, and gender boundaries on the baseball field have become blurrier than in the past.

The diversification of Taiwanese baseball also includes the promotion of special needs baseball and the activity of amateur leagues across various age groups. These are ways that baseball exists in society outside of professional sports.

## Conclusion: The Eternal Dream of a National Sport

From the wooden bats of Hongye in 1968, to the lively stadiums of the CPBL Year One in 1990, the impact of the match-fixing scandal in 1997, and the MLB stage of Wang Chien-min in 2005, Taiwanese baseball has experienced various ups and downs over half a century.

The reason this sport became Taiwan's "national sport" is that it carries too many dreams and emotions of the Taiwanese people, far exceeding mere athletic achievement. Every international competition, every performance by a Taiwanese player overseas, moves millions of hearts.

The current task for Taiwanese baseball is to introduce scientific training methods and a broader international perspective while maintaining traditional technical advantages. The issues of grassroots talent supply, premature professionalization of players, and how to involve more diverse groups are topics that require specific policy responses.

The records left by Taiwanese baseball internationally give this sport a cultural weight in Taiwanese society that transcends sports. In every international competition, baseball becomes an outlet for the collective emotion of Taiwan—a function that is difficult to replicate in other sports.
