# Agricultural Technology and Precision Agriculture

## 30-Second Overview

With less than 40,000 square kilometers of land, Taiwan has created an agricultural miracle that has amazed the world. Starting with Iso Yong-ji (磯永吉), the "Father of Indica Rice," Taiwan's agriculture has undergone long-term innovation and breakthroughs, developing high-value products such as orchids, pineapple custard apples, and high-mountain tea to secure a key position in international markets. By integrating new technologies like the Internet of Things (IoT) and AI, Taiwan is moving toward the "Smart Agriculture 4.0" era.

Keywords: Precision agriculture, smart agriculture, orchid kingdom, pineapple custard apple, agricultural technology, variety improvement

## Why It Matters

In a small island nation with limited land area, Taiwan must replace "extensive farming" with "precision" and "labor" with "technology." Agriculture is no longer just a primary industry; it has become a sixth-tier industry that integrates technology, cultural creativity, and tourism. The success of Taiwan's precision agriculture proves that a small nation can find a unique niche in global agricultural competition.

For Taiwan, precision agriculture represents the maximization of land efficiency (creating maximum value from limited land) and a model of technological innovation (systematic technological integration from breeding to cultivation).

- **International Competitive Advantage**: Establishing an irreplaceable position in specific agricultural products.
- **Sustainable Development Model**: An agricultural development path characterized by environmental friendliness, high efficiency, and high value-added production.

## The Evolution of Taiwan's Agricultural Transformation

### Traditional Agriculture Era (1950–1980)

In the early post-war period, Taiwan's agriculture focused on food self-sufficiency. The "Land-to-the-Tiller" policy released rural productivity, paired with the promotion of high-yield rice varieties (the Green Revolution) and the extensive use of chemical fertilizers.

- **Foreign Exchange Earnings**: Exporting agricultural products such as rice, sugar, and tea to earn foreign exchange.

This stage emphasized increasing "quantity" but lacked considerations for "quality."

### The Rise of Precision Agriculture (1980–2000)

In the 1980s, Taiwan began promoting "precision agriculture" policies. The Taiwan Agricultural Research Institute developed high-quality new varieties, and facility agriculture, such as greenhouses and net houses, developed rapidly.

- **Quality Improvement**: Shifting from pursuing yield to pursuing quality.
- **Brand Building**: Brands such as "Taiwan Orchids" and "Taiwan Tea" established reputations in major export markets.

The key turning point was the realization that Taiwan could not compete in low-priced agricultural products and had to pursue a high-value-added route.

### The Rise of Technological Agriculture (2000–2010)

After joining the WTO, Taiwan's agriculture faced intense international competitive pressure. Biotechnology, such as molecular breeding and tissue culture, was widely applied, and GPS and GIS technologies were introduced into farmland management.

- **Cold Chain Logistics**: Developing preservation technologies to extend the shelf life of agricultural products.
- **Organic Agriculture**: Responding to consumer demand for food safety.

### The Era of Smart Agriculture (2010–Present)

As IoT and AI technologies matured, Taiwan promoted "Smart Agriculture 4.0." Sensors collect environmental data, AI analyzes optimal cultivation conditions, and robots and drones reduce labor requirements.

- **Blockchain Traceability**: Establishing a food safety tracking system from planting to consumption.
- **Circular Agriculture**: Turning waste into resources to establish sustainable production models.

## Success Stories in Precision Agriculture

### The Orchid Kingdom: Taiwan's Orchid Industry

Taiwan is known as the "Orchid Kingdom" and is one of the world's most important producers and exporters of orchids:

**Industry Scale**: In 2023, the total export value of orchids was US$197.68 million (approximately NT$6 billion), of which Phalaenopsis (moth orchids) accounted for US$159.38 million, or 80%. The top five markets were the United States, Japan, Vietnam, the Netherlands, and Canada[^1]. Taiwan accounts for approximately 30% of the global Phalaenopsis market; however, since 2014, the Netherlands has overtaken Taiwan to become number one with a market share of about 50%[^9].

**Technical Advantages**: Tissue culture technology supports the mass propagation of high-quality seedlings; hybrid breeding creates new varieties with independent intellectual property rights; and precise environmental control (temperature, humidity, light) combined with standardized production processes ensures consistent quality.

**International Competitiveness**: Taiwan's share of the U.S. import market for Phalaenopsis rose from 48% in 2005 to 68% in 2010[^9]. Vietnam was the third-largest export market in 2023, and Taiwanese companies have also established production facilities there[^1].

**Keys to Success**
The Tainan District Agricultural Research and Extension Station of the Ministry of Agriculture has long undertaken orchid variety research and industry guidance[^2], covering the entire industry chain from the collection and preservation of native orchids to hybrid breeding, tissue culture, and cultivation techniques. Regarding export channels, the Taiwan Orchid Biotechnology Park in Houbi, Tainan, is a major hub for Phalaenopsis exports. Orchid seedlings are transported via air cold chains, which also provides a boost to Taiwan's air freight system and floral cold storage facilities.

### Pineapple Custard Apple: A New Star of Taiwan Agriculture

While the pineapple custard apple is not a variety originally developed in Taiwan, it serves as an example of how Taiwanese cultivation techniques turned an exotic variety into an industry:

**Varietal Characteristics**: The pineapple custard apple (_anona pinea_) is a hybrid of the sugar apple and the soursop. The main variety grown in Taiwan, _Gefner_, was introduced from Israel for trial planting in 1965. It only formed an industry starting in 1997 after the Taitung District Agricultural Research and Extension Station used pruning techniques to produce winter fruit[^4]. It is characterized by dense flesh, high sweetness (approximately 20–25 degrees), and a pineapple-like fragrance for which it is named; it is also resistant to fruit cracking and suitable for transport.

**Industry Data**: The primary production area is Taitung County, which accounts for 99% of the total area in Taiwan. The cultivation area decreased from 2,951 hectares in 2021 to 2,454 hectares in 2025, with a projected 2025 yield of approximately 11,000 metric tons. Before the ban in 2021, 95% of the export market was concentrated in China[^4].

**Technical Breakthroughs**: Grafting technology allows for harvests the year after grafting; production period regulation extends the harvest season from December to the following April; standardized grading and packaging ensure export quality, while post-harvest storage technologies extend freshness.

**Challenges and Opportunities**
In September 2021, China implemented a total ban on Taiwanese pineapple custard apples. In 2023, they only authorized imports from 25 orchards and 3 packing plants; in 2024, the zero-tariff policy was replaced by a 29% tariff. Since 2021, the Ministry of Agriculture has offered incentives of NT$150,000 to NT$200,000 per hectare for crop conversion, resulting in over 200 hectares being converted, while also encouraging processing. In 2025, processed fruit exceeded 600 tons, with domestic sales accounting for more than half for the first time[^4]. This forced transformation has made the pineapple custard apple industry confront the structural issue of single-market dependence.

### High-Mountain Oolong Tea: A Miracle Created by Geography

Taiwan's high-mountain tea is a classic representative of precision agriculture:

**Geographical Advantages**: The combination of four conditions—high-altitude environments above 1,000 meters, large diurnal temperature variations (beneficial for nutrient accumulation in tea leaves), natural shading from mist and clouds, and well-drained soil—creates the unique quality of high-mountain oolong tea.

**Quality Characteristics**: The tea liquor features a fresh fragrance and sweet aftertaste (_hui gan_), rich layers, and thick leaves that are resistant to over-steeping. Each production area possesses a distinct "mountain charm" (_qi yun_) and has won numerous awards in international tea competitions.

**Famous Production Areas**: The Alishan tea region (altitudes of 1,000–1,700 meters, famous for _Zhu Lu_ tea) and the Lishan tea region (altitudes above 2,000 meters, Taiwan's highest altitude tea region) are the two flagship areas. The Shanlinxi tea region is shrouded in mist year-round, producing sweet tea; the Dayuling tea region, at altitudes of 2,200–2,600 meters, produces rare but extremely high-quality tea that is in high demand every procurement season.

## Applications of Smart Agriculture Technology

### Precision Agriculture Technology

Three core technical modules of precision agriculture support one another: Environmental monitoring systems provide real-time data on soil temperature, humidity, pH, EC values, and meteorological data to offer early warnings for pests and diseases; automated irrigation systems adjust water volume based on soil moisture, while integrated water-fertilizer systems allow for precise fertilization, improving water efficiency by 30–50% and reducing labor costs by 60%; production traceability systems use blockchain technology to record information throughout the entire process from planting to harvest, allowing consumers to query details by scanning a QR code.

### AI (Artificial Intelligence) Applications

Major application scenarios for AI in agriculture include: drones equipped with multispectral cameras to monitor crop growth, with AI analyzing plant diseases at an accuracy of over 95%, and automated quality grading to improve efficiency; in predictive analysis, AI combines historical meteorological data to predict optimal cultivation timing, market prices, and the probability of pest outbreaks; smart decision support systems integrate sensor data to provide cultivation suggestions and regulate greenhouse environments, reducing overall production costs by 20–30%.

### Agricultural Machinery Automation and Drone Plant Protection

Agricultural spraying drones only gained legal status in Taiwan following the implementation of the Civil Aviation Act's chapter on remote-controlled drones in March 2020: operators must simultaneously hold a professional operator certificate from the Civil Aviation Administration and a registration as an agricultural pesticide spraying technician from the Bureau of Animal and Plant Health Inspection and Quarantine. In July 2020, only 227 people nationwide held both certificates, and only 5 spraying service providers had passed review[^5]. Subsequently, the Ministry of Agriculture included agricultural spraying drones in the "Labor-Saving, High-Efficiency, and Carbon Sink Machinery Subsidy." Starting in 2025, legal spraying service providers must hang flags during operations for identification[^10]. Previously, it took two people one day to spray one _jia_ (approx. 0.97 hectares) of land; now, one person can operate a plant protection machine and finish in half a day, while also being able to work on steep hillsides and remote farmlands, filling the labor shortage gap. Fruit and vegetable harvesting robots are still in the field validation stage.

## Government Policies and Support

### Smart Agriculture 4.0 Program

The Council of Agriculture (now the Ministry of Agriculture) proposed the "Smart Agriculture 4.0" promotion plan in 2016, which was implemented from 2017 to 2020:

**Program Goals**: Ten pilot industries were selected—orchids, seedlings, mushrooms, rice cultivation, agricultural facilities, aquaculture/fisheries, poultry (including waterfowl), traceable agricultural products, raw milk, and the marine fishery industry. The goal was to use smart production and digital services to reduce labor demand and increase production efficiency. Three technical promotion groups were established: Smart Production and Human-Machine Assistive Tools, Agricultural Technology Expert Systems, and Digital Services and Traceability Data Exchange[^3].

**Key Projects**: The program started with smart production (sensors, automated equipment) and digital services (agricultural cloud platforms), extending to agricultural finance (using big data to improve financing) and cross-domain cooperation (integration of the ICT industry and agriculture).

### New Agricultural Innovation Promotion Plan

Since 2017, the Executive Yuan has promoted the "New Agricultural Innovation Promotion Plan" (2017–2020), with three main pillars: establishing a new agricultural paradigm, constructing an agricultural security system, and enhancing agricultural marketing capabilities, supported by ten key policies. The 2.0 version (2021–2024) shifted its three pillars to improving the farmer welfare system, strengthening the fundamental environment, and enhancing industrial competitiveness[^11].

### Organic Agriculture Promotion Act

The _Organic Agriculture Promotion Act_ was promulgated in May 2018 and implemented in May 2019. It includes eco-friendly farming in its guidance and establishes organic agriculture promotion zones, providing certification subsidies and marketing promotion. The target for organic and eco-friendly farming set by the New Agricultural Plan was 15,000 hectares; by the end of 2020, 15,754 hectares had been achieved[^11].

## Challenges and Future Development

### Main Challenges

Taiwan's precision agriculture faces four structural challenges. **Climate**: Increasing frequency of extreme weather and changing pest patterns require stress-resistant varieties and disaster warning systems. **Labor**: According to the 2020 Census of Agriculture, Forestry, and Fisheries, the average age of managers in agricultural households was 64.4 years, an increase of 0.9 years compared to five years prior[^12]. The proportion of youth entering agriculture is low, leading to a gap in technical succession. **Market**: Intensifying low-price competition from Southeast Asia and the rise of protectionism require strengthened differentiation advantages. **Environment**: Carbon reduction pressures require agriculture to reduce the use of chemical fertilizers and pesticides and develop circular agriculture models.

### Future Development Directions

The next step for precision agriculture is the popularization of sensor technology, iterative AI analysis, and reaching unmanned operation in certain fields. Biotechnology applications will focus on gene-editing breeding, biological pest control as a replacement for pesticides, probiotic soil improvement, and the commercialization of plant factories. The circular economy direction includes the resource utilization of agricultural waste, alternative proteins such as insect protein, and agricultural carbon credit trading. International cooperation will focus on exporting agricultural technology to Southeast Asia and Africa, developing agricultural technical service industries, and establishing overseas agricultural production bases.

## International Influence and Future Vision

The international influence of Taiwan's precision agriculture is primarily expressed through technology export: orchid companies set up production plants in Vietnam, and agricultural technical missions are dispatched to friendly nations year-round. In terms of branding, "Taiwan Orchids" has established a clear quality premium in the high-end U.S. market, while "Taiwan Tea" has done the same in the high-end consumer markets of Japan and Southeast Asia.

The transformation path of Taiwan's agriculture—from the breeding of Indica rice in the 1920s to today's AI image recognition for pests and diseases—is essentially a systematic raising of technical thresholds. The fact that Phalaenopsis occupies nearly 70% of the U.S. import market, and that an Israeli variety became an industry concentrated 99% in Taitung through local pruning techniques, illustrates the specific meaning of "precision" in the context of Taiwan's agriculture: it refers to a market position with a "moat," rather than mere claims of quality.
