> **30-Second Overview:**
> The CMC Veryca (中華菱利) is more than just a commercial vehicle; it is synonymous with Taiwan's "fortune van" (_facaiche_) culture. Launched in 1988, the "Varica" laid the foundation with an 80% market share, and its successor, the Veryca, which took over in 2000, continued this dominance to rule the light commercial vehicle market for years. The history of this vehicle encapsulates Taiwan's automotive industry's struggle and breakthroughs—moving from "technology introduction" to "independent R&D." In 2024, it rebranded as the J Space, attempting to move forward amidst waves of electrification and new safety regulations, continuing to carry the entrepreneurial dreams of countless Taiwanese families.

"Dad! Can this car really make us rich?" In Taiwan's traditional markets or industrial zones, this small blue van is almost the first "family member" of every entrepreneurial household. It is affectionately called the _facaiche_ (fortune van). This title comes not from expensive features, but from its resilience as it shuttles goods through streets and alleys during Taiwan's economic transformation.

### Core Tension: Japanese Backbone, Taiwanese Soul

The origins of the CMC Veryca can be traced back to the Mitsubishi-licensed Minicab. However, the reason the Veryca became a "god-tier vehicle" in Taiwan was not because it was a perfect Japanese car, but because it was "not original, but born for Taiwan." The Japanese _K-Car_ specifications often struggled to adapt to Taiwan's steep slopes, high temperatures, and overloading environments.

1. **The Turning Point of Independent R&D**: In 1988, CMC invested NT$1.4 billion in the independent development of the "CMC Varica" (中華威利). At the time, this was the most significant independent attempt by Taiwan's automotive industry since the Yulon Bluebird 101 [^1].
2. **Localized Specification Evolution**: The Veryca upgraded the original Japanese Minicab's micro-engine from 1.2L to later 1.3L and 1.5L versions, and even developed a four-wheel drive (4WD) version to handle the harsh terrain of Taiwan's mountain tea gardens and orchards [^4].
3. **Resilience for Survival**: During the era when Taiwan joined the WTO and faced challenges from imported cars, the Veryca built a "maintenance wall" through extremely high parts commonality and ease of repair, ensuring that almost any roadside motorcycle shop could handle its basic maintenance.

📝 Curator's Note: The Veryca wasn't designed in a laboratory; it was tempered on the steep slopes of Taiwan's orchards and in the puddles of traditional markets.

### From "Minicab" to "Veryca": A Carrier of Economic Miracles

The history of commercial vehicle development in Taiwan is almost identical to the CMC family tree. In 1978, the localization of the third-generation Minicab became the "CMC Minicab" (中華百利), initiating the long-term partnership between CMC and Mitsubishi [^3]. The subsequent Varica and Veryca took the baton to complete the landscape of Taiwan's light commercial vehicles.

| Model                      | Production Years | Key Status                                                                                                                                      |
| :------------------------- | :--------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **CMC Minicab** (中華百利) | 1978 - 1988      | CMC's first commercial vehicle, opening the domestic light commercial market                                                                    |
| **CMC Varica** (中華威利)  | 1988 - 2000      | Taiwan's first commercial vehicle developed with NT$1.4 billion in independent R&D; captured 80% market share within three years of launch [^5] |
| **CMC Veryca** (中華菱利)  | 2000 - 2024      | Succeeded the Varica, long-term dominant force in the light commercial market [^2]                                                              |
| **J Space**                | 2024 - Present   | A major redesign and rebranding of the Veryca, enhancing safety and driver assistance [^7]                                                      |

### Challenges and Controversies: The Arrogance and Awakening of a Monopolist

For a long time, the Veryca had almost no competitors in Taiwan, which led to controversies regarding its slow updates in features. Many owners criticized its insufficient safety equipment and outdated chassis technology. It wasn't until 2022 that the Toyota Town Ace entered the market aggressively, breaking the Veryca's twenty-year monopoly.

"This is a long-overdue competition," noted automotive critics. The arrival of the Town Ace forced CMC to launch the majorly redesigned **J Space** in 2024, which not only increased power to 107 hp but also introduced a Level 2 autonomous driving assistance system for the first time [^7]. This competition has not only benefited consumers but also proves the forced transformation of Taiwan's commercial vehicle market driven by safety regulations.

### Cultural Resonance: More Than Just a Vehicle

The Veryca holds a special place in Taiwanese culture. It appears not only in the drifting performances of racer Feng Jen-chih [^6] but also in countless films and literary works, symbolizing the spirit of grassroots struggle.

In 2010, under the leadership of the Ministry of Economic Affairs' Department of Technology, the Veryca also became a pioneer in Taiwan's development of light electric commercial vehicles [^4]. Although the road to electrification is long, this small blue vehicle continues to attempt to adapt to a new era.

The value of the Veryca lies not in where its gearbox comes from, but in the sense of security it provides to countless entrepreneurs when it starts up at four in the morning.
