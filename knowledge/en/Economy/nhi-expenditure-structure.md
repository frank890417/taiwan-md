In 2024, the NHI's overall drug cost ratio was 27.71%. [^1]

In the same year, the total number of NHI special projects was 61, a 69.4% increase from 36 items in 2020. [^1]

One is the proportion of money spent on drugs; the other is how many special projects the budget is divided into. Both lines are trending upward.

## Twenty-Seven Point Seven One Percent

The drug cost ratio calculation is straightforward: the ratio of drug costs to total medical expenses. Because drugs use a fixed point value (each point pays NT$1), an excessively high drug cost ratio crowds out other medical services. [^1]

In 2022 it was 27.32%, in 2024 27.71%, a slight increase over three years. Excluding new drug costs, 2022 was 24.2% and 2024 26.0%, also rising. [^1]

The report explains the rise in the "excluding new drugs" ratio as follows: new drugs are defined as those listed within the past five years; the 51 drugs listed in 2019 (claimed at approximately NT$14.94 billion in 2024) have exited the new drug category, including hepatitis B oral antivirals, SGLT-2 inhibitors, immune checkpoint inhibitors, CDK4/6 inhibitors, and other high-claim-cost drugs. [^1]

## Differences by Hospital Level

The overall 27.71% is the full-year aggregate ratio; hospital-level figures are much higher.

In 2024, medical centers' drug cost ratio exceeded 36%, regional hospitals approached 30%, and both increased compared to 2023. [^1]

Primary care and district hospitals were lower. [^1]

## Why the Increase

The NHIA explains the 2024 increase as: addition of temporary payment funds and implementation of parallel review, accelerating the listing of multiple new drugs and expansion of reimbursement criteria. [^1]

The NHIA Council's comment recommends that the NHIA continue monitoring drug usage, track the utilization of new drugs under temporary payment, and conduct cost-effectiveness evaluations; simultaneously noting that new drugs are expensive and international drug prices are rising, so advance planning is needed. [^1]

## Special Projects Growing from Thirty-Six to Sixty-One Items

The other line is special projects. The NHI global budget contains many special projects, most of a pilot nature, which should transition into regular payment or exit upon maturity. [^1]

But the numbers show the opposite direction. Total special projects increased from 36 items in 2020 to 61 in 2024, a 69.4% increase; hospital-sector projects grew from 13 to 25 items, a 92.3% increase. [^1]

Total special projects grew from 50 items in 2022 to 61 in 2024, with only 2 items exiting, both in dental outpatient. [^1]

## Execution Rates

With more projects, execution rates became the second issue.

In 2024, the hospital sector's budget execution rate still fell below 80%; Western medicine primary care dropped from 87% in 2023 to 71.76%; dental outpatient rose from 59% in 2022 to 91.11%; Chinese medicine outpatient increased and reached over 75%. [^1]

Some new projects had particularly low execution rates: District Hospital Whole-Person Whole-Community Care Plan 5%, Regional Alliance Aortic Dissection Surgery Patient Care Cross-Hospital Cooperation 36%, Regional Alliance Acute Ischemic Stroke Endovascular Thrombectomy Patient Care Cross-Hospital Cooperation 42%, Antimicrobial Stewardship and Infection Control Quality Improvement Plan 44%. [^1]

The NHIA explains that some projects took effect late (hospitals had 4 items taking effect only in June to August), and healthcare providers were unfamiliar with new project content; for example, the Western medicine primary care Cancer Treatment Quality Improvement Plan had only a 4% execution rate. [^1]

KPI achievement rate is another angle. Over the past three years, each global budget sector's selected special project KPI achievement rate was 100%, except dental outpatient in 2022 which was 0% due to the pandemic. [^1] The NHIA Council recommends reviewing KPI reasonableness and establishing an exit mechanism.

## Commonalities of the Two Lines

The drug cost ratio rose from 27.32% to 27.71%, and even excluding new drugs it rose from 24.2% to 26.0%, indicating that new drugs listed in recent years have become part of routine medication use. [^1]

Special projects grew from 36 to 61 items. The NHIA Council recommends that after a pilot period, execution effectiveness and KPI reasonableness should be reviewed, mature and stable items should be incorporated into general services, and an exit mechanism established for underperforming ones. [^1]

In the same year, the NHIA Council issued review recommendations for both drug costs and special projects.
