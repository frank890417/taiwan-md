> 30-Second Overview: Taiwan's textile industry was a foreign-exchange powerhouse in the 1960s, yet by the 1990s it was tagged a "sunset industry" due to cost pressures. However, through 30 years of technological transformation, Taiwan now supplies over 70% of the world's sports performance fabrics[^1]. From the "waste-gas-to-garment" uniforms of Taiwan (competing as Chinese Taipei) at the 2024 Paris Olympics[^2] to 23 of the 48 teams at the 2026 FIFA World Cup using Taiwan-made yarn[^3], this island is redefining global sustainable fashion standards with waste and high-tech innovation.

---

## The Invisible Protagonist of the Paris Olympics: When "Waste Gas" Becomes Battle Robes

In July 2024, the parade uniforms for Taiwan (competing as Chinese Taipei) at the Paris Olympics debuted on the banks of the Seine. The most surprising aspect of this uniform, designed by a team led by designer Chou Yu-ying, was not its aesthetics but its composition: it was the world's first Olympic uniform to incorporate "carbon capture" technology[^2].

This technology, developed through a collaboration between Taiwan's textile leader **Far Eastern New Century** and the Hsu Hsiang Group, captures and purifies carbon dioxide waste gas emitted from factories, then re-polymerizes it into low-carbon, eco-friendly polyester fiber. This means athletes are wearing not just clothing, but transformed greenhouse gases.

"We want the world to see what 'Far Eastern New' means—we are not just raw material suppliers, but textile manufacturers with high technological content."[^4] Far Eastern Group Chairman Douglas Hsu once emphasized. This logic of converting negative waste into high-value products is a microcosm of Taiwan's textile industry turnaround. Beyond the Taiwan team, Far Eastern New Century's recycled green textiles were also adopted by the national teams of the United States, France, Canada, and others[^2].

📝 **Curator's Note:** While the world talks about carbon reduction, Taiwan's traditional industries are already practicing "wearing carbon on your body." This is not a mere environmental slogan, but the ultimate demonstration of transforming an existential threat into global competitiveness.

## R&D as King: Eclat's "Art and Orders" Philosophy

If Far Eastern New Century is the upstream material magician, then **Eclat** is the mid-to-downstream R&D heart. This garment OEM stock king develops over 3,000 new fabrics annually[^5], with an exceptionally high proportion of R&D personnel.

Eclat founder Hong Zhen-hai came from a farming family in Miaoli and started as an apprentice at a dyeing and finishing factory. He once said: "A person must have dreams in life; as a child, seeing others driven in sedans was my dream."[^6] But he more soberly recognized: "What matters is the market acceptance rate of R&D products. If none are selected, it's just art."[^7]

Amid the 1990s wave of industry offshoring, Eclat chose to stay in Taiwan and establish R&D and testing centers[^5], proactively developing fabric varieties for clients to choose from rather than passively waiting for orders. This shift from "OEM" to "co-development" allowed Taiwan's textile industry to avoid the quagmire of low-price competition, becoming an indispensable pillar for global brands like Nike and Lululemon.

---

## Counterattack from "Garbage Island": From Discarded Fishing Nets to Circular Economy

Taiwan was once dubbed "Garbage Island" by international media, but this pressure spurred globally leading recycling technologies. Beyond a PET bottle recycling rate of 95%, the world's highest[^8], Taiwanese manufacturers have extended their reach to the more difficult "marine debris."

**Hong Yuan Industrial** collaborates with international environmental organizations to recover discarded fishing nets and ocean plastics, processing them through complex desalination, washing, and pelletizing into high-quality nylon yarn[^9]. These yarns not only reduce dependence on petroleum-based raw materials but have also become the designated "ocean eco-fibers" for sustainable brands like Patagonia[^10].

Additionally, Renmei Trademark uses eco-friendly wood fibers and recycled "oyster shell" yarn to create sustainable woven labels[^2], which are sewn into garments from major brands like Nike and Adidas, silently proving the depth of Taiwan's circular economy.

📝 **Curator's Note:** The success of Taiwan's textile industry is actually a counterattack by the "resource-poor." Because island resources are limited, we understand better than anyone how to dig gold from garbage.

---

## Challenges and Controversies: The 2030 Green Storm

Although Taiwan's textile industry currently leads globally, new challenges are imminent. The EU recently unveiled the "EU Strategy for Sustainable and Circular Textiles," strictly prohibiting "downcycling," meaning Taiwan's prized "bottle-to-fiber" (Bottle to Fiber) technology may face termination by 2030[^11].

The future standard is "fiber-to-fiber" (Fiber to Fiber), a massive test for current recycling technologies. Major players like Far Eastern New Century and Everest Textile have already begun deploying smart manufacturing and chemical recycling technologies[^12], striving to maintain their leading position in the next green storm.

When Everest Textile Chairman Chou Li-ping passed the baton to second-generation successor Chou Hsin-peng, he stated that the future growth blueprint would rely on "smart manufacturing" and "digital transformation"[^13]. This race from sunset to dawn has no finish line.
