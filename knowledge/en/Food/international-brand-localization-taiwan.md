**30-second overview:** When international brands enter the Taiwan market, they not only bring globalized products but also, through deep localization strategies, integrate Taiwan's unique food culture and consumption habits into their products and services. From McDonald's corn soup, KFC's Portuguese egg tarts, to Pizza Hut's adventurous pizza flavors, as well as 7-ELEVEN's _lu wei_ (braised snacks) and OPEN's bento boxes, even Kaiten-zushi's love for salmon — these cases demonstrate the brands' respect and flexibility toward the Taiwan market. Going further, Starbucks' specialty stores, Costco's global top-selling store status, MOS Burger's rice-based food culture, IKEA's dining miracle, and even Carrefour's transformation after acquisition and renaming, all together paint a picture of how international brands create unique success formulas through cultural integration.

International brands, in their process of global expansion, often face the challenge of finding balance between standardization and localization. In Taiwan, this challenge has given birth to many unique products and services, not only bringing these brands closer to Taiwanese consumers' lives but also accidentally creating many surprising "Taiwan-exclusive" experiences. These localization strategies are not only commercial successes but also reflect Taiwan's rich and diverse food culture and consumption habits.

## McDonald's: Corn Soup and the Allure of Local Sweet Potatoes

The global fast-food leader McDonald's has demonstrated its localization flexibility in the Taiwan market. Among its most well-known offerings are its menu items **corn soup** and **golden sweet potato fries**. The corn soup is unique to McDonald's in Taiwan, tracing its origins back to 1978, when a local Taiwanese fast-food shop called "McDonald's" was already offering "chicken-flavored corn soup"[^1][^6]. After McDonald's entered Taiwan (1984), in order to cater to the Taiwanese habit of "eating rice with soup," it specifically retained and optimized this product, making it a classic combo option for McDonald's Taiwan[^6].

> 📝 **Curator's Note:** The localization of corn soup is not just a flavor adjustment, but a deep understanding of Taiwanese eating habits. A bowl of hot soup carries the Taiwanese people's expectations and comfort for their main meal.

In addition, McDonald's Taiwan has also launched **golden sweet potato fries** made with Taiwan's local Tainung No. 64 red heart sweet potatoes[^1]. This product not only satisfies Taiwanese consumers' love for sweet potatoes, but also demonstrates the brand's connection with local agriculture in ingredient selection. These localized products have successfully transformed McDonald's from a simple "American fast food" into a more "Taiwanese-flavored" dining choice.

## KFC: Dominance of Portuguese Egg Tarts and Innovation of Paper-Wrapped Chicken

KFC's localization strategy in Taiwan is exemplified by the tremendous success of its **Portuguese egg tarts**, which have even been nicknamed "a pastry shop disturbed by fried chicken"[^7]. The introduction of Portuguese egg tarts stemmed from the Portuguese egg tart craze that swept Taiwan in the 1990s. KFC introduced the recipe from Macau's Maa Chi Kei in 1998 and quickly made it a signature item[^7]. This strategy successfully expanded KFC's brand image in Taiwan from a simple fried chicken shop to a dining choice where both desserts and main dishes coexist, with egg tarts becoming one of the primary reasons many consumers visit KFC.

In addition to egg tarts, KFC in Taiwan has also launched its **paper-wrapped chicken series**, a cooking method that uses paper-wrapped baking to lock in juices. While relatively uncommon across KFC globally, this approach aligns with Taiwanese preferences for tender, juicy textures[^2]. Furthermore, products such as **sweet potato QQ balls** and **sweet potato fries** leverage locally sourced sweet potatoes and incorporate elements of Taiwan's night-market snacks, further deepening KFC's connection to the local Taiwanese market[^2].

## KFC: Challenging the Limits of Taste with Creative Pizza

KFC's localization strategy in Taiwan can be considered the most experimental and discussion-worthy among international brands. From "Cilantro, Century Egg, and Blood Cake Pizza" to "Stinky Tofu Pizza" and "Bubble Tea Pizza," these creatively provocative products that challenge consumers' taste buds have repeatedly sparked heated discussions on social media[^3][^8]. For example, the cilantro, century egg, and blood cake pizza launched in 2021 set a record of over 100,000 Google keyword searches in a single day on its launch day[^3].

Behind these seemingly "quirky" flavors is KFC's General Manager Liang Jiajun (from Hong Kong), who has driven an "e-commerce mindset" and the "WOW Pizza strategy"[^3]. Liang believes that in Taiwan, where delivery platforms thrive, KFC is effectively an e-commerce entity. Thus, "Talkability," "Traffic," and "Transaction" have become the key 3T strategy[^3]. By continuously launching highly discussion-worthy localized products, even if consumers do not necessarily purchase these special flavors, it effectively boosts the brand's online visibility and attention, thereby driving overall sales[^3]. This strategy not only broke the conventional impression that Taiwanese people only eat pizza during festivals but also successfully positioned KFC as a brand full of innovation and vitality[^3].

## 7-Eleven: Practitioner of Lifestyle Proposals and the Charm of the OPEN Warrior

As a representative of Taiwan's convenience store culture, 7-Eleven's localization has long transcended the realm of mere products, deeply embedding itself into the daily lives of Taiwanese people. Beyond the well-known **tea-egg** (with annual sales exceeding 100 million pieces), in recent years 7-Eleven has launched "Star-Rated Feast Braised Snacks," standardizing the braised snack culture of Taiwan's traditional night markets and offering consumers 24-hour access to convenient delights[^4][^9].

> 📝 **Curator's Note:** 7-Eleven's success lies not only in the diversity of its products, but in how it transforms the convenience store into a platform that provides "lifestyle proposals," satisfying Taiwanese people's dual needs for convenience and local cuisine.

Another successful localization case is its exclusive mascot, the **OPEN Warrior**. The OPEN Warrior was jointly developed by Uni-President 7-Eleven and Dentsu in 2005. It not only has a complete character setting and backstory, but has also successfully integrated into Taiwan's pop culture through peripheral products and themed activities, even creating a business value of NT$1 billion in annual revenue for 7-Eleven[^4][^10][^11]. The success of the OPEN Warrior proves the great potential of brand mascots in the Taiwan market and their importance in building brand emotional connections.

## Kaiten-zushi: Taiwanese Passion for Salmon

In recent years, kaiten-zushi (rotating sushi) brands that have rapidly risen in the Taiwanese market, such as Sushiro and Zau Sushi, have demonstrated interesting localization phenomena — namely, Taiwanese consumers' extreme passion for **salmon**. Compared to Japan's domestic market, kaiten-zushi restaurants in Taiwan typically offer a wider variety of salmon items and have higher sales volumes than other fish[^5]. This phenomenon even sparked the "salmon chaos" in 2021, when Sushiro held a name-change promotion that attracted over 300 people across the country to change their identification cards to "salmon" in order to enjoy free sushi[^5][^12].

Taiwanese people's fondness for salmon can be attributed to its widespread perception of having a high CP value and rich flavor, as well as salmon being the most popular sashimi choice during the early promotion of Japanese cuisine in Taiwan[^5]. Data shows that Taiwan's largest salmon importer, Chia Hsin, imports approximately 2,500 tons of salmon annually, accounting for about 20% of the total import volume in Taiwan[^5][^13]. This passion for salmon has prompted kaiten-zushi brands to launch a wider variety of salmon products in the Taiwanese market to meet consumer demand.

## Starbucks: The Aesthetics of Localization in the Third Space

Starbucks' localization strategy in Taiwan is not only reflected in its beverages but also in its "third space" business philosophy. Starbucks in Taiwan has transformed historical sites or incorporated local landscapes to create unique stores, such as the Bo'an Store located in the historic Yeh Jin-tu Residence in Dadaocheng, the former joint office building store in Huxi, Yunlin, and the Hualien Lixiang Dadi Store (花蓮理想大地門市), among others[^14][^15]. These themed stores not only serve coffee but also serve as carriers of local culture and history, reducing the gap between international brands and the local environment, and enhancing consumers' spatial experiences[^15].

In addition, Starbucks has actively launched festival gift boxes with Taiwanese characteristics, such as mooncakes for the Mid-Autumn Festival, zongzi for the Dragon Boat Festival, and local ingredient products, including a local tea beverage series and _yutou mengbula_ (taro mont blanc)[^14]. The launch of these products aims to meet the Taiwanese consumers' emphasis on festival customs and their preference for local flavors, further deepening the connection between the brand and Taiwanese culture.

## Costco: The Perfect Blend of Global Store King and Localized Services

The American warehouse club Costco's success in Taiwan is considered a model for international brand localization. Costco stores in Taiwan, especially the North Taichung Store, have held the title of "Most Profitable Store in the World," while the Neihu Store and Zhonghe Store have consistently ranked among the top ten globally[^16][^17]. Costco has over 4 million members in Taiwan. With an annual fee of NT$1,390, membership fee revenue alone reaches NT$5.4 billion, confirming its business model where 80% of profits come from membership fees rather than product margins[^17].

Costco's localized services in Taiwan are also quite prominent, such as having gas stations, which are rare globally. The North Taichung Store's fuel sales are the highest in Taiwan[^18]. Additionally, while Costco focuses on bulk products, Taiwan stores have increased Taiwanese-style deli and snack options to cater to local family structures[^17]. This strategy precisely targets the Taiwanese consumer psychology of seeking high cost-performance value and family shopping, leading to great success in the Taiwan market.

## MOS Burger: The Eastern Fast Food Representative of Rice Culture

Originating from Japan, MOS Burger has successfully promoted its "Eastern flavor" and "high-tech service" features in the Taiwan market. The most representative product is the **rice burger**. In 1991, Taiwan's MOS Burger launched the "MOS Barbecue Pearl Burger," using local Taiwanese rice as the main ingredient, successfully integrating rice culture into fast food, making it a signature product of the brand[^19][^20].

MOS Burger has actively promoted the use of local ingredients, such as becoming the first store in Taiwan to receive the "Taiwan Pig Certification Mark" in 2020, and emphasizing the use of products with the Taiwan Rice Label[^19]. Compared to other American fast food brands, MOS Burger is more in tune with the Taiwanese reliance on refined fast food and rice culture, successfully establishing a unique position in the market.

## IKEA: The Restaurant Distracted by Furniture and Creative Marketing

The Swedish furniture brand IKEA in Taiwan has become deeply associated with its image as a "restaurant distracted by furniture." Taiwan's IKEA food revenue accounts for as high as 12%, nearly three times the global average (4-5%), ranking first globally[^21][^22]. Among these, the mere NT$10 **soft-serve ice cream** alone sells over a million units annually, with its sales volume even accounting for 30% of food revenue[^23]. This "leading chicken" strategy attracts foot traffic through low-priced food, not only enhancing the customer shopping experience but also creating a psychological impression that "IKEA products are all very cheap"[^24].

IKEA's localized marketing in Taiwan is also full of creativity, such as its **BLÅHAJ** shark plush toy sparking a craze in Taiwan, and launching themed foods tied to Taiwanese festivals, such as wasabi soft-serve ice cream and soy milk soy sauce-flavored soft-serve ice cream[^25]. These strategies have successfully transformed "shopping for furniture" into "family dining outings," satisfying the Taiwanese people's high interest in dining.

## Carrefour: From International Brand to Deep Localization as "Prosperity Plaza"

The French hypermarket brand Carrefour's localization strategy in Taiwan has undergone significant changes, from aggressive acquisitions to brand transformation. In 2020, Carrefour acquired Wellcome and Jasons supermarkets for NT$3.2 billion, aiming to strengthen its presence in the "community-type supermarket" sector to respond to the intense competition in Taiwan's retail market[^26][^27]. Through this acquisition, Carrefour was able to rapidly expand its store network, penetrate deeper into Taiwan's communities, and provide services more closely aligned with residents' needs.

Carrefour also actively promoted its "Influence Center" initiative, partnering with Taiwanese small-scale farmers to promote local ingredients and friendly eggs, demonstrating its support for local agriculture[^28]. However, with Uni-President completing the transaction on June 30, 2023, officially acquiring 100% of the shares of Carrefour Taiwan, making it a purely Taiwan-invested company[^29][^30]. This change in ownership also led to a brand name transformation. Due to the expiration of the brand licensing agreement with the French Carrefour, Taiwan Carrefour decided to terminate the licensing on December 19, 2025, and officially changed its name starting **July 1, 2026**[^31][^32][^33].

The hypermarkets will be renamed **"Prosperity Plaza"** (Prosperity Plaza), while the supermarkets will be renamed **"Uni-Prosperity"** (Uni-Prosperity)[^34][^35]. This renaming is not merely a change of signage, but symbolizes Carrefour's formal integration into the Uni-President retail system, creating deeper synergies with brands such as 7-ELEVEN and RT-Mart, and representing a shift in brand image from "French brand" to "local brand," emphasizing the auspicious meanings of "Prosperity Plaza" and "Uni-Prosperity," which better align with Taiwanese consumers' cultural preferences[^36][^37].

## Conclusion

The localization strategies of international brands in Taiwan represent a flavorful evolution that fuses global vision with local insights. From McDonald's corn soup and KFC's Portuguese egg tarts to Pizza Hut's creative pizzas, 7-Eleven's stewed snacks and OPEN Junior, as well as conveyor belt sushi's salmon craze, these cases are not merely commercial successes but vivid portrayals of Taiwan's unique culinary culture and consumer habits. Furthermore, Starbucks' specialty stores, Costco's global store king status, MOS Burger's rice culture, IKEA's dining miracle, and Carrefour's local acquisitions and brand transformation all collectively illustrate how international brands on this land achieve distinctive success formulas through cultural integration and business innovation. These brands, through constant innovation and adjustment, prove that only by deeply understanding and embracing local culture can one stand out in a fiercely competitive market and forge deep emotional connections with consumers.
