In 2023, the average length of stay for acute beds in Taiwan was 7.3 days. [^1]

In the same year, the OECD average was 6.7 days, while Japan's was 15.7 days. [^1]

## The Coordinates of 7.3 Days

When comparing these countries, Taiwan falls in the upper-middle range: 0.6 days longer than the OECD average, but 8.4 days shorter than Japan. [^1]

Taiwan is closest to South Korea (7.2 days), Germany (7.5 days), and the UK (7.5 days). Canada is longer at 7.9 days, France is shorter at 5.5 days, and Sweden has the shortest recorded stay at 5.3 days. [^1]

The report's commentary notes: Taiwan is similar to South Korea, Germany, and the UK; it is far lower than Japan but higher than the OECD average. [^1]

The OECD average is based on data from 33 member countries, excluding years with no available data. [^1] Cross-national comparisons should focus on magnitude rather than decimal precision.

## Three Tiers, Three Lengths

The internal disparities within Taiwan are more noteworthy than the international differences.

Medical Centers have the lowest average length of stay among the three tiers; the report indicates that overall, as well as for Medical Centers and Regional Hospitals, there has been a downward trend over the past three years. [^1]

Regional Hospitals, however, moved in the opposite direction: 8.31 days in 2022, 8.14 days in 2023, and 8.33 days in 2024. The 2024 figure is not only the highest in the last three years but also the highest among the three hospital tiers. [^1]

```tw-line
Average Length of Stay for Acute Beds at Regional Hospitals (Days)

2023 | 8.14 | Decrease
2024 | 8.33 | Rebound; highest among the three tiers
Source: NHIA "2024 National Health Insurance Monitoring Indicator Results Report," Indicator 1.1
```

While both involve hospitalization, patients at Regional Hospitals stay longer on average than those at Medical Centers. The report notes that this gap is primarily driven by respiratory care cases. [^1]

## Why Regional Hospitals are the Highest

The report's analysis is direct: the higher average length of stay for acute beds in Regional Hospitals is mainly due to respiratory care cases. [^1]

Patients requiring long-term ventilator use naturally occupy acute or intensive care beds for longer periods. Since 2000, the National Health Insurance (NHI) has promoted the "Prospective Payment System for Integrated Care of Ventilator-Dependent Patients," which provides four stages of care based on patient condition: Intensive Care Unit (ICU), Sub-acute Respiratory Care Unit (RCC), Chronic Respiratory Care Ward (RCW), and Home Respiratory Care (RHC). [^1]

The design aims to facilitate the movement of patients into appropriate care stages to avoid long-term occupation of acute beds. However, the data shows that in 2024, the length of stay at Regional Hospitals rose back to 8.33 days, which may suggest that this transition is not always seamless.

## Recommendations from the Report

The NHIA (National Health Insurance Administration) has two recommendations: continue to improve the utilization rate of hospice care for ventilator-dependent patients, and provide guidance to Regional Hospitals with higher average lengths of stay for acute beds. [^1]

The first relates to hospice care: the hospice utilization rate for ventilator-dependent patients was approximately 61.4% in 2018 and rose to approximately 76.1% in 2024. [^1]

The second returns to management: identify hospitals with high lengths of stay to determine whether the issue lies in operational processes or patient case structures.

## How to Read This Number

Average length of stay is an indicator that is easily misinterpreted.

First, it is influenced by case mix. A hospital that admits more chronic respiratory care cases may have a longer average stay; this does not necessarily indicate poor efficiency, but rather a different functional role. The report treats the average length of stay as one of the operational efficiency indicators and suggests providing guidance to Regional Hospitals with higher durations. [^1]

Second, a short length of stay does not guarantee high quality. If patients are discharged early simply to reduce the number of days, it may actually increase emergency room visits and readmissions.

Third, be cautious with international comparisons. Classification methods, as well as the availability of long-term care and home medical services, vary by country; Japan's 15.7 days likely reflects differences in their care system rather than just medical efficiency.

A more honest way to read these figures is to view them side-by-side: 7.3 days is the national average, 8.33 days is for Regional Hospitals, and 15.7 days is for Japan. Each number answers a different question and they cannot be used interchangeably.
