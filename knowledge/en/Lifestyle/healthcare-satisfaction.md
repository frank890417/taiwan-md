In 2024, public satisfaction with hospital medical outcomes was 94.1%.[^1]

The same figure in 2022 and 2023 was also 94.1%, completely unchanged for three years.[^1]

## Four Sectors, Four Trend Lines

Each year the NHIA submits the public satisfaction survey results for the four global budget sectors (hospitals, Western medicine primary care, dental outpatient, Chinese medicine outpatient), and the NHIC analyzes them.[^1]

The 2024 results: hospitals 94.1%, Western medicine primary care 96.4%, dental outpatient 96.3%, Chinese medicine outpatient 95.3%.[^1]

```tw-bars
2024 Medical Outcome Satisfaction by Sector (%)
Western medicine primary care | 96.4 | 2022 94.2%
Dental outpatient | 96.3 | 2022 95.3%
Chinese medicine outpatient | 95.3 | 2022 94.7%
Hospitals | 94.1 | 2022–2024 all same
Source: NHIC "2024 National Health Insurance Monitoring Indicator Results Report" Indicator 3.1
```

Three sectors are rising, only hospitals are flat. Western medicine primary care rose from 94.2% in 2022 to 96.4%, the largest improvement among the four sectors.[^1]

## The Other Trend Line: Dissatisfaction

Beyond satisfaction, the survey also asks the proportion of "dissatisfied" and "very dissatisfied".

In 2024, hospital dissatisfaction was 3.1%, 1.6% in 2022, 2.4% in 2023.[^1]

Other sectors moved differently: Western medicine primary care fell from 1.5% to 1.2%, dental outpatient rose from 1.3% to 2.0% then fell to 1.6%, Chinese medicine outpatient rose from 1.6% to 2.2%.[^1]

```tw-line
Hospital Sector Dissatisfaction (%)
2022 | 1.6 | —
2023 | 2.4 | Increase
2024 | 3.1 | Increase again
Source: NHIC "2024 National Health Insurance Monitoring Indicator Results Report" Indicator 3.1
```

Hospital satisfaction unchanged, dissatisfaction nearly doubled. The two are not contradictory: satisfaction is the ratio of "satisfied + very satisfied" among all who expressed an opinion, dissatisfaction is the other end, the remainder being "neutral" and "no clear opinion".

## Reasons for Dissatisfaction

The report compiles reasons for dissatisfaction. All four sectors are led by "poor physician medical technique", "physician cursory consultation", "physician insufficient explanation of condition".[^1]

Hospital sector additionally has "long wait times", Chinese medicine sector has "high out-of-pocket costs".[^1]

The report lacks proportions for each reason, and cannot distinguish technique from communication. "Long wait times" appears only in the hospital sector, and is listed as an "other" item after technique, cursory, and explanation.

## How the Survey Was Conducted

This is a telephone survey. In 2024, valid samples per sector were: hospitals 2,075, Western medicine primary care 1,958, dental outpatient 2,010, Chinese medicine outpatient 1,985; at 95% confidence level, sampling error approximately ±3 percentage points.[^1]

Therefore the gap between 94.1% and 95.3% may be within the margin of error, and should not be directly interpreted as "Chinese medicine more satisfied than hospitals". The NHIA considers this hospital gap still within the margin of error, with all global budget sectors' 2024 satisfaction showing flat or slight increases.

The survey method has also changed. Since 2017, the survey has been gradually revised toward a "medical visit experience" direction; in 2024 a dual-frame telephone survey combining residential landlines and mobile phones was piloted, aiming to better reflect actual usage habits.[^1]

## How to Read 94.1%

The first reminder is the ceiling effect. When satisfaction stays long-term above 94%, marginal changes are hard to discern; the real signals are often in dissatisfaction and reasons for dissatisfaction.

The second reminder is sector differences. Hospital and Chinese medicine outpatient dissatisfaction rising, Western medicine primary care falling, dental outpatient rising then falling — this looks more like "differentiated experiences across sectors" rather than overall medical care worsening.

The NHIC recommends urging each global budget sector to strengthen publicity and study improvement measures targeting public dissatisfaction reasons, and continue observing trends.[^1]

Comparing with another figure gives more sense: the hospital emergency department boarding rate exceeding 24 hours is 3.68%, also rising.[^1] Long wait times and ED boarding may be two scenarios of the same experience.

## Reading Alongside Process Indicators

Satisfaction is subjective perception; the same report also has two sets of objective process indicators for comparison.

The ED boarding rate exceeding 24 hours: 2.56% in 2022, 3.32% in 2023, 3.68% in 2024, rising three consecutive years; inpatient surgical wound infection rate: 1.24%, 1.26%, 1.24%, 1.19%, 1.21%, largely flat.[^1] The former's direction aligns with hospital dissatisfaction, the latter shows no clear deterioration.

The two process indicators align directionally with hospital dissatisfaction, but the report does not link them; they are merely listed side by side.
