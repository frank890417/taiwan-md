In 2023, Taiwan's palliative care utilization rate in the year before death was 30.9%; the U.S. counterpart for the same year was 30.3%.[^1]

The same annual report also records another set of figures: as of end-2024, cumulative signings of advance directives for palliative care and life-sustaining treatment choices reached 1,031,936, covering approximately 5.2% of the population aged 18 and above; advance medical decisions totaled only 93,981, a signing rate of about 0.5%.[^1]

## The Meaning of 30.9%

The palliative care utilization rate measures "the proportion of decedents who received palliative services in the year before death." Taiwan's 2023 figure of 30.9% means roughly one in three decedents had contact with palliative inpatient, shared-care, or home-based services in their final year.[^1]

This figure does not look poor internationally. The annual report directly compares it with the U.S. 30.3% and notes the two are on par. The 2025 Global Palliative Care Map published by the ATLANTES Global Observatory of Palliative Care at the University of Navarra ranked Taiwan third worldwide, behind only Germany and the Netherlands, and first in Asia; Taiwan was sixth globally in 2015 and rose to third in 2021.[^1]

Both the ranking and the utilization rate indicate the system is in place, but they do not answer another question: how many people, while still healthy, have put their end-of-life medical choices in writing.

## Two Laws, Twenty-Five Years

Taiwan promulgated the Hospice Palliative Care Act in 2000; the minister's preface in the annual report calls it Asia's first law respecting the medical wishes of terminal patients and safeguarding their rights.[^1][^3]

In 2016 the Legislative Yuan passed the Patient Autonomy Act, which took effect in 2019 as Asia's first dedicated law protecting patient autonomy. It expanded applicability from terminal patients to five clinical conditions and allowed the public to use advance medical decisions to pre-select acceptance or refusal of life-sustaining treatment and artificial nutrition.[^1][^4]

In 2020, the Ministry of Health and Welfare (MOHW) integrated the operations of the "three laws for a good death"—the Human Organ Transplant Act, the Hospice Palliative Care Act, and the Patient Autonomy Act—into the Organ Donation and Transplant Registry and Patient Autonomy Promotion Center, which is also the publisher of the primary source for this article.[^1]

## 5.2% and 0.5%

The signing rates under the two laws differ greatly. Advance palliative care directives have accumulated since 2000, reaching over 1.03 million by end-2024; advance medical decisions only launched in 2019, accumulating 93,981.[^1] A MOHW press release for World Hospice and Palliative Care Day 2025 further notes that after the National Health Insurance (NHI) card added a channel for advance directives, online signings for advance palliative care directives reached 1.097 million.[^2]

Annual signing volume is also worth examining. Advance palliative care directives numbered 107,480 in 2018, then declined steadily to 55,189 in 2023, before rebounding to 87,835 in 2024. The annual report suggests the post-2020 decline may relate to constrained palliative care capacity during the pandemic.[^1]

The signatory profile is highly concentrated. In 2024, women accounted for 61.5%; those aged 60 and above combined for 57.8%, with the 60–69 cohort largest (26.0%), followed by 70–79 (20.2%); those under 39 made up only 10.1%.[^1]

The advance medical decision distribution is similar but skews further toward women and middle-to-older age: women 63.5%–67.1%. Averaging 2019–2024, the top three age groups are 60–69 (28.8%), 50–59 (22.2%), and 70–79 (17.8%).[^1]

## Distribution of 581 Institutions

In 2024, 581 institutions nationwide provided hospice palliative care: 85 inpatient, 168 shared-care, 488 home-based; by classification, 143 Class A, 377 Class B. Institution types included 187 hospitals, 257 home nursing agencies, 135 clinics and health centers, and 2 home respiratory care agencies.[^1]

Beds are another dimension. Nationwide there were 871 palliative inpatient beds, or about 4 per 100,000 population; the annual report notes this is comparable to Canada's palliative bed count per 100,000.[^1]

The European Association for Palliative Care recommends one home palliative team and one hospital palliative team per 100,000 population. By contrast, Taiwan has approximately 2.1 home palliative teams, 1.6 community home palliative teams, and 0.8 hospital palliative teams per 100,000.[^1] In other words, home-based capacity is relatively adequate, while hospital-side density remains below the recommended level.

## Who Signs, and in Which County/City

Advance palliative care directive signings are highly concentrated in the six special municipalities. Combined 2020–2024, the six accounted for over 70% of the national total; the five-year cumulative share was highest in New Taipei City at 20.1%, followed by Taipei City 12.3%, Kaohsiung City 12.1%, Taichung City 11.2%, and Tainan City 7.8%.[^1]

The annual report attributes this distribution to dense medical resources in metropolitan areas, yielding better promotion and implementation. Conversely, outlying islands show low signing rates, with the report explicitly stating "promotion and resource input still have room for strengthening."[^1]

Growth rates also reveal policy momentum. In 2024, New Taipei City saw the largest year-on-year increase in signings, followed by Taichung City.[^1]

## Workforce and the Pain That Is Seen

For the system to function, people must be on the ground. In 2024 Taiwan had 926 palliative care specialist physicians and 564 palliative care specialist nurses; 1,334 completed PGY2 palliative care training that year.[^1]

Continuing education capacity better reflects the daily reality of this work. In 2024, nurses led participation in palliative care continuing education at over 110,000 (person-times and hours listed separately in the report), physicians about 11,000, and social workers and psychologists in the thousands.[^1]

Two clinical quality indicators are also rising. Since 2012 the NHI added a "palliative care family consultation fee" to encourage physicians to hold family meetings for terminal inpatients; claimants grew from 46,732 in 2021 to 54,181 in 2024, a four-year increase of 15.9%.[^1]

On pain control, the 2023 opioid use rate for pain relief among cancer patients during end-of-life care was 87.7%.[^1] This figure indicates "medication was given," not "pain was fully controlled," but it at least shows opioid accessibility has been widely established.

## ACP: 279 Institutions and Two Thousand Sessions

The prerequisite for advance medical decisions is advance care planning (ACP). In 2024, 279 institutions nationwide provided this service: hospitals 59.5%, clinics 26.9%, health centers 13.3%.[^1]

Promotion activity volume is also substantial. In 2024, 2,048 Patient Autonomy Act promotion sessions were held with 233,366 participant-times; palliative care public promotion reached 1,981 sessions with 269,586 participant-times.[^1]

On professional training, 2024 saw 572 physician person-times, 2,215 nursing person-times, 265 social worker person-times, and 133 psychologist person-times complete the 4–6 hour ACP basic course.[^1]

Placing these numbers alongside the 0.5% signing rate reveals a gap: 279 counseling institutions, over 200,000 participant-times in promotion, yet only ~94,000 completed advance medical decisions. The annual report does not directly answer what lies in between; what is certain is that the distance is not in the quantity of promotional sessions.

## After Ranking Third

The 2025 ATLANTES assessment placed Taiwan third worldwide, following its sixth place in 2015 and third in 2021—again listed among the leaders by an international body; the minister's preface credits this achievement to medical teams and policy promotion.[^1]

But looking at the domestic distribution, the gaps are concrete: advance medical decisions at 0.5%, signers under 39 only 10.1%, low signing rates in outlying islands and non-metropolitan areas, hospital palliative team density at 0.8—still below the European recommendation of 1.[^1]

The Control Yuan's Audit Report on the 2021 Central Government General Final Accounts also raised the same issue: NHI palliative care service targets have gradually expanded, yet普及率 (popularization rate) remains to be raised, palliative resource allocation shows gaps, and some counties/cities have palliative bed occupancy rates below 50%.[^5]

The system itself is written; both laws have been in force for over twenty years. What remains is the hardest slogan: getting people, while still healthy, willing to sit down and talk once about how they want their final journey to go.
