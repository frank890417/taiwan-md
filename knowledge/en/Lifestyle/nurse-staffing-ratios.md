On May 8, 2026, the Legislative Yuan passed the third reading of amendments to the _Medical Care Act_, formally writing the three-shift nurse-to-patient ratio into law.[^2]

Minister of Health and Welfare Chiu Tai-yuan (石崇良) explained that evening that nursing groups had hoped for implementation in December 2027, but considering the year-end job-hopping wave and the Lunar New Year holiday, the ministry ultimately recommended May 1, 2028 as the effective date.[^2]

Between legislative passage and formal implementation lies roughly two years. The staffing that must be filled during those two years is the subject of this article.

## What the Standard Looks Like

The third-reading text stipulates that the first regulated three-shift nurse-to-patient ratio standard shall be based on the one effective March 1, 2024.[^2]

For medical centers: day shift 1:6, evening shift 1:9, night shift 1:11; for regional hospitals: 1:7, 1:11, 1:13; for district hospitals: 1:10, 1:13, 1:15.[^2]

This standard is stricter than the original "all-day average nurse-to-patient ratio." The original facility standard looked only at the all-day average: medical centers ≤9 patients per nursing staff member, regional hospitals ≤12, district hospitals ≤15.[^1]

The difference lies in "average" versus "by shift." The all-day average can mix low night-shift volume with high day-shift volume; the three-shift system requires every single shift to meet the standard.

## Turnover and Vacancy Rates

To understand why a buffer period is needed, one must first look at the workforce attrition numbers.

The Legislative Yuan Budget Center's September 2024 assessment report, citing MOHW data, shows the nationwide hospital nursing staff turnover rate rose from 11.12% in FY 2019 to 12.61% in FY 2023 (112th year), while the vacancy rate climbed from 4.52% to 9.05% — both higher than pre-pandemic levels.[^4]

In 2024, the NHI Administration added the "Strengthening Inpatient Nursing Capacity Program," citing "post-pandemic nursing workforce attrition may lead to insufficient care staff and rising nurse-to-patient ratios," and used night-shift incentives to encourage retention.[^1]

In other words, between 2023 and 2024 the system simultaneously saw "people left" and "the workload on those who stayed got heavier."

## That Nurse-to-Patient Ratio Line: 42.7% to 8.5%

The figure in the NHI supervisory report that best illustrates the changing workload is the share of months where medical centers' all-day average nurse-to-patient ratio fell below 7.0.

In 2022 (111th year) it was 42.7%; in 2023 (112th year) it dropped to 10.3%; in 2024 (113th year) only 8.5% remained.[^1]

This decline is not good news. A lower nurse-to-patient ratio means each nurse cares for fewer patients; the share of months below 7.0 falling from four-tenths to less than one-tenth means the ratio in the remaining months shifted above 7.0. The report also notes that the ratio is affected by changes in patient volume and bed count (medical center acute general beds increased by 1,881 compared to end-2023).

The other end of the same chart is also growing. The share of months where medical centers' ratio fell in the 8.0–8.4 range reached 18.2% in 2024; the 8.5–8.9 range accounted for 5.5%.[^1]

```tw-line
Medical center all-day average nurse-to-patient ratio below 7.0 — monthly share (%)
2022 | 42.7 | Four-tenths of months below 7.0
2023 | 10.3 | Sharp decline
2024 | 8.5 | Less than one-tenth
Source: NHI Supervisory Indicator Monitoring Results Report 2024, Indicator 2.4
```

For regional hospitals, the <10.0 monthly share fell from 78.8% in 2022 to 58.2% in 2023, then rebounded to 61.9% in 2024 (up 3.7 percentage points from 2023).[^1]

## Compliance Bonus Share: District Hospitals Lowest

Another angle is the "bonus-eligible monthly share" — the proportion of months hospitals reported qualifying for the inpatient nursing care add-on payment.

In 2024, medical centers 99.1% (100% in 2022 and 2023; the 2024 dip due to one medical center undergoing renovation), regional hospitals 99.3% (99.2% in 2022, 99.0% in 2023), district hospitals 90.9% (91.8% in 2022, 92.4% in 2023).[^1]

```tw-bars
2024 all-day average nurse-to-patient ratio bonus-eligible monthly share (%)
Regional hospitals | 99.3 | Highest of three tiers
Medical centers | 99.1 | 100% in 2022–2023
District hospitals | 90.9 | Lowest of three tiers
Source: NHI Supervisory Indicator Monitoring Results Report 2024, Indicator 2.4
```

District hospitals have been the lowest for three consecutive years. But in 2024, only 0.3% of months were truly "non-compliant"; the remaining ~8.8% were "not applicable" (no inpatient cases admitted that month or not reported).[^1]

## Money: NT$33.5 Billion and NT$27.5 Billion

System investment can be divided into two ledgers.

From 2009 to 2024, the NHI side continuously allocated inpatient nursing-related budgets totaling NT$33.5 billion, used to adjust inpatient nursing care payment standards; in 2025 an additional NT$6.5 billion from the general budget was injected to strengthen inpatient care capacity.[^1]

The Executive Yuan then approved the "12-Strategy Nursing Workforce Policy Preparation Plan" in July 2024, spanning 2025–2028, totaling NT$27.5 billion.[^2]

The item most directly tied to retention is incentives. Chiu Tai-yuan explained that starting in 2024, NHI budget has funded night-shift subsidies, and plans roughly NT$2 billion annually in "three-shift nurse-to-patient ratio compliance bonuses," with funds entirely for nursing staff, usable flexibly by hospitals for day-shift, specialist nurses, or holiday allowances.[^2]

## Two Senses of Time

In the same meeting, the medical and nursing communities held different views on "when it should take effect."

According to CNA, attending Yang Ming Hospital Superintendent Hsieh Ching-hsiang (謝景祥) said the medical community worries nursing staff cannot be filled in time and hopes for a buffer period; otherwise it would not be a matter of short-term or long-term pain, but continuous pain.[^2]

Nursing Association Federation Chairperson Chen Li-chin (陳麗琴) stated that nursing groups hoped for December 2027 implementation, while the MOHW's proposed May 2028 "differs by several months"; but she emphasized the key is that the buffer period must not be a vacuum — it must have substantive supporting measures.[^2]

The MOHW's observation metric is the compliance rate. Chiu said the nationwide three-shift nurse-to-patient ratio compliance rate has risen from under 50% in the first half of 2025 to 70% in the second half.[^2]

## Penalties and Monitoring

Once enacted, consequences for non-improvement are written into law. By tier: district hospitals fined NT$250,000–500,000; hospitals NT$500,000–1,000,000; medical centers NT$1,000,000–2,000,000 (mountain and offshore medical institutions excepted), fines may be imposed per occurrence continuously; three cumulative failures to correct result in suspension of operations for 1 month to 1 year.[^3]

The monitoring mechanism returns to the NHI. Chiu said future monitoring will use NHI global budget to track inpatient service volume; if a hospital shows abnormal volume reduction, its individual global budget base period will be adjusted, and a complaint platform will be established to supervise bonus fund flow.[^2]

Putting these lines together, the significance of writing the three-shift nurse-to-patient ratio into law is not merely adding a standard, but turning "nursing workforce" from a hospital internal management issue into a public indicator with legal provisions, penalties, incentives, and monitoring.

Whether May 1, 2028 can truly launch, and whether the medical center line will rebound afterward, depends on the compliance and turnover rates over the next two years. The NHI's annually published monthly nurse-to-patient ratio shares will be the first place to verify.[^1][^2]
