> **30-second overview:** On January 11, 2021, Taiwan issued its new chip passport. The cover enlarges "TAIWAN" but retains "REPUBLIC OF CHINA" and the national emblem. This seemingly minor adjustment of text and layout did not change the passport's legal nature, yet it enabled a travel document to more clearly address three things: who issues it, where the holder comes from, and how border personnel should identify it.[^1] [^2]

At airport counters, passengers usually only confirm name, validity, and photo. The cover stays in hand only briefly—too briefly to resemble a political object. It is more like a tool jointly used by airlines, immigration officers, and automated clearance gates. Yet the cover of the Taiwan passport is precisely amplifying a place name that needs to be recognized to the most prominent position.[^2]

![2021 Republic of China (Taiwan) Passport Cover](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/REPUBLIC_OF_CHINA_%28TAIWAN%29_Passport_2021.png)

The contrast here is: **The more a passport resembles a daily necessity, the more it can carry the most difficult-to-articulate questions of the state.** It not only proves that a person can travel, but also brings the issuing authority, nationality law, international travel, and identity naming to every border. This article does not rank passports, nor treat visa counts as a measure of state value. We simply examine how this small booklet operates between institutions and daily life.

## What Got Bigger Isn't the Country Name, But the Opportunity to Be Seen

In September 2020, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs announced the design direction for the new passport. The official explanation for the redesign was "minimal changes": retaining the Republic of China, `REPUBLIC OF CHINA`, the national emblem, `TAIWAN`, `PASSPORT`, and the original colors. The main adjustment was enlarging `TAIWAN` and placing it closer to `PASSPORT`, forming a more recognizable visual message of "Taiwan's passport."[^2]

The design did not remove all old elements. The English official country name remains printed, encircling the national emblem. The change was a reorganization of visual weight within the same cover.[^2] Thus, the 2021 new version is not a renaming that clears away the old version, but a method of adjusting recognizability within existing systems.

When announcing the design, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs stated that the redesign aimed to enhance the Taiwan recognizability of the passport cover and protect the travel rights and convenience of overseas nationals. The announcement also recorded that the case was decided by the Legislative Yuan and then reported to the Executive Yuan for approval.[^2] These procedures show that the cover is not a poster decided by designers alone, but a public document jointly handled by administrative, legislative, and diplomatic practice.

> **📝 Curator's note:** The most interesting aspect of the passport cover is not which character it chose, but that it must allow two systems of recognition to coexist simultaneously: legal documents must be precise, airport clearance must be fast, and the holder hopes others will recognize Taiwan at a glance.

On January 11, 2021, the new chip passport was officially issued. Applications can be accepted both domestically and at overseas missions. Even if the current passport has a long validity period, it can continue to be used until expiration, without needing to replace it immediately due to the cover redesign. Regular passport fees remain NT$1,300 per book, and children under 14 pay NT$900.[^1]

This detail separates symbolism from administrative cost. The cover can change, but the holder's travel rights need not be interrupted. "Looking more like Taiwan" and "having to pay for another application" are not the same thing. The new recognizability is a policy choice; the continued validity of old passports is administrative continuity.

## A Passport Is First a Set of Qualifications, Not a Book

Whether a passport can be obtained is first determined by rules. The current "Passport Application and Issuance Measures" stipulates that the competent authority is the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Applications for ordinary passports can be submitted to the Bureau of Consular Affairs, Ministry of Foreign Affairs, overseas missions, or diplomatic missions.[^3]

For those registered in Taiwan, first-time applicants for an ordinary passport must submit a National ID card. Children under 14 who have not yet received an ID card may submit a household register or a household registration extract issued within the past three months.[^3] These documents appear to be part of the administrative process, but in reality they connect "who I am" to household registration and nationality data.

The rules also do not treat all holders as the same type of person. Different application criteria, supporting documents, validity periods, and passport endorsements apply to: stateless persons residing in Taiwan, naturalized nationals who have not yet settled their household registration, and those with identities from mainland China, Hong Kong, or Macao who are permitted to reside in Taiwan.[^3]

The "Passport Law" defines passports as international travel documents and nationality certificates, specifying that passports are divided into diplomatic, official, and ordinary categories. Diplomatic and official passports are issued by the competent authority; ordinary passports may be issued by the competent authority or overseas missions. Ordinary passports are generally valid for ten years; those under 14 are valid for five years. Diplomatic and official passports are limited to five years.[^10]

Therefore, the blue cover of the passport is merely the entrance; what truly supports it is a series of judgments that will never be printed on the cover: how the applicant's identity is verified, which agency is responsible for issuance, whether additional endorsements are needed, and how long the document remains valid. A passport does not flatten all identities into one, but rather delegates differences to regulations.

The same institutional traces appear in replacement procedures for lost passports. Domestically, replacement of a lost ordinary passport generally requires a police-issued lost report along with other application documents. Replacement passports are valid for five years; those under 14 are valid for three years. For those urgently needing a passport abroad and unable to wait for the standard chip passport processing, a one-year validity passport without an embedded chip can be applied for at overseas missions.[^3]

These provisions remind us that a passport is not a binary "have or have not" object. It may be an ordinary passport, a diplomatic or official passport, a replacement for a lost passport, or a temporary solution in an emergency. Each form answers the same question: how does the state confirm identity while allowing people to continue moving.

The system is also extending the counter online. The Bureau of Consular Affairs, Ministry of Foreign Affairs announced in 2026 that conditionally online passport renewal will be implemented starting January 1, 2025. From July 1, 2026, adults with household registration in Taiwan who currently hold a passport that has expired or has less than one year of validity, have not lost it, and whose data has not changed, can use their personal digital certificate to apply for renewal, with the announcement noting that over 110,000 people had already applied through this measure by that time.[^11]

This change does not remove the passport from physical form. The application process moves online, but pickup still requires presenting identification and the current passport at a designated location. What changes is how data reaches the issuing authority, not how the document is verified at the border.[^10] [^11]

## From Paper Pages to Chips, Borders Begin Reading the Invisible

The Taiwan passport did not become electronic only in 2021.

![1946 Republic of China Passport Image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Republic_of_China_Passport_1946.jpg)

Image source and licensing: [Wikimedia Commons file page: Republic of China Passport 1946](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Republic_of_China_Passport_1946.jpg) — Original source: Hsinchu City Cultural Bureau collection; creator: Ho Yun-yuan. File page indicates public domain under Taiwan law.

According to Ministry of Foreign Affairs data, the second-generation chip passport was issued starting December 25, 2017, featuring a third image of the holder, metallic surface-raised anti-counterfeiting film, and updated inner page designs.[^4]

The significance of the chip is not merely "an electronic component added inside." ICAO Doc 9303 is the specification entry point for machine-readable travel documents, including TD3-sized machine-readable passports and other machine-readable travel documents.[^5] This means that for a passport to function across international borders, it must simultaneously comply with domestic issuance systems and international machine-readable standards.

ICAO also clarifies that the chip of an electronic passport must be verified during border inspection. If the chip is not verified, the advantages of an electronic passport over a traditional non-electronic passport are limited.[^6] Thus, a passport has two readers: one is the traveler holding it and looking at the cover, and the other is the system checking the authenticity of the data—usually not fully displaying the results to the traveler.

This change splits "being recognized" into two levels. The human eye needs to see `TAIWAN` and `PASSPORT` within seconds. The machine must verify whether the chip data is trustworthy. The cover is responsible for reducing misidentification; the chip and international verification mechanisms are responsible for reducing the risks of forgery and impersonation. The two do not replace each other, but rather assign the same clearance issue to different readers.

> **📝 Curator's note:** Electronic passports have not made the state disappear; instead, they have made the state become more layered interfaces: the cover for human eyes, the machine-readable zone for devices to scan, the chip for system verification, and the law determines who qualifies to hold it.

## Visa-Free Is Not the Single Score of a Passport

On July 23, 2026, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs Bureau of Consular Affairs updated the "Travel Information for Overseas Nationals," listing countries and regions where visa-free, landing visa, or e-visa entry is available, and reminding that visa and immigration laws of each country will change, so actual treatment should be based on the latest announcement by the destination government. Generally, countries also require the passport to have at least six months of validity.[^7]

This reminder is easily overlooked, because when people discuss passports, they often think of "visa-free" as a fixed capability of a passport. In reality, visa-free is another country's entry arrangement for specific travel conditions. It may be affected by travel purpose, length of stay, passport validity, return ticket, or other documents.[^7]

In other words, a passport is not a membership card with a fixed score written on it. It is a document that allows other governments to identify the holder and then decide, according to their own laws, whether to grant convenience. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs attaches the warning "reconfirm before departure" to its list precisely because the ease of travel changes with the other party's policies, and cannot be judged solely by the numbers circulating online.[^7]

Earlier Ministry of Foreign Affairs data also recorded that holders of the Republic of China passport can enjoy visa-free, landing visa, or e-visa conveniences. These conveniences are very practical for travelers, but they do not equate to full diplomatic recognition, nor can they alone represent the effectiveness of a passport in all scenarios. Equating visa convenience directly with state status flattens a set of complex entry decisions into a single number.

Border personnel of the destination country also handle more than just a cover name. They must verify whether the passport is valid, whether it meets machine-readable and chip verification standards, whether the traveler meets visa-free conditions, and whether additional visas are required.[^5] [^6] What passengers feel at the counter is a stamp or a clearance, but behind it is a multi-system alignment within seconds.

## The 2025 Shortage Reveals the Supply Chain Behind the Cover

Passports are usually quiet until inventory issues arise. In April 2025, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs responded to inquiries about whether the passport shortage was related to the 2021 redesign, stating that the shortage was related to insufficient safety stock and budget cuts that year, and denying that the redesign caused the shortage.[^9]

The value of this controversy lies not in concluding for either side, but in revealing that a passport is no longer just about the cover and regulations. A passport also needs blank booklets, chips, special anti-counterfeiting materials, printing, and inventory management. Any discrepancy in the chain may cause passengers to discover at the moment they truly need to depart that "having a valid passport" is not automatically guaranteed.[^1] [^9]

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs announcement in 2021 clearly stated that replacing the new version is not mandatory, and old passports can be used until expiration.[^1] This arrangement reduced the impact of the redesign on application volume at the time. However, when travel demand recovers and inventory and budget become public issues again, the materiality of the passport resurfaces: it is not an identity in the cloud, but an item that must be printed, verified, stored, and delivered.

This is also why "design" cannot be understood as merely aesthetics. How much the text is enlarged and where elements are placed affects recognition. How chips and anti-counterfeiting measures are configured affects inspection. How print volume and budget are arranged affects whether people can depart on schedule. The cover, system, and supply chain together constitute the actual function of the passport.

> **📝 Curator's note:** A credential is truly reliable when no one notices it. Only when the cover is misidentified, the chip cannot be verified, or the inventory is insufficient do we see that "the state" is actually a service chain that needs to be maintained every day.

## How a Small Booklet Carries Identity Across Borders

A passport also has an often-overlooked function: it temporarily separates "nationality" from "travel eligibility." Whether the applicant holds Republic of China nationality is an issue for the issuing side. Whether the holder can enter a country visa-free is a decision made by the destination side based on its own laws and current conditions. The passport carries the former judgment to the face of the latter, but cannot pre-guarantee the result of the latter.[^3] [^7]

This distinction is important for travelers. A valid passport may still require a visa. A traveler enjoying visa-free convenience may also be unable to enter directly due to passport validity, travel purpose, return arrangements, or the latest regulations of the destination country. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs placing "reconfirm before departure" at the front of the overseas information is not bureaucratic formality, but an acknowledgment that cross-border travel is jointly decided by multiple sovereignty systems.[^7]

### Traveling to Mainland China: Mainly Using the Mainland Travel Permit, Not Using the Passport as an Entry Document

For Taiwan residents traveling to Mainland China, the main document used is the "Mainland Travel Permit for Taiwan Residents," commonly known as the "Taiwan Compatriot Permit" (hereinafter referred to as the "permit"). It is not the same document as the Republic of China passport, and its functions cannot be substituted for each other. The service guide of the National Immigration Administration of Mainland China lists the permit as a special document for Taiwan residents to travel to and from Mainland China, requiring application forms, photos, and verification of Taiwan ID cards and exit-entry documents from the Taiwan region.[^12]

Therefore, when discussing "where the Taiwan passport can go," one cannot simply include Mainland China in the general visa-free or visa list. For Taiwan residents, the entry, exit, and stay in Mainland China are practically handled through a valid permit. The National Immigration Administration service guide lists two types of permits: one valid for multiple entries over five years, and another valid for a single entry within three months. If arriving at the port without a valid document, there is also a system for applying for a single-entry permit.[^12]

The relationship between the two documents can be distinguished as follows:

| Document                                                               | Main Function                                                                             | In the Context of Traveling to Mainland China                                                                             |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Republic of China Passport                                             | International travel document and nationality certificate issued by the Taiwan government | Cannot be used alone to replace the permit as the main document for Taiwan residents traveling to and from Mainland China |
| Mainland Travel Permit for Taiwan Residents (Taiwan Compatriot Permit) | Travel permit issued by Mainland China for Taiwan residents                               | Used for Taiwan residents' travel to and from, entry, and stay in Mainland China                                          |

This distinction is especially clear when documents are lost. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs Bureau of Consular Affairs Q&A states that when a Republic of China passport is lost in Mainland China, in addition to reporting to the local public security department, one must also handle a temporary permit to return to Taiwan through the relevant route; another Q&A also lists the documents required for returning to Taiwan due to expired or lost passports as two separate items: "passport" and "permit," showing their different functions in the return process.[^13] [^14] In other words, the passport handles the international travel identity of Taiwan nationals, while the permit handles the travel eligibility between Taiwan residents and Mainland China.

The validity of a passport is not judged solely by whether the cover is damaged. The machine-readable zone, photo, data page, and chip together form the inspection target. ICAO specifications handle how travel documents are read by machines. PKD handles the verification trust chain of electronic passports. Travelers may not see these procedures, but automated clearance equipment and manual inspection must determine within a limited time whether the document is trustworthy.[^5] [^6]

This makes the passport a very special public interface. The interface of a general website serves a single logged-in user, but the passport interface simultaneously serves the holder, the issuing authority, the airline, the immigration officer of the destination country, and the border equipment. Each party reads different content: travelers care about name and validity, airlines care about whether they can carry the passenger, machines care about whether the data can be verified, and the destination country decides whether to allow entry based on its own rules.[^1] [^3] [^7]

When these readings are completed smoothly, the traveler will think they have simply "gone abroad." Only when data is inconsistent, chip reading fails, the passport is lost, or visa conditions change does this layer of interface suddenly gain weight. At that moment, the passport is no longer an item kept in a pocket, but a fine thread connecting personal identity to the systems of several countries.

Therefore, passport redesign cannot be evaluated solely by "whether the cover looks good." The 2021 adjustment handled human visual recognition. The second-generation chip and anti-counterfeiting design of 2017 handled document credibility. The application measures handled who can obtain it, where, and how to replace it after loss. The visa information handled how another country responds to this document.[^2] [^3] [^4] [^7]

Returning to the airport, the traveler only needs to hand over the passport. This action looks private, but it simultaneously triggers several public judgments: whether the issuing authority is trustworthy, whether the document is still valid, whether the holder matches the photo and data, whether the chip can be verified, and whether the destination country provides corresponding visa convenience.[^3] [^5] [^6] [^7]

The 2021 cover enlarged `TAIWAN`, did not eliminate the legal text, and did not independently create visa-free treatment. What it did was make Taiwan more visible to the human eye on a highly standardized travel document. The rest of the work is still completed by passport regulations, international machine-readable standards, chip verification, and each country's entry policies.[^2] [^5] [^6]

This division of labor also explains why the meaning of a passport cannot be judged solely by the cover. The cover provides recognition clues, the law provides the basis for issuance, the chip provides machine-verifiable data, and visa policies determine whether the traveler can enter another country. No single layer can represent the entire passport alone, but if any layer is missing, travel may stop at the counter.

Therefore, the most memorable thing about the Taiwan passport is not the list of "where it can go," but how it transforms abstract identity into an operable document. It prints the country name on the cover, writes qualifications into regulations, places anti-counterfeiting in materials, stores data in chips, and delivers this entire arrangement to border systems around the world for reading.

## After Leaving the Counter, the Passport Still Needs to Be Properly Stored

In the end, the passport still returns to the traveler's hands. It may be tucked into the inner pocket of a backpack, waiting for the next departure. It may also be filed away in a drawer, only to be reopened when the validity period is near. At that moment, the text on the cover, the data on the page, and the verification in the chip all work together, allowing a person with a document recognized by the system to walk toward another system's door.

But this "taking it along" also means the holder must bear the responsibility of custody. When a passport is lost, replacement is not as simple as mailing a new booklet. The applicant must first complete a lost report, then submit documents according to location and application channel. Domestic and overseas procedures differ, and overseas emergencies may first obtain a one-year validity passport without an embedded chip.[^3]

The regulations detail these situations precisely because cross-border identity cannot be completed with a mere verbal explanation. Document loss, insufficient validity, or data inconsistency may all cause the holder to be forced to stop at the moment they most need to move. The convenience of a passport has never been unconditional freedom, but rather is based on the consistency of identity data, document security, and inspection procedures.[^3] [^6]

When the passport is put back into the drawer, these rules do not disappear. They simply retreat to the background of daily life, waiting for the next trip to call them back.

The Taiwan passport does not resolve the identity question into a single sentence. It simply turns the question into a daily object that can be carried, inspected, and crossed—and can be redesigned. **The more routinely it is opened, the closer it is to how a country introduces itself to the world and how it demands the world to read it correctly.**
