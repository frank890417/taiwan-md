> **30-Second Overview:** In 1994, due to poor management at a seafood stall in Tainan's Xiao Bei Night Market, founder Huang Pu-wen decided to invest his remaining NT$1 million to remodel it into a hardware and grocery store. Following an accidental late-night encounter where a customer knocked on the door seeking help, Xiao Bei pioneered Taiwan's first 24-hour hardware retail model. Rather than chasing modern retail "sales per square foot," the store insists on keeping low-turnover, niche items—ranging from professional plumbing parts to cassette tapes. Today, under second-generation leadership, Xiao Bei is navigating the growing pains and rebirth of digital transformation, attempting to preserve its "human-centric convenience" in the era of e-commerce.

### From Seafood Stir-fry to Hardware Department Store: A Grand Transformation

In the 1990s, Tainan's Xiao Bei Night Market was a hub of gourmet food and crowds. At the time, Huang Pu-wen (黃卜文), who had contracted polio, operated a seafood stir-fry restaurant with his brothers. Although business was once prosperous, profits gradually declined as competition in the catering market intensified[^1]. In 1994, facing the greatest crisis since starting his business, Huang decided to close the seafood stall and invest his remaining NT$1 million into setting up a simple shed on the same site. He pivoted to selling affordable hardware and groceries, retaining the name of the night market: "Xiao Bei Department Store" (小北百貨) [^2].

The early days of the transition were difficult; Huang once suffered losses of up to NT$500,000 due to theft. To protect his life's work, he decided to sleep directly in the shop. One late night, a sudden knocking at the door woke him—it was a customer in urgent need of daily necessities. This experience of "opening late at night" gave Huang a flash of inspiration: if convenience stores could operate 24 hours a day, why couldn't a hardware store?[^3] His brother, Huang Yi-hung (黃義弘), later recalled this secret to success with a tone that was casual yet revealed a fierce determination:

> "Because we always do what others don't want to do, sell what others don't want to sell, and keep the talent that others cannot hold onto!" [^6]

### The 24-Hour "Parental Lifesaver"

On Taiwanese social media platforms, the title most frequently attributed to Xiao Bei Department Store is "Parental Lifesaver." Many parents have had similar experiences: late at night, a child suddenly remembers they need colored paper, a recorder, or a jump rope for tomorrow's art class. With most stores already closed, the brightly lit Xiao Bei Department Store becomes the only hope[^9]. This social function of "emergency relief" has allowed Xiao Bei to build an extremely high emotional connection within the Taiwanese retail market[^4].

Xiao Bei's display philosophy runs contrary to the "sales per square foot" efficiency pursued by modern convenience stores. They insist on "selling what others won't," even if it means stocking low-turnover, niche items that might only sell a few times a year—such as cassette tapes, specialized toilet fittings, fisherman's aprons, or even rice sieves used for traditional wedding rituals[^5]. Store managers are intimately familiar with these obscure goods; when a customer asks for a "_mā-tā_" (罵瘩, baseball bat) using the Taiwanese pronunciation, staff can immediately point out its exact location on a specific floor and aisle[^5].

### Decision-Making for Those Who Hear the Cannon Fire

Unlike many chain systems that rely heavily on centralized management, Xiao Bei Department Store grants its store managers immense authority. At Xiao Bei, full-time employees make up over 90% of the workforce, and most managers are veteran employees who even hold shares in their respective stores[^6]. During his lifetime, Huang Pu-wen had a unique method for selecting talent: he would occasionally put an extra NT$1,000 in an employee's pay envelope to see if they would proactively return it, using this to select honest and trustworthy cadres[^3].

This "people-oriented" management style is reflected in the differentiated operations of each store. For example, stores near industrial zones will specifically increase stock of the strong-scented shampoos preferred by migrant workers, while stores near school districts will place stationery and craft tools in the most prominent positions. Even traditions that place a heavier burden on employees, such as "water carrying services" (helping customers carry heavy crates of water to their cars), are regarded as an unshakable corporate spirit[^15].

### The Young General's Digital Growing Pains and Vision

In 2017, founder Huang Pu-wen passed away suddenly from a myocardial infarction at the age of 54[^7]. The following year, his second-generation successor, Huang Chien-tang (黃健瑭), took over as General Manager at the age of 28, embarking on the digitalization journey of this traditional hardware business. This is a "boss-level" challenge: Xiao Bei skipped physical membership cards and moved directly to a digital App-based membership system, which has now surpassed 1.5 million members[^4][^16].

However, the greatest challenge of succession lies not in the systems, but in communicating with the older generation. Huang Chien-tang once stated candidly:

> "90% of my uncle's judgments were correct, which is why we are where we are today, but times have changed." [^15]

Facing competition from Poya (寶雅) and its "Baojia" (寶家) brand, Huang Chien-tang has been investing NT$1 billion to build a smart logistics center in Hukou, Hsinchu, while simultaneously insisting on retaining those items that are "not profitable but serve the people." He refused to follow industry trends by canceling bagging services, believing that when others pursue efficiency at the expense of service, Xiao Bei's "doing a little more" becomes its strongest competitive differentiator[^15].
