# Golden Melody Awards: Taiwan Awarding Its Own Music, and the Moments to Remember for a Lifetime

> **30-Second Overview:** The Golden Melody Awards (GMA) is where Taiwan awards its own music. Since its first ceremony in 1990, it has reached its 37th edition by 2026. It began with a letter written by singer Tsai Chin to the Director of the Government Information Office; its judges vote behind closed doors, deliberately ignoring sales figures; it has crowned the biggest names in Mandopop like <a href="/people/周杰倫" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Jay Chou</a>, <a href="/people/張惠妹" target="_blank" rel="noopener">A-Mei</a>, and <a href="/people/蔡依林" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Jolin Tsai</a>, while also granting its highest honors to those singing in Taiwanese, Hakka, and Indigenous languages. Over thirty-five years, it has recorded how this island listens to itself.

On June 29, 2024, at the Taipei Arena, <a href="/people/草東沒有派對" target="_blank" rel="noopener">No Party For Cao Dong</a> swept three major awards: Album of the Year, Best Mandarin Album, and Best Band. This was a rare feat in GMA history—the second time a single band had swept three major categories in one year[^17]. The audience sat waiting for them to take the stage.

But it wasn't the band members who walked up; it was their manager. Drummer Tsai Yi-fan (known to everyone as Fan-fan) passed away three years ago at the age of 26[^19]. Facing the microphone, the manager said what needed to be said, and ended with three words: "Thank you, Fan-fan."

The entire hall fell silent for a few seconds.

Those few seconds perfectly illustrate what the Golden Melody Awards are about. It is not a "whoever is most popular wins" leaderboard; it is where Taiwan awards its own music and decides which voices deserve to be remembered. A band's drummer is gone, but her drumming remains on the album _Ugly Slave_ (醜奴兒); three years later, when her band stands on the highest stage for a second time, the entire industry is willing to pause and remain silent for a name. This weight is something accumulated over thirty-five years.

_No Party For Cao Dong 〈Da Feng Chui〉. This song led them to sweep three major awards—Song of the Year, Best New Artist, and Best Band—at the 28th GMA in 2017, winning with an overwhelming 18 and 19 votes in the first round of voting. Fan-fan's drumming is right there in it._

In the following article, we want to introduce you to this award: how it grew from a single letter, what happens inside that closed-door voting room, which superstars it has crowned, how it provides stages for four different languages, and how even its critics have stood upon its stage. Thirty-five years—did you know?—is actually a very long story.

## A Letter, and a Director Whose Whole Family Were Her Fans

The starting point of the Golden Melody Awards was a letter written in 1982 by a folk singer in her twenties.

That year, Tsai Chin was at the height of her fame; almost everyone could hum 〈Qia Si Ni De Wen Rou〉 (Just Like Your Tenderness). Feeling that while Europe and America had large pop music award ceremonies like the Grammys or the AMAs, Taiwan—as a major hub for Mandopop—lacked even a national-level music award, she sat down and wrote an eight-page letter. She sent it to the then-Director of the Government Information Office, James Soong (宋楚瑜), suggesting that Taiwan host an "award ceremony specifically for Mandopop singers"[^2].

She later recalled that the letter was extremely detailed, even listing potential award categories one by one. Years later, when she recounted this on a TV show, host Ma Shih-fang laughed and said, "You even wrote out the national constitution!"

> 💡 **Did you know?** The original copy of Tsai Chin's letter has never been made public, so no one can verify word-for-word exactly which awards she listed. What we do know is what she later said herself on the PTS program _Music All Around_: she had even pre-listed the award categories. For a singer in her twenties to be serious enough to design the entire framework of a ceremony—that in itself is very Taiwanese.

Even more wonderful was Soong's reaction. According to Tsai Chin, when the Director received the letter and saw the name, he replied: "Miss Tsai Chin, how can you say in your letter that you are 'the Tsai Chin who wears glasses and sings 〈Just Like Your Tenderness〉'?! You don't need to introduce yourself; my whole family is your fan!" Two days later, he invited her to the Government Information Office.

_Tsai Chin 〈Forgotten Times〉. The person who wrote the letter to James Soong suggesting the GMA was that "Tsai Chin who sings while wearing glasses." An idea from a single singer eventually grew into one of the most important music ceremonies in the Sinophone world._

There is still a long road for an idea to become a national ceremony. The Government Information Office first held three warm-up events called "Singing Good Songs Together" starting in 1986, and only began formal planning for the Golden Melody Awards at the end of 1988. Applications opened in August 1989, nominees were announced in September, and the ceremony was originally intended to be held earlier, but due to the renovation of the National Sun Yat-sen Memorial Hall, it was delayed until January 6, 1990[^1].

That night at the Taipei National Sun Yat-sen Memorial Hall, Tsui Tai-ching (崔苔菁) entered in formal wear to host, accompanied by the orchestra playing the intro to her famous song 〈Love God〉. Twenty-five judges, twenty-eight record companies, and 410 entries eventually resulted in eleven awards. The King of Song was Yin Cheng-yang, the Queen was Jiang Hui, the New Artist award went to Wu Ssu-kai, and the Song of the Year was Zhao Chuan's 〈I Am Ugly, But I Am Very Gentle〉[^3].

![Taipei National Sun Yat-sen Memorial Hall—The venue for the 1st Golden Melody Awards in 1990. Due to renovations, the ceremony was delayed until January 6th.](/article-images/music/sun-yat-sen-hall-1990-gma-venue.webp)

The first ceremony was actually quite simple: no red carpet, no large-scale performances like those seen later, and even the "Best Vocal Album" award hadn't been established yet—that came in the second year. But the framework was set. A letter, a Director whose family were all fans, and several years of preparation meant that from then on, Taiwan had one night every year dedicated to deciding "which voices are worth remembering this year."

## The Room with Closed Doors

To understand why a Golden Melody is so highly regarded, one must first look at how they are judged.

The judging system of the GMA can be summarized as a group of people locked in a room, listening intently to all the works, and then voting. It sounds simple, but the difficulty lies in the "intently" and the "locked."

The process is divided into three rounds: during the preliminary round, judges listen to songs individually via an online system and score them independently; the second and final rounds move into closed-door meetings. The final decision is made on the morning of the ceremony, where judges vote anonymously via computer to select the winners to be announced that night.

> 📝 **Curator's Note:** Many people's intuitive reaction to "closed-door judging" is "black box" or "cliques." But by locking the room, the GMA is actually trying to block a different kind of force—the market. When all judges are isolated, forbidden from looking at sales figures, and prohibited from being lobbied, the only thing they can rely on is their own ears and professional judgment. In other words, that closed door blocks the inertia of "whoever sells well should win." Half of a Golden Melody's credibility comes from this door.

How strictly is this secrecy maintained? _Blow_ (吹音樂), which has reported on the event, described the scene of the final round: judges enter an isolated small room; they are accompanied by staff for smoking and restroom breaks; they cannot speak to anyone on the way, and they vote anonymously via computer until the results appear. Even mobile phones are collected en masse and only returned after the final award is presented[^4].

This isn't a legend. When Chen Hsien-ching (陳嫺靜), a winner of the 37th GMA in 2026, took the stage, she joked that she had prepared a speech but "helplessly had her phone taken away"—so she had to improvise. The fact that phones move constantly between judges and winners is itself the best footnote to this secrecy mechanism.

So how are judges selected? The official stance is to look for "foresight" and works with definite artistic value that do not necessarily follow commercial market trends. Di-la (迪拉), the manager of Yan She (顏社) who has served as a judge for three terms, offered a more colloquial standard. He said that when faced with a difficult choice between two excellent albums, he asks himself one question:

> "Would I want to buy this record?"

The charm of this phrase lies in how it condenses the criteria of an industry award into the most honest impulse of a music lover: _Would I personally spend my own money on this?_ Di-la also said something to those who feel that "not winning means losing": "Being nominated is recognition; it's real."[^5]

Of course, this system is not without its critics. Some say it lacks explicit selection criteria and relies too heavily on the professional ethics of individual judges; others say that when one person is nominated for multiple awards, judges tend to spread the awards out. These discussions are constant: but it is precisely because people care and discuss it that what happens in that room is worth taking seriously.

## The Golden Melodies of Kings and Queens

The GMA is, of course, the coronation stage for the biggest stars in Mandopop. Almost all the names you have heard since childhood have left their peak moments on this stage.

First, let's talk about the record holder. Jay Chou has won fifteen Golden Melody Awards, the highest record for a male singer. His night of true legendary status was at the 13th GMA in 2002: with the album _Fantasy_, he personally took home four awards: Best Mandarin Male Mandarin Album, Best Composer (for 〈Love Before the Year 2000〉), and Best Producer. This doesn't even include Fang Wen-shan taking Best Lyricist for 〈William's Castle〉 and Chung Hsing-min taking Best Arranger for 〈Double截棍〉[^6]. One album nearly swept all the technical awards that year. For a young man who had only been active for a few years, that night signaled the beginning of an era.

_A-Mei 〈Listen to the Sea〉. She has been nominated for Best Female Mandarin Singer 14 times—a record in GMA history: winning the title three times, specifically in 2002 with *Real*, 2010 with *Amit*, and 2015 with *Paranoia*._

The story for female singers is just as exciting. A-Mei holds a record that is very hard to break: 14 nominations for Best Female Mandarin Singer[^7]. Her win in 2010 was particularly interesting: she used her "Amit" persona, which carries her Bunun heritage, to compete and win Best Female Mandarin Singer with the album _Amit_, essentially living two different musical lives.

In terms of "most wins," Tanya Chua (蔡健雅) is another legend. She has won Best Female Mandarin Singer four times: _Bi-Sheng Animal_, _Goodbye & Hello_, _Talking About Love_, and _DEPART_—making her the record holder for this category. And, did you know? Tanya Chua is Singaporean. The GMA has never been reserved solely for singers with Taiwanese passports; it is awarded to those who sing the best works in Mandarin. Her four wins are the best proof.

Jolin Tsai holds an achievement even certified by Guinness World Records: the person with the most Song of the Year wins. With 〈Today You Are Marrying Me〉, 〈The Great Artist〉, and 〈Rose Boy〉, she has taken this top prize three times, earning her a Guinness World Record for "Most Golden Melody Awards for Song of the Year." From teen dance tracks to works like 〈Rose Boy〉 that speak for gender issues, the GMA has accompanied her growth.

Some choose to step away at their peak. <a href="/people/江蕙" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Jiang Hui</a> is a legend among Taiwanese-language singers, having won thirteen Golden Melodies in her life, including consecutive Best Female Taiwanese Singer awards from the 11th to the 14th editions. But after winning again with _Red Thread_ in 2003, she made an unexpected decision: announcing she would no longer compete for the Best Female Singer award. Leaving the stage for the next generation—this gesture was as moving as standing on the stage to receive an award.

The stories of kings and queens could go on forever: <a href="/music/五月天" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Mayday</a> going from underground to stadiums, every transformation of <a href="/people/蔡依林" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Jolin Tsai</a>, and <a href="/people/張惠妹" target="_blank" rel="noopener">A-Mei</a>'s nominations spanning over twenty years. The list of winners left by the GMA has become a generational coordinate for youth in the Sinophone world.

## Four Languages, Four Stages

If you only remember the superstars, you will miss the most special thing about the GMA: it allows four different languages to have their own highest honors. This is a design that is almost unique among music awards worldwide.

The story begins with a Taiwanese-language rock song. In 1991, at the 3rd GMA, <a href="/people/林強" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Lin Chiang</a>'s 〈Xiang Qian Zou〉 (Moving Forward) won Song of the Year. Before that, Taiwanese songs were mostly perceived as sorrowful or tragic; 〈Moving Forward〉 used a rock rhythm to tell the story of a rural youth heading north to struggle, completely flipping the vibe of Taiwanese music. The album sold over 400,000 copies and was seen as a representative work of the "New Taiwanese Song Movement"[^8]. (Interestingly, while it won Song of the Year, the Best Producer award that year went to Chen Sheng, <a href="/people/李宗盛" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Jonathan Lee</a>, and others, not Lin Chiang himself.)

Initially, the GMA did not distinguish by language; the first two years only had "Best Male Vocalist" and "Best Female Vocalist." It wasn't until the 3rd GMA in 1991 that it split into "Mandarin" and "Dialect"; by the 14th GMA in 2003, the dialect category was further subdivided into separate awards for Taiwanese, Hakka, and Indigenous languages[^18]. Why subdivide? Because in previous years, the winners of "Best Dialect Vocalist" were almost exclusively Taiwanese singers, while Hakka and Indigenous creators were long kept at the periphery. That year, applications surged to 128 record companies and over 4,800 works, with hundreds coming from Hakka or Indigenous albums: the GMA simply opened a door for each of them.

Opening these doors led to some of the most moving moments in Mandopop.

In 2000, at the 11th GMA, a Bunun police officer from Taitung stood on the highest stage. <a href="/people/陳建年" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Chen Chien-년</a> won Best Mandarin Male Singer with his album _Ocean_: note, it was "Mandarin" male singer, not Indigenous language—he defeated contemporaries like Jacky Cheung, Wang Leehom, David Tao, and Harlem Yu, four superstars at their peak. A policeman who worked at a police station by day, singing songs he wrote about the sea and mountains, beat the hottest stars of the Mandopop industry. His grandfather was the Bunun folk song master Lu Sen-pao; his grandson has carried that musical lineage for thirty years[^9].

If Chen Chien-년 proved that Indigenous creators could beat superstars, then <a href="/people/阿爆" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Abao</a> (A-Neng-neng) at the 31st GMA in 2020 proved that an album sung entirely in an Indigenous language could win the highest honor. Her _kinakaian_ (Mother's Tongue)—an album entirely in Paiwan—swept three awards that night: Best Indigenous Language Album, Song of the Year for 〈Thank You〉, and finally, the top prize of Album of the Year[^10]. An album where you might not understand a single lyric was chosen by judges as the best album in Taiwan that year.

When Abao accepted the Album of the Year award, she said something very "GMA": "If you don't know why this album became the Album of the Year, please listen to it once; if you don't like it, please listen a second time!" She also told the Indigenous children watching through the camera: "Don't waste your talent, and don't rely solely on talent."

> 💡 **Did you know?** There is almost no other music award in the world that sets a highest honor for four different languages within its own borders. You can create the best album in your own language—whether Taiwanese, Hakka, or Indigenous—and be crowned at the same ceremony. For Hakka and Indigenous languages, whose speaker populations are declining, the GMA stage provides more than just honor; it gives these voices a reason to be heard nationwide and a reason for new generations to keep learning them.

This is what makes the Golden Melody Awards something to be proud of. It never pretends that Taiwan has only one voice. Mandarin, Taiwanese, Hakka, and Indigenous languages—four languages, four stages, lit up one after another on the same night.

## Even Its Critics Stand Upon Its Stage

A big enough award must be able to accommodate those who criticize it. One of the most fascinating aspects of the GMA is that several times it has handed the microphone to its sharpest critics—and those moments have become its most memorable images.

In 2007, at the 18th GMA, Lin Sheng-hsiang (林生祥), who comes from <a href="/music/交工樂隊" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Gonggong Band</a>, was nominated for and won two awards—Best Hakka Singer and Best Hakka Album—for his album _Planting Trees_. Then he did something no one in the 18-year history of the GMA had done: he refused the awards on the spot.

He was on stage for only thirty seconds, but those thirty seconds were planned for two years[^11]. He said these were the two awards he "least wanted to receive." His criticism was directed at the system itself, not any individual: he felt that using language to categorize music was putting the cart before the horse. "Why can a Best Producer cross linguistic boundaries, but an album cannot?" He also stated, "For such an important Chinese-language music award to be categorized by language makes it impossible for other countries to enter." He declined both awards and donated the NT$250,000 prize money to the Meinong tree-planting team, two local publications, and the "White Rice Bomber" Yang Ju-men[^12].

Interestingly, the GMA did not change this design just because it was publicly criticized. It heard the criticism and continued the discussion, but the linguistic categorization remained. An award ceremony that can calmly receive the voice of "your award system is problematic" possesses a certain level of confidence.

How language should be categorized remains an ongoing debate. Scholar Kuo Li-hsin (郭力昕) has criticized that categorizing by Mandarin, Taiwanese, Hakka, and Indigenous languages "seems to protect each language, but in reality, it consolidates a cultural system that continues to marginalize minority language groups." He believes that rather than setting "exclusive" awards for the GMA that push Hakka and Indigenous music into niche categories, substantive educational promotion resources should be provided[^13].

> ⚠️ **Controversial Viewpoint:** Linguistic categorization also creates an awkward situation—works that mix multiple languages often have nowhere to go. In 2022, at the 33rd GMA, Kollagi's _MEmento·MORI_ was half Taiwanese and half Amis (with a large amount of Japanese mixed in). As a result, it couldn't fit into any single language category because it didn't meet the requirement of being "primarily performed in one language," so it had to compete for the non-language-specific annual awards. A work that sings in several languages of this land became an outlier in the classification system. The GMA is still searching for a solution to this dilemma.

_Kollagi 〈Wan Qian Hua Rui Ci Mu Bei Ai〉. Mixing Taiwanese, Amis, and Japanese, it couldn't fit into any linguistic track of the GMA, eventually winning Best New Artist at the 33rd GMA in 2022 with *MEmento·MORI*._

These debates are not flaws of the Golden Melody Awards; they are evidence that it is still alive. A thirty-five-year-old award that is still debating with itself about "how language should be divided" and "what to do with multilingual works"—this means it hasn't become stagnant; it is still growing alongside Taiwan's music.

## The GMA Also Records Taiwan's Eras

Since the Golden Melody Awards is Taiwan's largest musical stage, some winning moments are about more than just music; they carry the social emotions of their time. For this part, we will simply record the facts.

In 2015, at the 26th GMA, <a href="/music/滅火器樂團" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Fire EX.</a>'s 〈Island Sky〉 (島嶼天光) won Song of the Year. This song was born during the 2014 Sunflower Student Movement: lead singer Yang Ta-cheng was invited by students from Taipei National University of the Arts to compose it; he spent two days writing the music and one day recording, and on March 27, he led a sing-along at the Legislative Yuan for the first time. Upon receiving the award, Yang said: "After 15 years as a band, our first Golden Melody! ... Dedicated to our beloved Taiwan." He also shouted, "God bless Taiwan"[^15]. This winning moment was skipped in the Tencent broadcast version in China.

In 2024, at the 35th GMA, Panai (巴奈) won Best Taiwanese Album for _Ye Po_ (夜婆). She walked onto the stage wearing a yellow ribbon that read "No one is an outsider," and her speech included this passage: "The Golden Melody Awards have been around for 35 years. Did you know? The Golden Melody Awards was once a stage shared by singers from both sides of the Strait. In 2001, at the 12th GMA, Na Ying became the first Chinese singer to win Best Female Mandarin Singer with _Sad Romance_, also winning Best Lyricist that year. Chang Chen-yüeh (崔健) became the first Chinese national to be named Best Male Mandarin Singer at the 33rd GMA in 2022. However, in the twenty-one years between these two, only these two Chinese nationals have won the top singer titles—the golden age where the GMA and the Chinese market truly overlapped was actually the 2000s, when Taiwanese music dominated Mandopop sales in China, rather than a period of many Chinese singers winning at the GMA.

These moments are placed here without further judgment. The Golden Melody Awards is Taiwan's stage; what Taiwan has experienced, what it cares about, and whom it draws close or distances itself from will naturally leave traces on this stage. By now, you likely have a picture in your mind.

## An Opening on a Python's Back, and Two People Who Waited a Long Time

Thirty-something years might sound like a long time ago, but the Golden Melody Awards is always growing in the present tense. Just last month, on June 27, 2026, the 37th GMA concluded at the Taipei Arena.

That night, A-Lin hosted for the first time. For her opening, she stepped onto a massive python's head to enter the stage, paying homage to <a href="/people/蔡依林" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Jolin Tsai</a>'s classic performances—it was an incredible sight. As a winner herself, the movie theme song she sang, 〈Happiness is Singing〉, won Song of the Year.

The two biggest winners that night were both people who had waited a long time. Jolin Tsai competed for her fifth Best Female Mandarin Singer title with the album _Pleasure_ and finally achieved her wish, winning her second title after nineteen years. She swept Album of the Year, Best Female Mandarin Singer, and Best Recording, with nine total nominations. The Best Male singer's story was a different kind of waiting: Chang Chen-yüeh (張震嶽), after thirty-three years since his debut, won Best Male Mandarin Singer for the first time, also taking home Best Mandarin Album and Best Composer. One waited nineteen years, one waited thirty-three; the Golden Melody Awards is never in a hurry to give awards to the fastest.

The stages for the four languages were all lit that night: the Best Taiwanese Male Singer was <a href="/people/蕭煌奇" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Hsiao Huang-chi</a>, the Best Taiwanese Female Singer was PiA Wu Pei-ya, the Hakka singer was Chen Yi-nuo, and the Indigenous singer was Suming <a href="/music/當代原住民創作歌手" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Shumi'en</a>. Best Band went to Taipei's <a href="/music/落日飛車" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Sunset Rollercoaster</a>, another band that grew from the indie scene and bypassed the mainstream industry to reach the highest stage. As for that Best New Artist who "had her phone taken away" and had to improvise her speech, it was <a href="/music/陳嫺靜" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Chen Hsien-ching</a>, whom we met in the section about the judges.

## Thirty-Something Years: How This Island Listens to Itself

Returning to those few seconds of silence.

At the 2024 Taipei Arena, No Party For Cao Dong achieved legendary status again; a manager accepted on behalf of a deceased drummer, saying "Thank you, Fan-fan." Fan-fan is gone, but her drumming remains in _Ugly Slave_—every time someone presses play, that drum beat sounds once more. A departed drummer is remembered by an entire industry because of the GMA; a three-year-old album is heard anew by a whole generation because of it.

This is perhaps what the Golden Melody Awards does: from that simple first ceremony in 1990 to the 37th edition today, it answers the same question for Taiwan every year—which voices on this island this year are worth remembering.

It remembers Jay Chou's sweep with _Fantasy_, A-Mei's persistence through 14 nominations, a Taitung policeman singing of the sea, an all-Paiwan album, a Hakka singer refusing an award, and a song born from the streets. Mandarin, Taiwanese, Hakka, and Indigenous languages—the million-selling hits and the songs you can't understand a word of, the superstar kings and the quiet songwriters—it keeps all these voices on the same stage.

Over thirty years, the Golden Melody Awards hasn't just been about giving awards. It has been recording, year after year, how this island listens to itself.
