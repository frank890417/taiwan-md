# Gwei Lun-mei: The Low-profile, Resilient Character Actress

Gwei Lun-mei is one of the most internationally recognized faces among Taiwan's new generation of actors. She debuted in 2002 with _Blue Gate Crossing_, gained widespread fame starring in the 2007 film _Secret_ directed by [Jay Chou](/en/people/jay-chou/), and won Best Actress at both the 49th Golden Horse Awards and the 55th Asia Pacific Film Festival in 2012 for _Girlfriend, Boyfriend_.[^1]

## Starting from _Blue Gate Crossing_

Born on December 25, 1983, Gwei Lun-mei graduated from the Department of French at Tamkang University. In 2002, she made her debut in _Blue Gate Crossing_, directed by Yi Chih-yen, playing the introverted and sensitive high school student Meng Ke-rou, a performance that garnered significant attention.[^1]

During the same period, she also appeared in the music video for Cheer Chen's "Minuet," which was also directed by Yi Chih-yen. In the following years, she steadily accumulated experience in art-house cinema through roles in _Subway_ (2003), Cheng Wen-tang's _Passing By_ (2004), and Lin Ching-chieh's _The Most Distant Distance_ (2007).[^1]

## The Breakthrough of _Secret_

In 2007, Gwei Lun-mei starred in Jay Chou's directorial debut, _Secret_, playing the mysterious student Lu Xiao-yu in a romance that transcends time and space. This film, which blends romance, fantasy, and music, earned her widespread critical acclaim for her acting and introduced her to a much larger audience.[^1]

The film was released under the same title in South Korea, where it drew 158,000 viewers and grossed NT$33.4 million. In 2015, it was voted by Korean audiences as the number one film they most wanted to re-watch in theaters, leading to a re-release.[^1]

## Expanding Genres and Collaborations

In 2008, she participated in Tsui Hark's _The Myth_ (also known as _Women are not Bad_) and Chung Mong-hong's _Parking_. In the former, she played a band vocalist and boxer; in the latter, she played the wife of Chen Mo. From 2010 onwards, she appeared in a succession of films including Hsiao Ya-chuan's _The 36th Story_, Hsueh Hsiao-lu's _Ocean Heaven_, and Lin Chao-hsien's _Line Walker_.[^1]

Her 2011 filmography was even broader, including Chang Chih-liang's _Butterfly on my Shoulder_, Tsui Hark and Chang Chih-liang's co-directed _Dragon Gate_, Lin Shu-yu's _Starry Sky_, and the short film anthology _10+10_. Among these, the segment in _10+10_ directed by Takeshi Kaneshiro (Dai Li-ren) served as the opening film for the Golden Horse Awards.[^1]

## 2012 Golden Horse Best Actress

In 2012, Gwei Lun-mei won Best Actress at the 49th Golden Horse Awards and the 55th Asia Pacific Film Festival for her role in Yang Ya-che's _Girlfriend, Boyfriend_, and received a Best Actress nomination at the 7th Asian Film Awards. In the film, she played Lin Mei-pao, portraying the multi-year emotional entanglements between three people alongside Feng Hsiao-yueh and Chang Hsiao-chuan.[^1]

In 2013, she was nominated for Best Actress at the Golden Horse Awards again for the film _Christmas Rose_, directed by Carina Lau (Yang Cai-ni). In 2014, she received another nomination for _Black Coal, Thin Ice_, marking three consecutive years of Golden Horse Best Actress nominations and tying the record held by Michelle Chia (Yang Hui-shan).[^1]

## _Black Coal, Thin Ice_ and Chinese-language Cinema

Directed by Diao Yinan, _Black Coal, Thin Ice_ features Gwei Lun-mei as the laundry worker Wu Zhi-zhen. The film won the Golden Bear at the 64th Berlin International Film Festival and grossed RMB 104.3 million. In 2019, she collaborated with Diao Yinan again in _My Evergreen_ (also known as _Meeting in the Southern Station_), playing Liu Ai-ai.[^1]

In 2017, she appeared in He Wei-ting's _A Beautiful Accident_, and in 2020, she was nominated for Best Actress at the 57th Golden Horse Awards for Chang Yao-sheng's _Legs_, marking her fourth nomination for the Golden Horse Best Actress award.[^1]

## Film Festival Jury and Social Engagement

Gwei Lun-mei has also served as a jury member for various film festivals, including the Asian New Talent Award at the 2011 Shanghai International Film Festival, the 2013 Taipei Film Festival, and as a final round juror for the Golden Horse Awards in 2015 and 2022.[^1]

Regarding social issues, following the 2011 Tohoku earthquake and the Fukushima nuclear disaster in Japan, she joined directors and arts community figures such as Takeshi Kaneshiro (Dai Li-ren), Ke Yi-cheng, and Ono in the anti-nuclear movement against the Fourth Nuclear Power Plant. On January 23, 2016, she also participated in the "123 Homeless People's Rights Year-end Concert" and assisted with cleaning efforts.[^1]
