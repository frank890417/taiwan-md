# Black Cat Teacher: From an Overheating Graphics Card to Twenty Years of Returning Traffic to Readers

> **30-second overview:** Black Cat Teacher (黑貓老師) is a Taiwan self-media creator, history storyteller, and writer. Starting from a Facebook page left behind by an overheating graphics card, he journeyed through cats and memes, WWII history explainers, _History Is War_, Facebook Live, podcasts, and the _Black Cat Creation Report_. He did not rise to prominence by picking the right platform from the start: the page was accidentally retained in 2015, history articles pushed him to more readers in 2016, dry eye syndrome forced him off live streaming in 2020, and in 2024 he distilled years of experience for fellow creators. This is not a straight line from traffic to success, but a creator who, amid repeated shifts in platforms, body, and income, kept making complex material understandable enough that people would stay to listen. [^1] [^2] [^4] [^5] [^10]

In the summer of 2015, Black Cat Teacher was just a player who wanted to learn to play _League of Legends_ like a pro. He set up a Twitch account, installed streaming software, picked a name for his Facebook fan page, and when he went live for the first time, his graphics card overheated and crashed. The game stream never started; that fan page, however, was left behind. [^1]

A few years later, this failed gaming channel became a self-media brand that would share photos of his black cat Ma-liu (馬六), tell history, deconstruct internet traffic, and lay the creator's own struggles bare on the table. Black Cat Teacher's story is not "how one person seized traffic"; it is closer to: **how a person, after every platform shift, decides anew what they want to hand to their audience.**

## A Fan Page That Never Successfully Launched First Learned to Wait

In his own retrospective, Black Cat Teacher says this name was not a carefully designed brand strategy, but an accident from his cram-school work. In late 2015, he entered the cram-school industry as a substitute teacher; colleagues couldn't pronounce his English name Kurt, and seeing his profile photo was a cat, "Black Cat Teacher" was simply called out. [^1]

That job quickly turned the two characters "teacher" into the reality of long hours: teaching, materials, admin support, website and Facebook, including a period of 12-hour workdays. The fan page was originally just an experimental arena he dug up to test marketing tactics without using the company account; only when he quit from overwork did he discover the page had accumulated about 20,000 followers. [^1]

There is an important contrast here: Black Cat Teacher did not have a brand first and then fill it with content; he first turned a failed streaming entry point into a name readers recognized, in the gaps between constantly changing jobs. The brand that came later looks like a plan, but a large part of it was actually something accidentally left behind by time.

> 📝 **Curator's Note**: Some online identities are not designed; they are just left undeleted after a crash.

## Ma-liu Got the Likes First; History Made People Stay

Black Cat Teacher's early internet writing did not start from a "knowledge creator" stance. In interviews, he recalls using "Salmon" (鮭魚) as his ID in junior high, active on Bahamut and the BBS "Stupid Board" (笨版), writing game guides and even serving as a Bahamut KUSO board moderator. Back then, he treated writing as a game, and article heat as an achievement to unlock. [^2]

After the Facebook page was established, content ranged from netizen culture, current events chatter, cram-school daily life, to GIF memes and cat photos. Ma-liu was the most consistent character: Black Cat Teacher once explained this interaction logic with a half-joking observation: "Humans will hit like as soon as they see a cat." [^2]

But the cat did not explain why he could later write long-form. In 2016, a group of high school students dressed as Nazis and Hitler for a school anniversary, sparking media and social criticism; Black Cat Teacher first wrote a commentary, then, prompted by netizens' questions, produced a WWII and Nazi Germany explainer. That article broke 10,000 shares in a day, and the page crossed from about 80,000 likes to over 100,000. [^1]

This turning point deserves a slower look. The original action of "speaking for the students" did not stop at defending one side; it unfolded backward into a history lesson for strangers. Black Cat Teacher acknowledged in the article that he did not support students role-playing Nazis, but also wanted to ask "why these kids would do this," and how society could prevent similar incidents. [^3]

Thus his history storytelling does not compress complex events into a few terms, but starts from a scene witnessed by a Taiwanese person, then leads readers toward more distant times. His 2018 book _History Is War_, published by Yuan-shen Publishing, writes modern wars, international situations, and strategic intrigues as stories; the publisher's introduction positions it as a history reader structured around ten major wars. [^4]

> 📝 **Curator's Note**: Ma-liu opened the door for him; what truly made readers sit down was his willingness to finish explaining the "why."

## From Facebook's High Reach to Where His Eyes Would No Longer Allow

After the page grew, Black Cat Teacher did not stay only in text. Interview data shows he once signed with Facebook for game streaming, later served as social media manager for a listed company and editor-in-chief for other large channels; but long hours of streaming caused dry eye syndrome, and in 2020 he ended Facebook Live and entered recuperation. [^5]

This is not a breezy "successful multi-platform management" segment, but the body hitting pause on platform strategy. When he had to reduce screen time, audio became the medium replacing text. He produced _Black Cat Radio_, moving storytelling and interviews to podcasts, and by inviting other creators to record episodes, rebuilt a way of working less dependent on screens. [^5]

In his later podcast tutorials, Black Cat Teacher breaks production into planning, equipment, recording, hosting, publishing, community, and continuous improvement; he also does not package the show as a dream achievable with passion alone. Equipment, quiet recording environment, RSS feed, platform review, and consistent updates are all creative labor. [^7]

A 2025 interview adds another layer: he once did two seasons, over 50 episodes of podcasts, initially curious "what am I recording this for," later discovering this medium let him talk more deeply with creators; but when full-time creation required calculating ROI, podcasts also paused because they didn't make money. [^6]

This makes the word "pivot" imprecise. Black Cat Teacher did not simply move from Platform A to Platform B once, but continuously adjusted which kind of creation was worth continuing among vision, income, interest, and audience relationship.

## He Cuts One Fish into Articles, Videos, Audio, and Newsletter

Black Cat Teacher's most cited working method later on is "one fish, many meals" (一魚多吃). In a 2026 retrospective article, he describes how one piece of content flows from newsletter article to social post, video, podcast audio, then cut into short videos; the same material need not serve only once, but each medium must still be thought through at the front end. [^8]

He puts it directly: "The key to my survival these years has been one fish many meals, because it lowers cost." [^8] The point of this phrase is not content recycling, but time. For someone long not a full-time creator, if every platform demands a brand new piece of content, creation quickly becomes another form of overtime.

Therefore, he segments platforms: easier-entry content on the main channel, harder creative work on a secondary channel, insect topics on their own spin-off account. This aligns with his early advice to creators: understand your target audience, and don't stuff all content into one brand. [^9]

But multi-platform does not mean copy-paste everything. Black Cat Teacher reminds that before recording, you must think clearly which segments can become shorts, articles, or audio files; the more front-end thinking, the lighter the post-production. This is an engineer's content view: not infinitely converting the same data, but designing reusable structure first.

> 📝 **Curator's Note**: "One fish many meals" sounds like a traffic trick, but is actually more like a labor system that preserves creators' stamina.

## Newsletter Is a Path That Doesn't Need to Please the Algorithm First

In 2024, Black Cat Teacher started writing the _Black Cat Creation Report_. His reason was not "newsletters are trending again," but Facebook's high reach made him suspect who his readers really were. He wrote: "Are my articles published for my readers? Or for the algorithm's AI?" [^10]

This question is also the core of this entire profile. Black Cat Teacher knows the algorithm, knows how to boost engagement, knows how to cross-drive traffic across platforms. But the more familiar the rules, the more he could feel them rewriting his own voice. He worried: if every post only chases high traffic, will what remains in the end be just another content farm? [^10]

So the _Black Cat Creation Report_ is not just a new product, but an attempt to pull agency back. In the inaugural article, he said he wanted to write the amusing, embarrassing, frustrating, and small tricks from years of running a popular page for people who genuinely want to read them. Not every reader needs this content, but those who do can receive it directly. [^10]

Another public introduction in 2024 divides this project into four lines: "Black Cat Tells Stories" for history and mythology, the creator-facing newsletter, "Like-Baiting Notes" (騙讚筆記), and a compilation of traffic, monetization, and tools. When a person remembered for cats and banter begins publishing his methods, entertainment and teaching are no longer two separate roads. [^11]

He also does not write the creator life as an advertisement for freelance freedom. Black Cat Teacher once reminded newcomers to first confirm they would still enjoy doing this even if they earned nothing. In another "Mindset" piece, he writes more directly: influencer work is high-pressure, income unstable, effort and harvest often disproportionate. [^12] A 2021 career article cites him: after 5 years running a Facebook page and 13 years of internet creation, he could finally eat from gigs. [^13]

This candor is among the few places in his content not relying on punchlines. Black Cat Teacher can call traffic "like-baiting," yet does not hide the cost behind jokes: dry eye syndrome, long hours, algorithmic uncertainty, the gap between fans and income — all are in creation's ledger.

## How a Person Avoids Being Trapped by Their Own Name

Black Cat Teacher's public identities have many layers: English teacher, cram-school worker, community manager, Facebook streamer, history storyteller, podcast host, beetle and aquarium content creator, writer, and self-media coach. These titles are not a pretty résumé, but traces left each time platform and life conditions changed.

If you only see the "influencer" result, you miss the most interesting part: what he truly stabilized was not the platform, but turning strange material into stories someone would stay to hear. Not a fixed form, but the willingness to relearn when the form fails. Flipped Education (翻轉教育) cites his view, treating "curiosity, learning new things anytime" as a more important creator ability than any single skill. [^14]

In 2025, he spoke of returning to Pingtung and truly devoting himself to his own channels. The same interview records him dividing creators into "artist type" and "merchant type," but ultimately placing physical and mental health and creative joy before income. [^6] This is not denying commerce, but acknowledging creation has two ledgers: one written in words, the other recorded in the body.

> 📝 **Curator's Note**: What is most worth remembering about Black Cat Teacher is not how many platforms he knows, but that he knows every platform may demand you surrender a piece of yourself.

That overheating graphics card in 2015 originally proved only one thing: this launch failed. But the fan page that failure left behind was first filled by a cat, then pushed toward strangers by a history article, and finally brought back by newsletter into the hands of people willing to truly finish reading.

This road has no fixed endpoint, nor is it as neat as "from influencer to writer." More accurately: Black Cat Teacher has kept switching media, yet repeatedly guarded the same question — **when content can be cut into more formats and distributed to more platforms, is there still a kind of creation meant to let a certain person truly understand, listen through, and choose to stay?**
