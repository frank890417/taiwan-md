# Huang Shan-liao: No One Watched the World Champion's Clothes, So He Started Selling "One-Sentence Comfort"

In June 2026, an old poster went viral on Threads.

The photo shows a 2014 London runway, where a young Taiwanese man walked out in six outfits under the theme "Kinmen 1969": military-style tailoring, bullet holes, camouflage netting fringe, and distressed fabrics printed with patterns from the _Kinmen Daily_ and _Zhengqi Zhonghua Bao_. That year, he won the International Grand Prize at Graduate Fashion Week in London. [^1]

The comment section below was solving the same math problem: Is this world-champion designer on the poster the same person as the bestselling author being mocked by 600,000 people under a single video this week for being "shallow" and "chicken soup"?

Yes. His name is Huang Shan-liao. The same person, two different products: one collection telling battlefield history through "Kinmen 1969"—a topic no media outlet had ever reported on—and a line of healing short sentences, each no longer than 28 characters, which have sold over 300,000 copies. He once defined those short sentences himself: they are for people who "need one-sentence comfort, much like flipping through a fortune-telling book." [^21] Since no one was looking at that collection of clothes, he switched to selling "one-sentence comfort," and thus became Taiwan's bestselling—and most mocked—author.

> **30-Second Overview:** Huang Shan-liao was born in Kinmen in 1992 into a family that ran a small eatery. In 2014, he won the International Grand Prize at Graduate Fashion Week in London with "Kinmen 1969," marking one of the years Shiffer University's Department of Fashion Design won awards for three consecutive years. However, upon returning to Taiwan to find work, he sent out over a hundred resumes and received zero interviews; an HR representative from one company could not find any news reports of his award online and suspected his resume was forged. This led to his epiphany that "the world is not about who has ability, but about who has the ability to speak." In a fit of indignation, he started his own media outlet, _One Shirt_ (2017–2021), to let the nameless "be seen," before transitioning into a healing-style author who continuously dominated the Eslite bestseller lists. In June 2026, a public critique video by YouTuber Domidoro surpassed 600,000 views in a single day, thrusting him into the center of the storm regarding "the greatest scam of Taiwan's bestselling author."

## The Unreported World Champion

First, let's clarify the forgotten fact: he really was a world champion.

Huang Shan-liao was born in Kinmen on May 3, 1992; his father is Huang Si-tsu and his mother is Chao Hui-ying, and his family runs a small eatery on the island. His father gave him the name "Shan-liao." An interview in the _Kinmen Daily_ recorded the meaning behind his father's choice: raw jade material that has not been weathered—pure, clean, fresh, and slightly angular. [^2] Raw material, not a finished product; angular, not yet smoothed down. Sometimes, a person's later story is hidden within their name from the very beginning.

He entered the Department of Fashion Design at Shih Chien University through an island recommendation system, not out of passion. He put it bluntly: "My ambition was never in fashion design; I entered fashion design simply because there was a system called 'offshore island recommendation' when I was in high school." [^3] A child from an outlying island with no particular passion for clothing produced a graduation collection four years later that had the editor-in-chief of Italy's _Vogue_ whispering in his ear.

"Kinmen 1969" consisted of six outfits telling the story of his hometown's battlefield memories: the strength of women and men, wartime women, and the military spirit. He made extensive use of military totems, bullet holes, combat boots, and camouflage netting, printing old newspaper images from the _Kinmen Daily_ and _Zhengqi Zhonghua Bao_ onto fabric with a faux-parchment texture, accented with black iron metal details. [^4] In the _Kinmen Daily_, he explained the design concept of this collection: "Memories are gentle; history is resilient. In terms of pattern and silhouette, I interpret military elements—bunkers, uniforms, stability, and strength." [^2] He also added a more complete thought: "The principle I follow is that every piece is an individual personality, a character; they tell the stories of that era while defending a cultural history that is gradually being destroyed by civilization." [^2]

Remember this, because it is the key to this entire article: **the pinnacle of his craftsmanship was about the war memories of his hometown.** For a man who was mocked as having "no substance" (_mei liao_), the moment he truly showed his substance was when he spoke for the history of a small island.

At the moment of winning, however, he was not as excited as one might imagine. He wrote in his book: "It turns out that becoming a world champion is not as moving as I imagined; there was no profound emotion from finally achieving a goal, no tears of being moved, and it did not fill me with hope or longing for the future. What I felt inside was nothing but bewilderment and dread regarding what lay ahead." [^5] According to his book, the presenter was _Vogue Italia_ editor-in-chief and GFW judge Franca Sozzani, who leaned close to his ear and said: "You are a very talented designer; I look forward to you coming to work in Europe." [^6]

This podium is no small feat. London Graduate Fashion Week was founded in the UK in 1991; its first Gold Award winner was Christopher Bailey, who later headed Burberry, and its alumni include Stella McCartney. Chinese-language media often refers to this top prize directly as a "world champion." [^1] The problem is: almost no one in Taiwan knows about this world champion.

## That Remark from HR

After returning to Taiwan, for a full two years, Huang Shan-liao sent out over a hundred resumes without receiving a single interview request. [^7]

What truly pinned him down was a response from an HR representative at a certain company. _Mirror Media_ recorded that dialogue verbatim: the HR person told him that while the manager had reviewed his resume and thought he was excellent, "he said his manager searched my name online but couldn't find much coverage in mainstream media, so he suspected my resume might be fraudulent." [^7] In other words: a world champion was treated as a fraud simply because there was no media coverage of the achievement.

This is an irreplaceable moment in Huang Shan-liao's life; it is where everything that followed grew from. His realization was also preserved verbatim: "It turns out this world isn't about who has ability, but about who has the ability to speak!" as well as "I have actually been constantly trying to compensate for that past deficiency of not being seen!" [^7] In another interview with _Kinmen Daily_, he expressed it differently: "At that time, I felt so frustrated—that my strength required media endorsement just to be recognized," followed by "I tripped in this place, so I stood up from where I fell." [^8]

To understand this wound fairly, one must zoom out a bit: behind it lies a structural issue within an entire industry, not just his personal bad luck. The starting salary for new fashion designers in Taiwan ranges from approximately NT$26,000 to NT$35,000; it takes about ten years to rise to the position of Design Director, and overtime is the norm with high turnover rates. Even the industry's administrative classification reveals the problem: in the UK and France, the fashion industry falls under the Ministry of Culture, whereas in Taiwan, the fashion industry is managed by the Ministry of Economic Affairs—a mindset that treats clothing as OEM (Original Equipment Manufacturing) rather than culture. [^9] Huang Shan-liao described this despair himself: "Many young guys and girls coming out now want to do very creative things, but those things, honestly, have no commercial value in Taiwan." This was followed by an even heavier remark: "Look at someone like me, who has already come back with awards, and even I am struggling this much. What about those who haven't won any?" [^3]

The common cynical narrative is that he discovered writing "chicken soup" (inspirational essays) was more profitable than making clothes, so he defected. While this narrative is convenient, it reverses the cause and effect. He was first taught a cruel lesson by the industry, and only later saw that there was money to be made: **to not be seen is to not exist.** If even a world champion can be deemed fake because no reports were found, how can an unknown person prove their existence? Everything he has done since then has been an answer to that question.

## Turning "Being Seen" into a Business

In 2017, at the age of 25, Huang Shan-liao borrowed NT$2 million and quit a design job he had held for only 11 months to found 《One Shirt》. [^10]

It was a media platform dedicated to making the unknown "be seen." Its Facebook page was positioned as "the urban daily scenery you paint," focusing on professional stories of "resigning—starting a business—pursuing dreams": he took the kind of life that had once trapped him and filmed it for others. In one year, he produced 161 character interviews, reaching nearly a million views per week, with approximately 13.38 million cumulative views on YouTube. At its peak, the team consisted of about 20 people, reaching roughly 20 million Chinese-speaking people. [^11] A person who had once faced job rejection because they weren't being reported on turned around to become the one reporting on others. This reversal itself is the most beautiful counterpoint in his entire story.

But as he continued, he found himself hollowed out. He stepped down in November 2020, and in June 2021, he announced the closure of 《One Shirt》. [^11] The reason he gave for shutting it down was not commercial failure, but that he disliked the distorted version of himself: "After taking on too much responsibility, I became impatient with people. I even grew to hate that version of Huang Shan-liao." [^12] There was also a more poignant sentiment: "《One Shirt》 is a baby I gave birth to myself; letting go is incredibly difficult. But it really is time to leave; I cannot hesitate any longer, and I cannot continue to sacrifice anyone else just because I am reluctant to let go." [^13]

Later, in a 2024 interview in Malaysia, he described that sense of alienation even more sharply: he said he was "not serving creativity, but acting like an advertising agency." [^14] This statement was a reflection from hindsight rather than something said at the moment he closed shop in 2021, but it precisely articulates a contradiction: he created media so that people could "be seen," but when "being seen" becomes a business, what is that business actually feeding—creativity, or traffic itself? Through his own experience, he hit the ceiling of the attention economy and chose to step down.

## The Bestseller Method of 28 Characters Per Line

After leaving the media industry, Huang Shan-liao set a goal for himself: to complete ten novels within five years. [^15]

He has been writing full-time since 2021. His first book, _Drifting Youth_ (2019), was actually released earlier, but his royalties for the first year were only NT$140,000; he himself remarked that it was precisely because of this that "I ended up staying at that company for another two years." [^16] His true breakthrough came after 2021 with titles such as _Live Well, Meet Slowly_, _Goodbye Properly, Do Not Regret the Encounter_, _That Girl Said to Me_, _The Rest of My Life is You, It's Okay if It's Late_, _Loneliness in the World, Yet Meeting You Like an Old Friend_, _A Small Heart is Enough to Hold onto Likable Things_, _Making Days Slowly Better_, and _It Is Enough to Have Once Held On_. Almost one to two books were released every year, all published by San-Cai Culture. They are all healing-style prose and novels characterized by gentle short sentences and significant use of white space. [^15]

The numbers are staggering. According to verified official annual rankings from Eslite: in 2021, _Live Well_ took the No. 1 spot in the Psychology & Self-Help category and ranked third overall for the year; in 2022 and 2023, he won the overall annual bestseller title for Eslite's unclassified category for two consecutive years (_Goodbye Properly_ and _The Rest of My Life is You_); in 2024 and 2025, he ranked second for the year, losing respectively to Tsai Kang-yung's _Would You Willingly_ and the _Morris Chang Autobiography_. [^17] Regarding sales, he stated in 2023 that his six books had accumulated over 300,000 copies; by 2025, media reported this figure had exceeded 400,000. For _A Small Heart_, the pre-order period sold 3,000 copies in just 6 minutes, surpassing NT$1 million in sales, with an initial print run of 30,000; for _Loneliness in the World_, the initial print run of 30,000 plus 5,000 signed copies was completely sold out. [^18][^19]

Most crucially is how he views his own work. In a 2025 interview with _Ming Weekly_, he spoke about his bestseller methodology without any concealment: "I have always felt that my competitors are not other authors, but TikTok, Facebook, and Threads." "The things I write can be read in a single glance while scrolling." "If a line is more than 26 to 28 characters, their eyes might not be able to digest it." [^20] In a 2023 interview with the _China Times_ Supplement, he stated his goal even more plainly: "My goal is to make people who do not usually read willing to finish this book." He wants people to treat his books "like flipping through a fortune-telling book, needing just one sentence of comfort." [^21]

> **📝 Curator's Note**
> It is easy to read this methodology as "he is admitting to being lazy." But it is more interesting to look at two products side-by-side: on the left is that cabinet from _Kinmen 1969_, printed with old newspapers, bullet holes, and camouflage netting, telling the war memories of an island, which no media outlet covered; on the right are sentences of 26 to 28 characters per line, where white space takes up half a page, selling over 300,000 copies. A person whose existence was denied ten years ago because "no one reported it" has now turned "how to be caught by someone scrolling through a phone within three seconds" into a science calculated down to the character count. He has not abandoned his craft; he has simply traded fabric for attention. He learned the lesson HR taught him that day all too well.

He is even honest enough to admit he does not come from an "artistic youth" (_wenqing_) background: "I used to write diaries on Wዳm (Wuming) Small Station, but I didn't read books; it wasn't until after finishing _Drifting Youth_ in 2019 that I developed the habit of reading." [^22] This sentence holds true for two different interpretations: critics will say, "No wonder the writing is shallow," while sympathizers will say, "A person who didn't read managed to climb to the top through self-study." The same sentence—it depends on which side you stand.

## A Shallow Pond, or a Mirror?

On June 5, 2026, YouTuber Domidolo uploaded a video titled 〈The Great Scam of Taiwan's Best-Selling Author〉, which surpassed 600,000 views in one day and approximately 700,000 within three days. [^23]

The criticism was harsh. Domidolo stated that although he "clearly read six books, the actual experience felt like only two," mocking the excessive use of white space: "Has your book ever been touched by Yubaba? The words are cut by more than half." He called his books a "textual version of Blue Enchantress" and, using his literary expertise, gave reviews such as "can only score 0 points" and "an unqualified writer, but a master of mass psychological manipulation." [^24]

However, what should be remembered most from this video is not those harsh words, but a metaphor: "Huang Shan-liao is not a deep well, but a very shallow pond; you see the bottom with one glance, but you can see your own reflection on the water's surface." [^23]

The reason this sentence is powerful is that it does not stop at mere insults. It precisely identifies Huang Shan-liao's mechanism of resonance: his books are mirrors, not wells. In those short sentences and white spaces, readers see themselves.

Then comes the paradox. Domidolo, who criticized Huang Shan-liao all the way to the bottom, and the hundreds of thousands of readers who have propelled him to the top of the charts, are actually describing **the same mechanism**. So are those defending him. On Threads, an author @authorlinyt spoke on his behalf, saying: "Huang Shan-liao's simple writing style is actually very close to the reading market of the last ten years; just as short videos and short dramas have risen, people's rhythm for consuming content has become thinner and faster." [^25] As for Huang Shan-liao himself, he admitted long ago: "The things I write can be finished in a single glance while scrolling." [^20]

All three parties agree on the essence of this matter: it is easy to read, like a mirror, designed to catch people scrolling through their phones in an era of attention competition. Domidolo calls it a "shallow pond," defenders call it "market structure," and Huang Shan-liao himself calls it "finished in one glance." **The three are describing the same mirror; they just happen to have opposite evaluations of it.**

> **📝 Curator's Note**
> This is what makes the Huang Shan-liao case truly interesting. Those who criticize him feel that "seeing oneself on the water's surface" is evidence of superficiality; those who love him feel that "seeing oneself on the water's surface" is precisely the moment of being understood and connected. While it seems they are arguing about Huang Shan-liao, they are actually arguing about a much larger issue: once an island's reading rhythm has been rewritten by short videos, does "reflecting oneself" still count as a form of value? Huang Shan-liao is simply the person standing right in the middle of that crack.

Facing this public trial, Huang Shan-liao's response was so quiet it was almost jarring. On June 6, he indirectly shared a sentence from his new book via an Instagram Story: "Many times, it is not that you are not good enough, but that the environment you are in was never healthy to begin with." [^26] On the morning of June 7, he released an official statement: "I understand that every reader has different favorite works and expects different reading experiences. This year marks my fifth year of full-time writing; my direction has always been to encourage more people who do not originally read to enter bookstores or even pick up a book. I still have much to improve upon, and I will continue to learn and grow. Thank you to all sectors for your suggestions, and thank you once again to all readers for your support throughout the journey." [^27]

A person who has won a world championship, when given a score of 0, says he "will continue to learn."

## Three Consecutive Wins, Three Different Fates

To understand why Huang Shan-liao is being criticized, the best method is to place him back into the world he left behind and view him alongside those who stayed.

Shih Chien University's Department of Fashion Design once achieved international top prizes at the London Graduate Fashion Week for three consecutive years: Jiang Yi-xun in 2013, Huang Shan-liao in 2014, and Chou Yun-ting in 2015. [^28] The same school, the same stage, but three completely different paths.

Jiang Yi-xun (Angus Chiang) stayed. His 2013 award-winning work, "Sailing to the Moon," stitched together elements of Taiwanese temple fairs with spacesuits (Western journalists even misread it as "Mexican spacesuits," a form of cross-cultural misinterpretation). [^29] Later, he founded his namesake brand ANGUS CHIANG in 2015 and became the first Taiwanese designer to be shortlisted as an LVMH Prize semi-finalist in 2017, appearing at Paris Fashion Week. [^30] He earned the respect of the international fashion industry and a niche following. Among the general public in Taiwan, far fewer people know the name Jiang Yi-xun than those who know Huang Shan-liao.

What this table reveals is less about the personality differences of three individuals and more like a question posed to Taiwan: **Does this island reward "doing things well," or "being seen by many"?** Jiang Yi-xun brought his work to international standards, yet the Taiwanese public hardly recognizes him. Huang Shan-liao made himself seen by as many people as possible, and the Taiwanese public scolds him every day. One is neglected; the other is trampled upon. The one who did things well receives no applause; the one seen by many is treated as a joke. These two classmates, both having taken their respective paths to the extreme, happen to demonstrate for Taiwan: on these two roads, there may be no applause at all.

## Beyond the Chicken Soup: Controversies That Must Be Honestly Recorded

Treating Huang Shan-liao as a mere victim is another form of laziness. His controversies are not as simple as "low literary merit"; there are several matters that must be honestly laid bare.

**The NT$250,000 Monthly Salary Plagiarism Controversy (October 2020).** His essay 〈People earning NT$250,000 and NT$30,000 have the same worries〉 was accused of having six similarities with a PTT post from August (e.g., "ordering NT$500 worth of Uber Eats at once," "taking a taxi when going out," "eating half of a large portion of fries").[^31] His rebuttal was: "I did not plagiarize!", "That is 'citation'", supplemented by "Perhaps I didn't write it clearly enough, that is my fault, I am stupid," and "It still hurts; I'm not made of iron."[^32] Influencer Chen Yi questioned whether the "neighbor" earning NT$250,000 a month in the article was entirely fictional. He responded: "Ten years of struggle cannot be overturned by pieced-together information from the internet."[^33] This controversy remains unresolved to this day, but the blurred line between "citation" and "plagiarism" was one of the starting points for the cracks in his credibility.

**The Domestic Violence Remarks Controversy (June 2024, Malaysia).** This matter must be handled with extreme care. In edited clips circulating online, his words were heard as: "If there is someone who is always domesticating violence against you, then if you can always withstand his violence, he is the right partner for you."[^34] After facing public backlash, he issued a public apology, stating verbatim: "Regarding domestic violence, I am one hundred percent opposed to it; I do not support anyone using violence to solve problems in a relationship. My expressive abilities are poor and my meaning was incomplete; domestic violence is wrong, and this is the stance I hold." He also stated that the clip had been edited.[^35]

Recording only his apology would not achieve balance for this incident. The positions of experts must be placed here with equal clarity: Lan Yi-feng of the Taiwan Association of Psychologists said, "Violence absolutely cannot be tolerated, nor should it be rationalized"; Chang Hsiu-yuan, Director of the Department of Protection and Services under the Ministry of Health and Welfare, said, "Violence is by no means a way to solve problems."[^36] Commentators also pointed out deeper issues; a headline from an article on The Reporter was 〈Huang Shan-liao's "Support for Domestic Violence Theory" Sparks Backlash: Great Problems with Expressive Ability Yet Becomes a Bestselling Author—You and I Both Gave Him a Push〉.[^37] Regardless of whether that segment was edited, placing the concept of "whether one can withstand domestic violence" into a sentence about "whether one is suitable to be together" is inherently dangerous. Violence allows for no compromise, and this point should not be diluted by anyone's apology or explanation.

There is a string of other controversies: In March 2021, he said, "People who are truly depressed to the bottom will not say they are depressed; they will simply disappear from the world," which was criticized as being fake positive energy;[^38] in August 2023, his "chicken soup" text posted on NTU campus bulletin boards was removed in less than six hours, with netizens sarcastically noting, "Ran out of material in just six hours."[^39] However, some have spoken up for him: in 2022, YouTuber Gua Ji criticized his books as being "of no help to society," to which he responded magnanimously: "Diverse ideas are important."[^40] Writer Wu Tan-ju also eased the tension for him, saying: If someone sees your book and doesn't like it, it means even those who don't like your books have seen them; you should be happy. [^40]

> **⚠️ A Matter That Must Be Clarified**
> In March 2026, there was an embezzlement case involving "Daily Scenery Co., Ltd." (日常風景有限公司), in which Huang Shan-liao was the **victim**, not the defendant. Chiu Chih-chien, a shareholder of his company, was indicted for forging documents and embezzling NT$5.74 million.[^41] During the public trial, some people online conflated this incident with him; it must be stated clearly here: in this case, he was the one who was embezzled from.

## I Actually Envy Those Who Are Already Completely Cancelled

Returning to that old poster going viral on Threads.

The youth walking the London runway in "Kinmen 1969" in 2014, and the bestselling author being mocked by 600,000 people this week in 2026 for having "no substance," are the same person. From a snack shop in Kinmen to the runways of London, from the top of Eslite's bestseller lists to a meme under a public condemnation video, this is a single line that has always wanted to do just one thing: **to be seen.** He used fabric to speak of his hometown's wars, used lenses to make the nameless appear, and used short sentences of 28 characters per line to catch those scrolling through their phones. The medium keeps changing, but that desire—which grew from HR's remark "no reports found"—has never changed.

He is a student personally taught by Taiwan. Taiwan told him that in this world, it is not about ability, but the knack for speaking; he believed it, practiced that knack until he was number one in the country, and then Taiwan turned around to mock him for having no real substance. He is the most complete living proof of this lesson.

In a 2025 interview with _Business Weekly_, he said something that now reads like a prophecy. He said: "I actually envy those public figures who are already completely cancelled, because those who are completely blacklisted have no one left to hold them hostage. Because when you look at someone who is fully 'black,' no matter how much more mud is thrown at them, they remain black." [^42]

A former world champion envies those who are already completely cancelled and no longer need to care about anyone's gaze. A man who practiced writing bestsellers with 28 characters per line finds that his deepest desire is actually the freedom of not being seen.

That cabinet of "Kinmen 1969" was seen by no one, so it vanished; that phrase "comfort in a single sentence" was bought by everyone, so he became a national meme. So, before you mock "that hollow chicken soup writer" next time, perhaps you should first look at the surface of that pond and ask yourself: does the line of text you find shallow reflect his shallowness, or does it reflect an island that only deigns to applaud what can be "read in a glance," but refuses to applaud what is actually "made"? Those who curse him, those who repost him, and those who defend him are all looking into the same mirror.
