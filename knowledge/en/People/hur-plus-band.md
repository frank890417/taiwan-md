> **30-second overview:** HUR+ (formerly HUR), the six-member runner-up "Storm Spades" from the 2020 survival show _DD52_, debuted under producer Felipe.Z, who founded AOA Entertainment Lab. They lost the finale by 2.5 points, but the winning group G.O.F saw their buzz collapse within a year, while fellow debutees PINK FUN faced member departures. Six years on, HUR+ is the only one of the three debut groups still releasing albums. Three albums (_REVELATION_, _Crimzon_, _9OF9 ARCANA_), a crowdfunding drive that raised NT$1.5 million in ten minutes for Korean promotions, and members spanning Mongolia, the UK, Indonesia, and Vietnam. As Felipe.Z put it: "If we use the same formula as Korea, how could Taiwan possibly win?"

On the evening of 4 September 2020, the _DD52_ finale.

Blazing Hearts: 57.9 points. Storm Spades: 55.4 points[^1].

A 2.5-point gap. The winners were Blazing Hearts, debuting as "G.O.F" (Girls On Fire). Storm Spades were the runners-up; six people left the show's stage.

In survival-show logic, runners-up usually go like this: viewers feel a pang of regret when voting, then forget their names a few months later. But these six met a producer who refused to play by the rules.

---

## A Producer's NT$50 Million

Felipe.Z had his eye on Storm Spades before _DD52_ even aired. After the show, he and Liang Ting-hao founded AOA Entertainment Lab, signed the six, and gave them a new name: **HUR**[^2].

HUR, from Hurricane (the English abbreviation of Storm Spades), also a homophone for Her, symbolising girl power[^2].

6 November 2020: HUR officially debuted. Less than two months after the _DD52_ finale.

Felipe.Z later broke down the numbers for a reporter:

> **✦** "Everyone thought we were making a girl group. Actually, we're running a business!"[^3]

He poured **NT$50 million** into HUR. The original estimate was NT$30–40 million. Once operations started, they discovered that to reach K-pop quality standards (MV production, choreography training, image packaging, overseas marketing), the real figure was **NT$300–400 million**[^3].

Between NT$50 million and NT$400 million lies the real distance between the Taiwanese and Korean idol industries.

## Six People

At debut, HUR was a six-member group:

| Stage name                            | Birth name    | Origin                | Position                   |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------- | --------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Cindy                                 | Li Shan-zhen  | Pingtung              | Leader, sub-vocal, visual  |
| Jasmine                               | Pei Xie       | Taipei                | Main vocal, center         |
| C.Holly                               | Wang Can-yun  | UK                    | Main rapper, main dancer   |
| **[Lian Ying](/en/people/lien-ying)** | **Lian Ying** | **Taichung**          | **Main dancer, sub-vocal** |
| Sizi                                  | Balen Yue     | Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia | Main vocal                 |
| Jennifer                              | Xi Zi-qi      | New Taipei            | Sub-vocal, sub-dancer      |

Two of the six were not Taiwanese: C.Holly from the UK, Sizi from Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia. This was deliberate on Felipe.Z's part: seeding "international DNA" into the lineup[^2].

Leader Li Shan-zhen's story deserves its own mention. Scouted for web shows and commercials in university, her family opposed an entertainment career. She earned a flight attendant licence, worked as cabin crew while doing gigs on the side, and only resigned from the airline in 2023 to focus fully on _NEXT GIRLZ_[^11]. In the Taiwanese idol industry's pay structure, going from flight attendant to idol leader is essentially economic suicide. She did it anyway.

5 November 2021: HUR released their first album, _REVELATION_[^2]. The opening track "Pain Killer" sparked intense discussion; the sound fused an R&B base with aggressive indie electronic, a total departure from the sweet mainstream route of other Mandarin girl groups at the time[^4].

> **📝 Curator's note**
> Among _DD52_'s four teams, Storm Spades were known for intense stage presence, contrasting with winners Blazing Hearts' "sweet, energetic" vibe[^1]. Post-debut, Felipe.Z pushed that contrast to the extreme: the goal was a group **deliberately unlike any K-pop group**.

## "The More Different, the Better"

If HUR+'s strategy had to be summed in one sentence, it would be this line from Felipe.Z:

> **✦** "Actually, the more different we are, the better. If we use the same formula as Korea, how could Taiwan possibly win?"[^3]

The numbers back this up. Korea's Big Three agencies post annual revenues in the hundreds of billions of won; pre-debut training for a new group routinely runs into billions of won. Taiwan lacks that capital scale; trying to match K-pop production specs head-on is a dead end.

Felipe.Z's move was differentiation in musical style. HUR+'s arrangements lean heavily on R&B and indie electronic elements, lyrics have a high English ratio, visuals favour dark tones and street aesthetics. Unlike the frequent "concept switches" of K-pop girl groups, HUR+'s style hasn't pivoted dramatically from debut to now—they've consistently been that aggressive, hard-hitting group[^4].

In 2023, they competed on _NEXT GIRLZ_ as the nine-member limited unit "Crimzon". Judges criticised them for being "too aggressive". Felipe.Z's social-media reply was six characters:

> **✦** "Stone-age thinking."[^5]

## From Six to Nine

_NEXT GIRLZ_ gave HUR two things: exposure, and the catalyst to expand.

After the show ended in 2023, three new members officially joined, and HUR became HUR+[^2]:

| Stage name | Birth name    | Origin                         | Strategic positioning                                 |
| ---------- | ------------- | ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Shannon    | Tong Kai-ling | Taipei (Taiwanese-American)    | Model background, bridges into high-end fashion       |
| Grace      | Lin Shi-ya    | Jakarta, Indonesia             | Entry point into the Southeast Asian market           |
| Lan Lan    | Liu Xiang-lan | Taoyuan (Taiwanese-Vietnamese) | Youngest (born 2008), Southeast Asian & youth markets |

Lan Lan's addition is especially notable. She "only started learning dance in 2022"; competing was an accident—she was recommended for auditions while taking dance classes[^11]. Yet at 15, _Mirror Media_ called her the "team ace". Her own take was light: "Since I'm young and have time, why not give it a shot?"[^11]

Look at that table and one thing stands out: **every new member brought a market entry point**[^2]. Shannon's mixed heritage and modelling point toward the Western premium market. Grace from Indonesia (Southeast Asia's largest economy). Lan Lan's Taiwanese-Vietnamese background plus her 2008 birth year point toward Southeast Asia and Gen Z.

Add original members C.Holly (UK) and Sizi (Mongolia), and HUR+'s nine-person nationality map covers cultural links to five countries: Taiwan, UK, Mongolia, Indonesia, Vietnam. Diversity and market expansion aren't mutually exclusive here. Like AKB48's "there's always one you'll like" turning differentiation into a business model, HUR+'s member map simultaneously showcases cultural diversity and serves as an **entry-point design** where every market has someone who resonates.

> **✦** "We're not just running a girl group. We're pushing an industry forward."[^3]

3 May 2024: second album _Crimzon_ released[^2]. 9 January 2026: third album _9OF9 ARCANA_ released[^2].

## Ten Minutes, NT$1.5 Million

In 2023, HUR+ launched the "HUR+ Asia Debut Support Project" on the WaBay crowdfunding platform, aiming to fund Korean promotions[^6].

**Ten minutes after launch, they broke NT$1.5 million**[^6].

The final target was NT$8 million, covering all costs for Korean promotions: flights, accommodation, music-show registration, MV shooting, local marketing[^7].

The amount itself isn't staggering. The **speed** is. Ten minutes for NT$1.5 million means HUR+'s core fandom threw money down the instant the campaign opened. An already emotionally invested core fanbase paid up in second one.

> **📝 Curator's note**
> Crowdfunding to go promote in Korea. That fact alone deserves pause. In Korea, the agency pays for music-show appearances as a baseline. In Taiwan, fans pay out of pocket to send the group to Korea. This is the real ecology of the Taiwanese idol industry right now: where official resources fall short, the fan community fills the gap.

## Evidence on Stage

HUR+ has physical stages, and venue scale has kept growing:

- Feb 2023: _ON MY WAY_ first concert at Huashan 1914 Creative Park[^2]
- Apr 2024: Legacy Taipei concert[^2]
- 2025: SUPER SLIPPA 14 (Kaohsiung Arena), IDOL KINGDOM Taipei[^2]
- Apr 2025: "GODDESS" digital single fan-meet, hosted by So-net's fan platform Dolfan, surprise performance of unreleased track "Walk By The River"[^12]
- **27 Feb 2026, Zepp New Taipei: _9 of 9: ARCANA CONCERT_**, largest headliner show in five years since debut[^13]
- **25–26 Apr 2026, two shows in Bangkok**, with Perfume Prod., first Southeast Asian stage[^14]

From Huashan to Legacy to Zepp to Bangkok. Six years of venue upgrades—that's a survival certificate for a Taiwanese girl group.

And the Zepp show left a moment that shouldn't be overlooked. Three days before the concert (24 Feb), C.Holly was in a car accident: left foot injured and bleeding, hand fractured[^13]. Any normal person would cancel. She didn't. She went on with a hand cast, in a wheelchair, sang "Three No-Marries" with dancer assistance, and at one point stood up to swing her waist and hips[^13].

> **💡 Did you know?**
> The clip of C.Holly performing in a cast drew reactions like: "How can funny and moving coexist like this?" and "Her standing up at the end was just insane"[^13]. "Professionalism" doesn't cover it. This is something a group cultivated over six years—they go on because they know the people below have waited a long time.

## The Thunders

HUR+'s fans are called **Thunder** (雷雷)[^12]. Slogan: "Join Thunder, never late."

In the Taiwanese idol context, HUR+'s fandom has an anomalous trait: **its core is in Hong Kong**.

HUR+ Hong Kong Fan Club (@hurhkfc) has **11k followers, 269 posts**[^15]. In the Taiwanese girl-group ecosystem, that's highly unusual. A Taiwanese idol group's most active fan organisation is based overseas. Hong Kong Thunders film covers, make fan art, and **fly to Taiwan** whenever there's a major show. At the 2025 "GODDESS" fan-meet, Hong Kong fans showed up; members were surprised and delighted: "Long time no see!"[^12]

The fan-meet itself illustrates the community's vibe: one fan brought a white Totoro plush onstage for a group photo—all nine couldn't put it down; someone wore a self-made T-shirt printed with Xi Zi-qi in a bikini, triggering laughter onstage and off[^12]. Thunders crowdfunded their Korea trip, bought CheerSPOT ads on power-bank stations across Taiwan, Japan, and Thailand[^16], turning the idol-fan relationship from one-way consumption into two-way co-creation.

> **📝 Curator's note**
> K-pop's support culture is highly systematised: birthday billboards, coffee cup sleeves, subway ads—every item has an SOP. HUR+'s Thunders learned that vocabulary, but speak it with a Taiwanese accent: Dadaocheng's small stages, ChargeSPOT digital signage on power banks[^16], a real Totoro. The "localised translation" of Taiwanese fan culture is happening right here.

## Why the Runner-Up Outlived the Winner

Back to the opening question: _DD52_ finale, Blazing Hearts 57.9, Storm Spades 55.4. Why is the runner-up still alive six years later?

The answer isn't in those 2.5 points. It's in everything outside them.

Winner G.O.F took the standard route: show ends, agency signs, drop album, wait for gigs. But "wait for gigs" in the Taiwanese idol market means what? Wait for a music-show circuit that doesn't exist, wait for uncertain brand deals, wait for the audience to slowly forget you[^9].

HUR took a completely different path. Felipe.Z didn't wait. He started his own agency, raised his own money, defined his own sound, decided "don't copy Korea". While the winners waited for others to give them resources, the runner-up's producer was already calculating how to stretch NT$50 million into a lifeline[^3].

> **💡 Did you know?**
> Social buzz data for the three _DD52_ debut groups: G.O.F 517 mentions, PINK FUN 621, HUR 604[^9]. The winner had the lowest numbers. In the Taiwanese idol industry, show ranking and post-show survival rate are two completely unrelated things.

The deeper difference: Felipe.Z never treated HUR as just a girl group. He treated it as a business[^3]. The group is one product line, but the business goal is building a sustainable idol-industry structure—music production, visual packaging, overseas marketing, fan-community management, all in-house.

This choice made HUR+ grow slower than K-pop groups. But it also let them find a survivable position in Taiwan's small market without relying on massive capital.

## How Far Can a Taiwanese Girl Group Go?

As of April 2026, HUR+ has nine members, three albums, 2.61 million cumulative YouTube views, a Bangkok debut just completed. Main dancer [Lian Ying](/en/people/lien-ying) has launched a solo career with hip-hop EP _EZ_, with coordinated fan support across three countries[^10]. Leader Li Shan-zhen quit her flight-attendant job for this group[^11]. C.Holly performed with a fractured hand in a cast[^13]. Hong Kong fans regularly fly to Taiwan to follow them[^15].

HUR+ isn't a success story—if "success" means TWICE or BLACKPINK. But it **is a survival story**. In an environment where Taiwanese idol groups average under three years, six years in and still releasing new music, still staging shows, still fighting from Taipei to Bangkok, still having fans willing to drop NT$1.5 million in ten minutes to send you to Korea—that itself is an answer.

Felipe.Z's line, "the more different, the better", sounds like a marketing slogan. But six years on, it looks more like a survival strategy. In an era where K-pop dominates Asia, a Taiwanese girl group can't survive by imitation. The only way out is making something different enough that "not like K-pop" becomes the identity marker.

HUR+ is proving that hypothesis. Not fully proven yet, but every extra year alive, every new country's stage reached, adds another piece of evidence.
