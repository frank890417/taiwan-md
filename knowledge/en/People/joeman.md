# Joeman: From Esports Analyst to Million-Subscriber Creator, A Long-Term Figure in the Lens

> **30-Second Summary:** Joeman, born Weng Chün-ming, initially sought a balance between esports analysis and work at MSI, before turning to YouTube after an unexpectedly viral unboxing video. From breaking one million subscribers in 2018 to being invited to Apple Park in 2025, his story is also a narrative of how Taiwanese creators transform personal interests into teams, shows, and public communication capabilities. [^1] [^2] [^3]

![Portrait of Joeman sitting at an indoor table](/article-images/people/joeman-portrait-desk-2017.webp)

_Figure 1 | Joeman sitting at an indoor table. Author: Joeman Weng Chün-ming; Source: [Wikimedia Commons Image Page](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Joeman-0617.jpg); Licensed under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International (CC BY-SA 4.0). [^16]_

In 2012, 25-year-old Weng Chün-ming returned to his dormitory at 1 or 2 a.m. after weekend matches, looked in the mirror, and asked himself: "Why did it come to this?" During the day, he worked as a product manager at MSI (Micro-Star International); on the other side, he served as an analyst for the Taiwan Esports League. Both jobs were related to the games he loved, but gradually, his interest became a source of pressure. [^1]

The official channel’s page in August 2026 showed approximately 2.88 million subscribers and 3,600 videos. This is a public snapshot that changes over time and should not be treated as a fixed resume. [^4] Starting from 3C (computer, communication, consumer electronics) unboxings, Joeman’s content gradually expanded to include budget vs. luxury showdowns, travel, real estate, interviews, cars, team management, and anti-drug and anti-fraud public service videos.

What is worth watching in this journey is not just the success story of "how one person became famous," but a more difficult question: **Once the audience has recognized a creator, can they still leave their most successful format?**

## Before Games Became Work: Analyst, Product Manager, and the First Letting Go

Joeman’s public career did not start at the unboxing desk. In a 2012 ETtoday report, it was noted that he had already graduated from National Chiao Tung University, working as a product manager at MSI while simultaneously serving as an esports analyst. Due to juggling two jobs and being unable to accompany his family, and because games no longer held their original charm once they became work, he decided to resign from the analyst role. [^1]

He wrote at the time: "This decision looks impulsive, but actually, I have considered it for a long time." The weight of this sentence lies in the fact that it does not read like an inspirational voiceover added by someone looking back at their later success. It was a judgment made by him regarding career fatigue before he became a million-subscriber YouTuber.

He also said: "In hindsight, I overestimated myself." Switching identities between full-time work and analyst duties did not leave the flexibility imagined. For someone who had just left campus and was trying to turn an interest into a career, the problem was not just time management, but that every choice redefined that interest: Was gaming still just a game? Or had it become a job that had to be performed, evaluated, and scrutinized by the audience? [^1]

## The Accidental Turn of Unboxing Videos Rewrote His Content Direction

A 2019 interview with _Vision_ magazine provided another detail of the turning point: Joeman originally intended to develop towards esports analysis and live streaming, but became unexpectedly famous due to a casually shot 3C unboxing video, leading him to seriously operate YouTube. The report quoted his observation that two years of traffic on Twitch were less than two weeks on YouTube, so he gave up game live streaming and shifted to making more types of videos. [^2]

The contrast here is clear. He did not first determine "that he was an unboxing-type creator" before starting to film unboxings. Instead, platform feedback first pointed out a path for him, and he then decided whether to walk into it. A creator's direction sometimes does not start with a self-introduction, but gradually takes shape after a certain piece of content receives audience response.

By late July 2018, Joeman reached one million subscribers. _Vision_ then summarized his content as interviews, unboxings, and travel. This combination already showed that he did not lock the channel into hardware specifications, but extended "I try it for you first" into "I walk into an unfamiliar scene for you first." [^2]

He later said in an interview: "The vulgarity we cater to is the vulgarity calculated by the algorithm." This sentence is not merely a complaint about the platform, but an admission that content creation must constantly adjust between expression and distribution. What the audience wants to see, what the algorithm pushes, and what the creator is willing to do do not naturally overlap; they can only be approached through repeated trial and error.

## Turning "Expensive vs. Cheap" into a Taiwanese Question

Joeman’s most recognizable show format places two price points into the same question: Where does the difference between high price and low price ultimately lie? _Joe's Showdown_ is easy to understand not just because numbers are written into the title, but because it allows the audience to代入 (substitute/identify with) their own life experiences.

Watching an episode of a high-end vs. budget cooking showdown, the audience may not necessarily want to buy either option. They want to know how service, materials, environment, and sense of identity are packaged by price. This question can be placed on a teppanyaki grill, or in a hotel, a house, a car, or a travel plan.

Therefore, Joeman’s shows superficially compare products, but fundamentally compare lifestyles. It places "what is good about things I can't afford" and "whether cheap choices can retain dignity" on the same table. This format is well-suited for Taiwanese audiences because Taiwan's consumer culture values both careful budgeting and curiosity towards expensive things.

But the more successful the format, the more easily the creator is defined by the format in return. When the audience expects every episode to be more expensive, larger, and more exaggerated, the show may become nothing more than a competition of specifications. Joeman later added documentaries, long-form travel, real estate, and team content, precisely to move the channel from "which of these two things is better" to "how a person or an industry is seen."

## From One-Person Filming to a Studio

A 2020 UDN Tech Gamer team interview recorded the stage where Joeman’s channel shifted from one-person production to multi-person collaboration. The team at that time already included a cameraman, editor, 3C editor, and manager. Different members handled image, planning, products, client communication, and post-production respectively. [^3]

In this interview, there was an important self-judgment: "Ten years like a dream of being an internet celebrity; many people think the profession of internet celebrity can only be short-term. Partly true, but not entirely. If you constantly correct direction and develop new content, I believe YouTuber can be a lifelong career!" [^3]

This sentence re-describes "internet celebrity" from a short-term traffic identity into a profession requiring long-term correction. Its key is not maintaining the same persona forever, but continuously developing new content so that the audience has reasons to accept the next version of themselves.

In the team workflow, planning is usually initiated by Joeman, and members then discuss and execute together. After filming is completed, the footage is handed over to editing and post-production; Joeman will go back and forth to confirm about two or three times before uploading. Commercial cooperation is not shooting directly upon receiving products, but going through topic selection, script, visuals, client verification, filming, and editing; completing one commercial video takes about a month. [^3]

These details illustrate that the channel's "natural feel" is actually built on a massive amount of process. What the audience sees is one person talking to the camera; behind the scenes, there are multiple discussions, version revisions, and negotiations of commercial boundaries. A YouTube personal brand, once it reaches a certain scale, must rely on a team to maintain the immediacy of the individual.

Joeman also stated the channel's review principles very directly: "Whatever the test result is, that is what it is." When clients were dissatisfied with the final result, the team once chose to provide the original master tape, preserving the possibility for the audience to see the actual test results. This does not mean commercial cooperation can completely exclude interest relationships, but rather that if review content loses verifiability, the channel's most important asset of trust will also disappear. [^3]

## From Product Unboxings to Apple Park: The Moment Creators Are Seen by the Industry

The currently visible content on the official channel already includes long-form travel, member-exclusive campus speeches, creative reflections, studio daily life, and numerous unboxings. Taking _I Travel Japan Alone_ as an example, the video takes a self-driving journey of over 4,000 kilometers as the main axis, going from the southernmost tip of Japan all the way to Hokkaido. [^4]

Unboxings and travel require two different types of viewing patience. Unboxings break products down into specifications, usage scenarios, and purchase judgments. Long-distance travel keeps time, fatigue, routes, and accidents intact. When Joeman moved from phones on a desk to the roads of Japan, the channel's content shifted from "trying it for you first" to "walking with you."

In September 2025, NOWnews reported that Joeman and other Taiwanese creators were invited to an Apple iPhone 17-related event, quickly releasing unboxing videos after the keynote. The report also compiled netizens' observations of the changes in his imagery from iPhone X to iPhone 17, making his "eight-year unboxing history" simultaneously a history of phone generations and the physical evolution of the creator. [^9]

Singaporean English-language media _8days_ also reported on his visit to Apple Park to preview the iPhone 17. This international perspective does not mean his reviews are necessarily correct, nor does being invited equate to brand endorsement. What it proves is that Taiwanese tech creators have become a recognizable media role in the international product launch system. [^10]

For Joeman, this position perhaps also responded to the young man in 2012 who was still working at MSI. He had wanted to make first-class laptop products back then. Years later, he stood at the product launch site, becoming the person who brought products back into the Taiwanese context for the audience. [^1]

## The Creator's Body Is Also Filmed Into the Channel: Creators Are No Longer Just Hosts

A 2025 NOWnews report pointed out that Joeman had publicly stated his past weight reached 103 kilograms, reducing it by 31 kilograms within a year through diet control and exercise, later dropping to 71 kilograms. He also mentioned receiving a blood sugar risk warning during a health check, which led him to start losing weight. [^11]

The reason this experience became news is precisely because the audience has been watching the same person for a long time. As products change generation by generation, the host's body, clothing, and way of speaking also change. The audience remembers not only the phone lens, processor, and battery life, but also the person standing in the frame during every unboxing.

This makes the creator's body part of the content, but also a source of pressure. Images can preserve change, but they also constantly bring back the past self for comparison. For someone who stands in front of the lens for a long time, transformation is not just changing topics, but also reprocessing the audience's expectations of their appearance, life, and age.

## The Marijuana Incident: Apology, Judiciary, and Subsequent Actions Must Be Viewed Separately

On November 6, 2023, Joeman's residence was searched for suspected possession of marijuana. The next day, he released an apology video on YouTube. The video began with: "Hello everyone, I am Joeman. This is an apology video." He then publicly admitted that he had consumed marijuana both abroad and in Taiwan, and possessed a small amount for personal use. [^5] [^6]

_The News Lens_ analyzed this video with "fast, sincere, accurate": He appeared the day after the incident, did not write a long essay seeking reasons, and expressed willingness to cooperate with the investigation. This is a media author's evaluation of public communication, not a legal conclusion from judicial authorities. The two need to be clearly separated. [^5]

The subsequent judicial process also requires precise description. A 2025 CNA report indicated that Joeman received a one-year suspension of prosecution for marijuana possession and was required to provide 100 hours of community service; the New Taipei District Prosecutors' Office later uploaded three collaborative videos, including a long anti-fraud film, a one-minute anti-fraud version, and a one-minute anti-drug public service video. [^7]

CNA also reported that the investigation determined he purchased 10 grams of marijuana in Sanchong, New Taipei; in the anti-drug video, Joeman said: "My house could be raided, so why wouldn't the police raid yours?" He used his own experience to urge people not to harbor a sense of luck when buying or selling drugs. [^7]

_United Daily News_ reported that the conditions for suspension of prosecution included writing a letter of repentance and 100 hours of community service; Joeman proactively proposed to offset the hours with public service videos involving planning, filming, editing, and post-production, with the videos not allowed to be monetized. [^8] These videos are not merely image repair content, but legal education work completed under judicial处分 (disposition/conditions).

In the long anti-fraud video, Joeman invited investment celebrities whose identities were impersonated and fraud victims to appear on camera, entered stock-saving fraud communities to observe their tactics, and then collaborated with police to arrest couriers. This process directed his content production capability towards public education; simultaneously, the incident itself does not disappear because the video is completed. The apology is immediate communication, the suspension of prosecution is a judicial procedure, and the subsequent work is another type of action that needs to be observed. [^7]

## How Controversy Forms: The Costs of Speed, Trust, and Judgment

Joeman's content operation has also been accompanied by other controversies. Publicly organized data lists the 2017 interview controversy, the 2018 misunderstanding of font licensing information, the 2023 discussion on convenience store co-branded products, and the 2025 event of taking down a video and apologizing for filming a controversial figure. Since the Wikipedia page itself states that more reliable sources are needed, these materials should be treated as an index in research, and individual events should still be verified against original reports and the parties' statements. [^12]

The commonality among these events is not that they all belong to the same type of error, but that they all occurred in an environment of high-speed publishing, multi-person collaboration, and high audience trust. Creators are expected to react instantly, but instant does not mean skipping verification; the audience likes authenticity, but authenticity does not mean no responsibility is required.

In the _Vision_ interview, Joeman said: "My interest is to frustrate those who hate me." This sentence has the provocative feel of the internet age, but also reveals his survival strategy: turning criticism into the drive for continuous exposure. However, continuous exposure is both a capability and a risk. When every choice is cut into short videos, turned into titles, and placed in the comment section, creators must bear not only the success or failure of their works, but also the public consequences of their own judgments. [^2]

## What He Truly Established Is the Ability to "Walk the Scene for the Audience First"

If we only call Joeman a 3C YouTuber, we miss the capability he truly established. 3C unboxings turn specifications into usage scenarios; budget vs. luxury showdowns turn price differences into discussable experiences; travel plans turn geographical distance into companionship; team videos turn behind-the-scenes division of labor into stories; anti-drug and anti-fraud videos turn a personal judicial experience into public education.

These topics seem scattered, but the core is consistent: **He is accustomed to walking the scene for the audience first, then editing the path walked into a version that can be watched, compared, and discussed.**

This also explains why his channel could grow from one unboxing video into multiple rooms. Products are the entrance, the host is the辨识度 (recognizability), the team is the production capacity, and audience trust is the foundation shared by all rooms. The larger the foundation, the more topics it allows; but once the foundation is damaged by erroneous judgment, repair will be much more difficult than repairing a single video.

## Walk Bell John Awards and _Joe's Showdown_: How Format Becomes a Representative Work

The official award video for the 6th Walk Bell John Awards in 2024 listed Joeman as the winner of the "Annual Individual Creator Award," with the winning work being "The Real Hotel Is Here! A Night in a Luxury Hotel for 150,000 Dollars!" from _Joe's Showdown S2_. [^17]

This award data makes _Joe's Showdown_ not just a show format for comparing high and low prices, but also the representative work recognized by external creator awards for Joeman. It also returns to the question at the beginning of the article: Once the audience has recognized a person, can they leave their most successful format? The answer currently visible is not abandoning the format, but making the format into a show that can carry characters, guests, scenes, and production scale.

This article does not interpret a single award as the absolute ranking of Taiwan's YouTube, nor does it automatically equate winning an award with all works being recognized; the official video can directly support only three data points: the award name, the winning channel, and the winning work. [^17]

## Conclusion: The Lens Is Still Asking

In 2012, Weng Chün-ming asked himself in the early morning dormitory why he had turned games into work. In 2019, he talked about how the algorithm calculated "vulgarity," and how to disappoint those who hated him. In 2020, he described YouTuber as a career that could be done for a lifetime, provided one constantly corrects direction and develops new content. In 2025, he stood at the product site of Apple Park, and also in the lens of anti-drug and anti-fraud public service videos. [^1] [^2] [^3] [^7]

Placing these few frames together, Joeman's story is no longer just "from small figure to big internet celebrity." It is more like a continuous negotiation: How does interest become work, how does work become a team, how does a team maintain authenticity, and how does authenticity bear errors?

One video can unbox a phone, but it cannot unbox a person's ten years within ten minutes. What the audience sees is every upload; what truly determines whether a creator can continue is, before the next topic selection, whether they are willing to re-examine what they are bringing into the lens.
