> **30-Second Overview:** Lee Tzu-siiuan is a political figure with an engineering background who has served as Deputy County Magistrate of Taipei County, Secretary-General of the Executive Yuan, Deputy Mayor of Kaohsiung, and Deputy Mayor of Taipei. He began his career within the engineering system of the Taipei Public Works Bureau before moving into local government and central administration. On March 10, 2026, he left the Taipei City Government to run for Mayor of New Taipei City.

On April 17, 2019, the filling of the Zuoying underpass in Kaohsiung began. At the time, Lee Tzu-siiuan, then Deputy Mayor of Kaohsiung, stated during a city government briefing that filling the underpass and restoring it to a surface road would reduce detour times. The city government also noted that this project would solve the problem of flooding in the underpass during heavy rainfall, with traffic expected to resume by the end of May. [^1]

## From Taipei Public Works Bureau to Taipei County Government

Lee Tzu-siiuan holds a Master's degree from the Institute of Electrical Engineering at National Taipei University of Technology. In his early career, he worked at the New Construction Division of the Taipei City Government Public Works Bureau, serving as Chief Engineer and Division Director, and also served as the Director of the Sanitary Sewerage Engineering Division. According to appointment data released by the Executive Yuan in 2014, he participated in the construction of projects such as Zhengqi Bridge, Xinyi Branch Line, Zhoumei Expressway, and various sports and leisure centers; the Executive Yuan also stated that during his three-plus years at the Sanitary Sewerage Engineering Division, he helped increase the sewerage connection rate to 80%. These details are descriptions of his resume and achievements from Executive Yuan appointment news and should not be equated directly with results achieved solely by him. [^2]

Appointment and removal records from the National Chengchi University (NCCU) Government Official Database provide a clearer chronological sequence: in the early 2000s, he served as Chief Engineer of the New Construction Division of the Taipei City Public Works Bureau before transferring to Director of the Sanitary Sewerage Engineering Division; in 2008, he became Director of the Taipei County Government Public Works Bureau; in 2009, he was appointed Deputy County Magistrate of Taipei County; and in 2011, he transitioned to Deputy Mayor of New Taipei City. [^3]

The significance of this path lies in the fact that it did not begin with elections. Lee Tzu-siiuan first accumulated professional expertise and connections within engineering agencies before moving into deputy leadership roles in local government. An engineer on-site deals with blueprints, progress, and quality; a Deputy County Magistrate deals with departmental division of labor, council budgets, and local politics.

The experience he accumulated in Taipei's public works system explains why the "engineering background" label followed him into politics. Working in engineering agencies involves more than just site supervision; it includes estimation, procurement, site inspections, land acquisition, traffic maintenance, and public communication. Most of this work does not appear on election posters, yet it determines whether a project moves from a proposal to a physical road. The Executive Yuan's appointment news specifically listed various construction projects and the sewerage connection rate, reflecting how the government at the time understood his expertise: using verifiable engineering progress as evidence of administrative capability. [^2]

The NCCU database provides another perspective. It does not describe "how well" he performed, but merely records the date of appointment, the date of removal, and which agency held the position. Placing this removal data alongside government press releases reveals how Lee Tzu-siiuan's public role shifted: first as a professional bureaucrat, then as a deputy leader in local government, and subsequently as a coordinator in central administration. [^2][^3]

## Secretary-General of the Executive Yuan: Bringing Local Experience to the Center

In February 2014, Lee Tzu-siiuan assumed the role of Secretary-General of the Executive Yuan. The Executive Yuan explained at the time that his duties included not only the internal operations of the Executive Yuan but also communication and coordination between the executive and legislative branches and various ministries. This position is far removed from an engineering site, yet it demands the same core competency: enabling different agencies and political actors to form workable arrangements for time-bound policies. [^2]

The NCCU Government Official Database records that Lee Tzu-siiuan was appointed Secretary-General of the Executive Yuan on February 27, 2014, and was subsequently permitted to resign in December 2014. The database also notes a record of reappointment on the same day and removal the following January. For verifying the timeline of this tenure, official appointment and removal data is more reliable than later resumes. [^3]

## Kaohsiung's Roads, Underpasses, and Water Conservancy Issues

In December 2018, Kaohsiung Mayor-elect Han Kuo-yu confirmed the recruitment of Lee Tzu-siiuan, then Deputy Mayor of New Taipei City, as Deputy Mayor of Kaohsiung. [^4] Public records from the Kaohsiung City Government show that following the railway undergrounding project, urban overpasses were progressively demolished and the Zuoying underpass was subsequently filled; Lee Tzu-siiuan provided the engineering rationale for this in a city government briefing. The official explanation for the Zuoying underpass filling project grouped traffic convenience, flooding risks, and regional development into a single engineering justification. [^1]

Another announcement from the Kaohsiung City Water Resources Bureau records that in April 2020, Lee Tzu-siiuan inspected a dredging site at Xingfu River, instructing officials to accelerate the handling of sediment and odor issues; the Water Resources Bureau divided the work into three zones for simultaneous dredging. While these cases do not constitute a complete list of his personal achievements, they illustrate the functional roles he was assigned within the Kaohsiung City Government: roads, water conservancy, drainage, and urban construction are all matters that require bringing departments, the council, and local residents onto the same "construction blueprint." [^5]

![The bed of Xingfu River in Kaohsiung after being drained, with dredging machinery parked on the bank](/article-images/people/kaohsiung-xingfu-river-dredging-2020.webp)

_April 20, 2020, Xingfu River in Kaohsiung being drained in preparation for dredging. Photo: Kaohsiung City Government Water Resources Bureau, released under open data declaration from the bureau's website._

The tone of the Xingfu River announcement differed from that of the Zuoying underpass. The former began with odors and water quality, tracing back to rainwater, gutter trash, grease, and sewage interception; the latter began with the traffic issues of the underpass, pushing forward toward surface roads and regional development. Together, these two sets of official data present the daily reality of local governance: what citizens feel is usually smell, standing water, or detours, while what the government must manage is a series of systems hidden beneath the ground. [^1][^5]

On June 6, 2020, Han Kuo-yu was recalled; June 11 was his final day as mayor. On that day, Lee Tzu-siiuan delivered a speech at a farewell event outside the city hall in his capacity as Deputy Mayor, before leaving the Kaohsiung City Government with the mayor's team. [^6]

## Taipei's Transportation Projects: Engineering Becomes Cross-Departmental Governance

In December 2022, Taipei Mayor-elect Chiang Wan-an announced his first wave of city government personnel, appointing Lee Tzu-siiuan as Deputy Mayor. [^7] One of the public issues he addressed was the traffic situation in the Neihu Technology Park. A 2023 announcement from the Taipei City Government showed that he served as the convener of a city-level task force for Neihu transportation; the city government focused on three main pillars: clearing intersection obstructions, reducing vehicle/motorcycle volume, and improving traffic engineering, while also discussing cooperation regarding buses, commuting, and green transport with enterprises in the park. [^8]

The city government announced that during weekday afternoon peaks, average travel times on major roads in Neihu decreased by approximately 5% to 11% compared to the first quarter of the previous year; the average waiting time for National Highway Bus Route 9005 decreased by 20 minutes, and the ridership of two express bus lines grew by 13% to 15% within two months. These figures are claims made by the Taipei City Government using telecommunications data and traffic statistics; they represent the city's method of measuring project success rather than an independent performance evaluation. [^8]

Neihu's traffic also pushed Lee Tzu-siiuan's role one step further than "understanding engineering." While road widening or underpass filling has clear construction boundaries, park transportation requires getting enterprises to adjust commuting methods, allowing bus routes, intersection management, parking, and public transport to change in unison. Here, engineering expertise refers not just to drafting plans and supervision, but also to transforming an urban problem into cross-departmental and cross-enterprise collaboration.

This collaboration also brings a limitation: the city government's own performance figures cannot replace a complete public policy evaluation. The Taipei City announcements list travel times, bus waiting times, and express bus ridership; these indicators show what the city _wants_ to measure, but they cannot independently answer whether Neihu's traffic has been "solved." Peak hours, different roads, corporate commuting patterns, and public transport capacity will always influence a reader's understanding of "improvement." [^8]

## The Same Resume, Different Problems in Different Cities

Looking at Lee Tzu-siiuan's various experiences together, one can see that "construction" is not a static job. His early public works roles in Taipei focused on the professional processes within engineering agencies; his deputy leadership roles in Taipei County and New Taipei City began to include local councils and intra-government coordination; his work as Secretary-General of the Executive Yuan expanded the scope of coordination to central ministries and the Legislative Yuan. By the time he reached Kaohsiung, underpasses, drainage, and rivers became living issues felt directly by citizens; returning to Taipei, Neihu's traffic required the government and park enterprises to collectively change commuting habits. These differences can all be found in official job titles, engineering announcements, and project divisions of labor. [^1][^2][^3][^5][^8]

## Leaving Taipei City Government to Run for New Taipei City Mayor

On February 10, 2026, Lee Tzu-siiuan announced that he would resign as Deputy Mayor of Taipei at the end of February to prepare for the New Taipei City mayoral election; New Taipei City Mayor Hou Yu-ih expressed full support. [^9]

On February 23, Lee Tzu-siiuan confirmed that he had submitted his resignation to Chiang Wan-an, with the final decision resting with the mayor. He also stated that if he moves to the New Taipei City Government, he will continue to provide advice to Chiang Wan-an regarding development in the dual-city (Taipei-New Taipei) area. [^10]

On March 5, Chiang Wan-an announced that he had approved Lee Tzu-siiuan's resignation effective March 10. Central News Agency reported that Lee Tzu-siiuan would return to New Taipei City in March to seek the Kuomintang nomination and enter the next New Taipei City mayoral election. [^11]

On November 3, PTS News reported that while campaigning at Sanlu Market, Lee Tzu-siiuan proposed policies including the Luzhe Bridge, Luzhou Hospital, and the Dahan Creek MRT North-South Line. [^12]

On May 29, 2026, _Mirror Media_ relayed a report from the online media outlet _Jimu Suo_, which questioned how Lee Tzu-siiuan and his wife obtained the funds for their residence on Minzu Road in Banqiao—noting a total price of approximately NT$46.77 million, a mortgage of NT$37 million, and questioning the source of the remaining NT$9.77 million in down payment. [^13] On July 22, Lee's campaign office explained that the house contract was signed in 2013, with the down payment originally scheduled for payment between 2013 and 2014. Due to water leakage issues and residents suing the developer, he suspended a final payment of NT$4 million, which was not fully paid until 2016. The campaign stated that the funds came from the couple's salaries, bonuses, dividends, and a mortgage on a previous home, adding that they would take legal action if the smear campaign continued. [^14]
