> **30-second overview:** Erin (連穎), born 1999 in Taichung, began ballet, folk dance, and Chinese dance in second grade, switching to street dance in high school. In 2020 on _DD52_, during the Episode 2 battle stage she told mentor Pan Wei-bo, "I want everyone on site to know **the dance standard a girl group should have**" — a line that became her signature. Runner-up team Storm Spades debuted as [HUR+](/en/people/hur-plus-band) with her as main dancer. In 2025 she released her debut solo EP _EZ_, taking a hip-hop/R&B direction with Taiwanese Hokkien elements, deliberately avoiding the safe ballad route. On her 2026 birthday, fans crowdfunded a stage at Dadaocheng where she premiered the new song "One Second Late." She releases group albums and solo EPs simultaneously, advancing both tracks in parallel. In Taiwan, she is the first girl group member to do so.

June 19, 2020, _DD52_ Episode 2.

104 girls competing for a debut spot. Storm Spades' Erin stood on the battle stage, facing C.Holly and Xile. She walked to the front, looked up at head mentor Pan Wei-bo, and said:

> **✦** "What I want to say is, today no matter who my opponent is, or how many people don't believe in me, I came to this stage without fear. I hope Teacher Wei-bo gives me a chance — I want everyone here to know **the dance standard a girl group should have**."[^1]

Pan Wei-bo chose her to advance.

That line is the anchor of the whole story.

## The Dancing Girl from Taichung

Erin was born April 27, 1999 in Taichung[^2]. In her _DD52_ contestant intro, she summarized her dance journey in a few short lines:

> **✦** "I started ballet, folk dance, and Chinese dance in second grade, all the way until high school when I started street dance, and I've been dancing ever since."[^3]

The transition from classical to street dance is far more complex than "just switched." Ballet and Chinese dance emphasize control, precision, lines; street dance emphasizes groove, improvisation, expression. Switching between two nearly opposite body-language systems — technique is only the starting point; the fundamental challenge is re-understanding "what is dance."

She also said she had "participated in many open auditions and been a trainee"[^3]. Before _DD52_, she had already tried many times. Long before _DD52_, she had been knocking on the idol door.

> **✦** "Ever since I was little I've had a star dream — I just want to be a star!"[^4]

In the official _DD52_ contestant profile, she described herself: "I'm a very typical Taurus girl — extremely stubborn about what I love and want. Slow to warm up, but once you know me you'll find I'm actually really goofy and a bit weird."[^3]

> **📝 Curator's Note**
> Erin's dance background differs from the typical idol audition trainee. In the Korean system, trainees usually start from zero with standardized company training. Erin entered with over a decade of dance foundation: classical base from second grade through high school, plus street dance training after. She is a dancer who walked into the idol framework, not a blank slate manufactured into one.

## DD52: 55.4 Points, Runner-Up

September 4, 2020, _DD52_ finals. Erin's team Storm Spades scored 55.4, losing to Blazing Hearts' 57.9, taking runner-up[^5].

After the show, producer Philip signed all six members of Storm Spades to form [HUR+](/en/people/hur-plus-band). Erin's role in the group is **main dancer and sub-vocalist**; the recognition she built on _DD52_ with that line directly translated into her group positioning[^2].

November 6, 2020, HUR debuted. _DD52_ won the 56th Golden Bell Award for Variety Show[^5].

Over the next three years, Erin experienced everything a new-generation Taiwanese idol group goes through: album releases (_REVELATION_ 2021, _Crimzon_ 2024), concerts (Huashan Creative Park, Legacy Taipei), participation in _Future Girls_ expanding to nine members, crowdfunding a Korean promotion trip[^6].

Throughout this group activity period, she was always the one who drew eyes on stage. Jasmine holds center position, but Erin's movement power and body control make the gaze involuntarily follow her in nine-person choreography.

That _DD52_ Episode 2 line — "the dance standard a girl group should have" — wasn't just talk. Every performance she delivers on it.

## Another Track Outside the Group

In 2025, Erin did something almost unprecedented for a Taiwanese girl group member: **released a solo EP while the group remained active**[^7].

In Korea, this is a mature industry mechanism. All four BLACKPINK members have solo albums; TWICE members have successively released solo works. Group and individual are two parallel tracks feeding each other: group activity builds popularity, individual activity expands audience, neither stops.

In Taiwan, this dual-track system barely exists. Taiwanese idol groups are scarce to begin with; those surviving past three years are fewer. Most groups exhaust resources just maintaining group activity; members' solo development is an unthinkable luxury.

But Erin did it. January 2026 she released the group's third album _9OF9 ARCANA_, late April the whole group flew to Bangkok for a show. Not a single group commitment missed, solo work advancing in sync. And she chose to test the waters song by song.

December 2024, she collaborated with Jincheng and Mongolian rapper GINJIN on "TAKE A CHANCE." May 2025 released "Cherry Pie," September duet "DIE 4 YOU" with Bobee, accompanied by an Official Visualizer MV[^6]. Three singles in half a year laid a path from collaboration to solo, testing market acceptance of "Erin solo."

Then came the formal EP. December 3, 2025, _EZ_ released. Five tracks[^7]:

| Track                     | Feature                         |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| EZ ft. Alph FRαNKIE       | Title track, hip-hop style      |
| Get Right Back ft. LanLan | Collaboration with HUR+ member  |
| Na$hley ADDachi Pt.2      | Pre-release MV nearing 1M views |
| That's Really Bad Remix   | **Taiwanese Hokkien elements**  |
| DIE 4 YOU ft. Bobee       | R&B                             |

The tracklist reveals her direction: hip-hop and R&B. Not safe ballads, not the healing route typical of Mandarin female singers. Especially notable: track four "That's Really Bad Remix" contains **Taiwanese Hokkien**. A girl group member from an idol audition show putting Taiwanese hip-hop in her solo work — in the context of Taiwanese idols, this is an almost rebellious choice[^7].

At the November 30, 2025 pre-release signing event, Erin personally proposed a "quick-change" striptease stage, transforming on the spot to embody "women be brave, make it look EZ"[^8].

A reporter asked if the scale was too much; she answered:

> **✦** "My style has always been more open — I don't think it's too much at all!"[^8]

In the same interview, she said another line:

> **✦** "Jolin is my goddess; I still have so much to learn from her!"[^8]

[Jolin Tsai](/en/people/jolin-tsai), the woman who went from "last place in dance" to Mandopop dance queen doing aerial hoops ten meters up, is Erin's openly acknowledged idol. The parallel is striking: Jolin used twenty-five years to transform from "not dance material" into a diva; Erin is walking a similar path in her own way, starting from dancer, expanding the idol frame.

> **📝 Curator's Note**
> Taiwan Hip Hop Voice Podcast S3EP.89 has an Erin interview titled "From Showing Girl Group Dance Standard to Redefining All-Around Singer-Dancer"[^9]. The title precisely describes her arc from _DD52_ to solo EP: 2020 she defined "girl group dance standard"; 2025 she liberated herself from that definition.

## Fans Built a Stage at Dadaocheng

April 11, 2026, around Erin's birthday, something happened: her fans (self-named "Leopard Disciples"[^4]) crowdfunded a birthday support stage at Dadaocheng[^10].

Fans organized, funded, and executed it themselves. On that stage, Erin premiered her new song "One Second Late"[^10].

Almost simultaneously, CheerSPOT launched coordinated support ads for Erin's EP _EZ_ across **Taiwan, Japan, and Thailand**, limited to 500 sets[^11].

Birthday support, crowdfunded stage, cross-border synchronized ads. These are all standard K-pop fan culture operations. Korean idol fans booking subway ads, café events, LED billboards for birthdays is a years-established playbook. But these things happening to a **Taiwanese girl group member**, on the stage of **Dadaocheng** — one of Taipei's oldest districts — not an East District LED wall.

> **💡 Did You Know**
> Erin's fandom name is "Leopard Disciples" (豹徒), nickname "Dongquan Sweet Chili Sauce"[^4] — a Taichung local code only locals get. Her Instagram @realerin6 has 136K followers[^4], Spotify monthly listeners 5,000+[^7]. These numbers are tiny by K-pop standards, but in the Taiwanese idol ecosystem, a girl group member's personal account reaching this scale already says something.

## No Compromise

In a 2024 NIUSNEWS interview, Erin shared her core creed: effort doesn't guarantee success, but no effort guarantees failure[^4].

In the same NIUSNEWS 2024 interview, a reporter asked how she handles criticism. Some say "what makes her the dance ace," others criticize "her mouth never closes in photos." Her response:

> **✦** "Didn't take it to heart at all — actually found it hilarious."[^4]

The same interview mentions a dedicated space at her home displaying fan-made support goods and photocards. She once personally attended a female fan's performance, moved to tears seeing the fan's glowing eyes[^4].

She describes herself as "super action-oriented," "can't let an idea sit in my head for more than a week"[^4]. This trait explains a lot: why she dared speak that line in _DD52_ Episode 2, why she stepped into solo while the group was still developing, why the EP signing event's striptease stage was her own proposal.

She doesn't wait for permission.

> **✦** "Effort doesn't guarantee success, but no effort guarantees failure."
> "You only live once — live your own life pursuing the goals you want."[^4]

## Six Years Later, That Line

Summer 2020, a twenty-one-year-old Taichung girl said on an audition stage: "I want everyone to know the dance standard a girl group should have."

Six years later, 2026, starting from girl group main dancer, she has her own EP, her own musical style, her own fandom, her own support ads synchronized across three countries. Her fans built a stage in Taipei's oldest district for her to sing a new song.

Did that line get fulfilled?

Maybe more accurately: that line is growing new meanings. 2020 Erin defined "the dance standard a girl group should have." 2026 Erin is defining something broader — **how much a Taiwanese girl group member can do simultaneously**. Group album, solo EP, hip-hop collaborations, Taiwanese Hokkien fusion, three-country support, Dadaocheng stage. All in parallel, all her.

The EP is named _EZ_. Make it look easy. But every step she took to get here was anything but.

Started dancing in second grade, transitioned in high school, was a trainee, went through many open auditions, bet all her chips on one line in _DD52_ Episode 2, endured years with the runner-up group while even the winning group couldn't survive, released her own EP in an environment where Taiwanese girl group members almost never go solo, got fans to build a stage at Dadaocheng. Every step felt like "this road should end here," but every step she kept walking forward.

"Effort doesn't guarantee success, but no effort guarantees failure" is the belief she wrote in that interview. Six years on, she added four words through action: **never stop**.
