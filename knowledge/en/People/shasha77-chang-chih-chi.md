> **30-Second Overview:** Chang Chih-chi (張志祺) is one of Taiwan's most influential news-focused YouTubers, known for his channel "Chih-chi 77" (志祺七七) and its signature style of daily updates featuring summarized "cheat sheets" that present multiple perspectives. Starting from a background in information design, he attempts to bring serious issues into the public eye through "translation," continuously seeking space for dialogue within a polarized society. This article explores how he turned social reform into a viable business and his journey of managing trust and influence in a complex environment.

"For an 18-minute video, I recorded for an hour and a half, constantly feeling like I was stuck."[^1] This was the reality for Chang Chih-chi in 2018, as he transitioned from a graphic designer to a YouTuber. At the time, he was already a co-founder of the well-known design firm "Simple Message Design" (簡訊設計), yet he decided to commit to a news channel that required daily updates without rest.

The decision seemed almost madness at the time, but it was backed by a rational "Time Machine Theory": news-focused YouTubers were already a mature genre in Europe and the US, and Taiwan would inevitably follow.[^1] Seven years later, "Chih-chi 77" has surpassed 1.65 million subscribers, accumulated over 1.67 billion views, and produced more than 7,650 videos. Chang has transformed from a designer hiding behind summaries into a public figure who is "guaranteed to be recognized 3 to 10 times every time I go out."[^1][^11]

### Early Years: An Atypical Youth in an Academic Family

Chang Chih-chi was born on June 1, 1991, in Hsinchu City, into an academic family of doctors and scholars. His grandparents moved to Taiwan with the National Revolutionary Army in 1949. His father had taught at a university in the United States; both parents held doctoral or dual master's degrees, and many family members possessed high levels of education. He was a top student in elementary and middle school, serving as class president for three consecutive years while attending Hsinchu High School. His passion for club activities and class competitions, however, led to poor academic performance during much of high school (at one point ranking second to last in his class). His family provided him with immense freedom and tolerance; his upbringing—which included a home without a television—meant he often struggled to keep up with pop culture trends, but it also fostered an open curiosity toward subcultures, anime, and the digital world. After graduating from university, he served as a Private First Class in the Republic of China Air Force Honor Guard (as a technical specialist in arts/design rather than a ceremonial performer), officially beginning his entrepreneurial journey after discharge.

During his senior year of high school, a remark from his father became a turning point: "You like organizing events and making friends, but all the talented people are at top universities; you should work hard for that." He began to push himself, eventually enrolling in the Department of Urban Planning at National Cheng Kung University. Another piece of inspiration from his father—"Do you want to make a living playing the guitar later?"—taught him to use the "process of elimination" to find his life's direction, discovering what truly suited him by identifying what he disliked. This "atypical" upbringing in an academic household laid the foundation for his diverse perspective in design and social communication.[^8][^9][^10]

### Origins: When Urban Design Met Wenlinyuan

Chang's motivation for entrepreneurship did not stem from a desire for fame, but from a sense of "discrepancy." While studying Urban Planning at National Cheng Kung University, he encountered the Wenlinyuan (文林苑) redevelopment controversy just before graduation. "I realized deeply that what I learned in textbooks had a massive gap compared to social reality."[^1] At the time, professors required students to sketch beautiful future blueprints, but cold data told him that the land should remain in its original, simple state. "It was a painful process—knowing you couldn't even convince yourself, yet having to explain it logically to others."[^4]

To bridge this information gap, he and partner Wang Cheng-hsiang founded "Simple Message Design" in 2015, along with the social-impact brand "Inconsistent Graphics" (圖文不符). Their first "keyboard revolution" was launching a crowdfunding course on Hahow titled "More Than Just Summaries," teaching the public how to use information visualization techniques for complex data.[^5] For him, information design is not just about aesthetics; it is "the infrastructure of social communication." His team has won numerous international design awards, including the Red Dot Design Award, Gold Dot Design Award, iF Design Award, GOOD DESIGN AWARD, and the German Design Award.[^13]

### The Turning Point: Seven Years of a "Self-Media Wikipedia"

In 2018, encouraged by his friend A-Di (阿滴), Chang launched the "Chih-chi 77" channel. To rapidly build trust and traffic, he set an extremely high-intensity goal of daily updates. This forced the team to establish factory-like rigorous processes: from topic selection, scripting, and filming to post-production, every stage has a dedicated person responsible.

This team-based operation gave the channel incredible stability but also brought commercial challenges. Because news topics often involve sensitive issues, the channel is frequently "yellow-flagged" by YouTube (meaning it is ineligible for ad revenue sharing). In 2020, over 55% of the channel's videos were flagged.[^1] To survive, Chang established a business model primarily based on "spoken advertisements" and managed memberships as a "safety belt" to ensure the channel could persist even when hit by algorithm changes.

Beyond current affairs, Chang has actively invested in the new media ecosystem. He co-founded the Taiwan New Media Video Creators Association with A-Di (serving as its 3rd term Chairperson) and co-initiated FEAT.CON, a Taiwan video creator annual conference. In 2021, he co-founded Heqi Capital to invest in film and entertainment projects. He has served as an official Pokémon Trading Card Game streamer in Taiwan and participated in large-scale civic initiatives such as #TaiwanCanHelp and #GoodHomeIAmHome. He also released personal books, _Welcome to Chih-chi 77_ (2020) and _Citizens Can Be Productive_ (2021), and relaunched the _Chih-chi 77_ podcast in 2022, which once reached number one on the Apple Podcast charts.[^0]

Chang has also extended his reach into children's education by founding the self-owned brand "Mang-Gou-Gou" (芒狗狗), developing literacy education picture books designed specifically for children aged 3–6.[^6] He attempts to use local teaching materials to help parents and children build shared cultural memories, translating complex financial literacy and social concepts for the next generation. Recently, he has expanded his influence with new works including public etiquette picture book series, treasure hunt books, and train-traveling etiquette sets.[^7][^35]

### Controversy: The Dilemma of "Neutrality" in a Polarized Era

Chang has stated that the core values of his channel are "truth, diversity, and voice,"[^1] attempting to present different viewpoints in every video. However, this "presentation of multiple perspectives" is often interpreted as "fake neutrality" or "playing both sides" in Taiwan's highly politically polarized environment.

In July 2025, legislator Wang Hung-wei accused Chang of "living off government contracts," sparking intense online debate.[^2] According to statistics, Simple Message Design undertook approximately 34 government contracts between 2016 and 2024, totaling about NT$37 million, with the Taipei City Government being its largest client.[^2][^25] Chang responded that for a company of 50 people, personnel costs over seven years reached NT$200 to 300 million, and contract revenue accounted for only a tiny fraction, noting that clients span various political parties and counties.[^2]

The subsequent "Mass Recall Summary" incident pushed the controversy to its peak. Some viewers felt the video content was biased, leading to an avalanche of unsubscribes and a drop in traffic. On Threads, Chang admitted he had considered stopping updates, lamenting: "In a society like ours that treats trust as its foundation, 'not easily believing others' sounds reasonable in theory, but is highly counter-intuitive in practice."[^3]

### Significance: The "Charismatic Aura" AI Cannot Steal

Facing the wave of AI and the impact of short-form video, Chang has demonstrated the resilience of "simple long-termism." He believes that while AI excels at aggregating information, it lacks the "charismatic aura" (魅力靈光) that touches the heart.[^1] "Many of the most important inspirations in my life often come from the parts that AI would delete—a small note, or a very useless little story."[^1]

For Chang, entrepreneurship and running a channel is a process of continuous trial and error. He describes his own life as simple, using mobile ordering systems that only save fixed dishes, pouring all his energy into "repeatedly doing boring but correct things."[^1] In an era where polarization is difficult to reverse, he does not seek universal approval; instead, he tries to find the group in Taiwanese society that "does not like heated discussions" and continues a long-distance run centered on understanding.
