# This Group Of People: Seven Members, Thirteen Years Without Anyone Leaving the Group; Hiatus but Not Disbanded

On February 15, 2024, This Group Of People (TGOP) uploaded a video nearly fourteen minutes long to their official YouTube channel, titled "This Group Of People Stops Updating." At the beginning of the video, leader Zhan Rong said, "Our channel began with a camera, and it ends with a camera."[^1] A reporter from Yahoo News wrote that he then gripped the members' hands and repeatedly asked, "Are you disbanding?" and "To all the media: are you disbanding?"[^1]

Counting from the upload of their first short film in 2011, this marks their thirteenth year of filmmaking, and not one of the seven members has ever left.[^2][^3] Since this video was uploaded, the main channel has not posted any new long-form videos; what has stopped is the practice of the seven members submitting two videos together every month.

> **30-Second Overview:** This Group Of People (TGOP) consists of seven members: Zhan Rong, Zhan Rui, Yin-Sheng, Mu Xing, Dong Zai, Nick, and Shi Tou. They uploaded their first video in August 2011, and over thirteen years, no one has left the group. They became famous through their "Classic Quotes" series, where members took turns playing waiters, instructors, or dentists, and in 2017, they became Taiwan's first YouTuber group to surpass two million subscribers.[^4] Since February 2024, the main channel has stopped updating long-form content, though members have repeatedly clarified that they have not disbanded. Zhan Rong stated there are currently no plans for group filming, but he did not rule it out entirely[^5], and several members have since performed on stage together. As of November 2026, the channel has approximately 3.4 million subscribers and 336 long-form videos, with many Shorts edited from older long-form content still being uploaded regularly.[^6]

## One Camera, One Photographer, and Eventually Seven People

In September 2019, Zhanrong wrote a post about Shi Tou on IG. According to NOWnews, Zhanrong said that in the beginning, there was only himself, Shi Tou, and Yin-sheng. Regarding Shi Tou's position in the group, he used this phrase: "Because he was the only photographer who believed Zhanrong could do it back then." Shi Tou considered himself unsuitable for acting and preferred behind-the-scenes work over being in front of the lens; by that time, he had already become the "behind-the-scenes coordinator for the entire company."[^12] Under that post, Dong Zai commented: "Without Shi Tou, there is no TGOP (because no one would film us)," while Mu-xing left: "Shi Tou is my master; your apprentice loves you."[^12]

Dong Zai always remembers the phone call Zhanrui made to her. In a Facebook post following their hiatus from uploading videos, she wrote: "I still remember that phone call Zhanrui made to me: 'What do you want to do in the future?'" She compared this call to "Ash Ketchum catching a Pokémon (and I am Delbuca)."[^13] Nick was also not part of the original lineup; in the farewell video, a member recalls that Nick had not yet joined at that time.[^14]

Yin-sheng, Zhanrong, Zhanrui, Mu-xing, and Dong Zai are all alumni of the College of Dramatic Arts, with Zhanrui studying in the _Gezaixi_ (Taiwanese opera) department.[^15][^16] In June 2008, a group of people with formal dramatic training formed a collective, specializing in humorous works that combined current events, workplace themes, and music.[^10] It was only after Shi Tou joined that they gradually transitioned into a team primarily focused on web shorts.[^3]

On August 3, 2011, the channel uploaded its first original short film, [《I Want To Watch Yin Di》](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XK4TaVMw3jo), which was just over three minutes long. Three more were uploaded consecutively within the same month.[^7][^17] In their early years, they filmed situational sketches that incorporated current events, quickly releasing relevant shorts.[^3] The real names of the seven members, their roles in the group, and their whereabouts after the hiatus[^18]:

## Waiters, Instructors, Dentists: Acting Out the People Everyone Meets

On August 22, 2012, the channel uploaded 〈Classic Quotes of a Waiter〉, a video less than two minutes long. [^27] Starting with this video, the "classic quotes" format became another trademark for the group, with subject matter ranging from students and teachers to the service industry, all drawn from daily life. [^3] Over more than a decade, they have filmed series on instructors, visiting the dentist, office life, flying on planes, and convenience stores—one setting after another, each titled as "Classic Quotes of [Subject]."

Waiters, instructors, and dentists are all people that Taiwanese people inevitably interact with from childhood to adulthood. As soon as a title is released, an image pops into the viewers' minds; to bring those faces to life one by one, they rely on those six members taking turns on screen. [^3]

[^28][^29]

Alina Zheng acted in the 2015 film _Our Times_, and in 2017, she played the protagonist's best friend in the PTS mini-series _The Mage_. In the digital age, she was dubbed the "National Bestie" due to these types of roles. [^30][^3]

On August 30, 2016, they uploaded 《Super Lousy Cover Songs》, using another ensemble piece to celebrate a milestone. The official video description stated that the video was "to celebrate TGOP reaching 1 million followers on Facebook/YouTube," and the team "conceived this fake music TV commercial project based on the background of TGOP's '7th Graders'." [^31]

In December of the same year, when YouTube Taiwan's annual rankings were revealed, TGOP won two championships with 《Super Lousy Cover Songs》: Most Popular Video and Most Popular Creator. Reporters noted that when they received the award, they thanked YouTube for making ordinary people extraordinary. [^9] When YouTube held its Million Subscriber Hall of Fame in December 2017, there were a total of nine creator channels in Taiwan that had surpassed one million subscribers in the previous five years [^32]; TGOP was ahead of the curve that year, becoming Taiwan's first group of YouTubers to surpass two million subscribers. [^10][^4] The video [〈Professionals Gone Wild〉](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5ZQUuER667w), uploaded in April of that same year, had accumulated approximately 17.93 million views by November 2026, making it the most-viewed long-form video on their official channel. [^33]

## Two Videos a Month to Support Twenty Employees

One video was uploaded on the 15th and the 30th of every month[^3], a rhythm they maintained until the very end; of the more than thirty long-form videos released between September 2022 and February 2024, almost all were dated around the 15th or the end of the month.[^17] Once they became famous, these two days when audiences waited for new content effectively became a company's production schedule.

Around the third year of the team's formation, they faced a disbandment crisis. Yin-Sheng later attributed the cause to their lack of structure: "It was mainly because the company's operations weren't systematized. I hoped the company could grow with more employees, but at that time, there were no employees."[^15] In November 2016, they registered an entertainment company.[^36] Around 2018, they were signed by Pressplay and launched an official website to sell merchandise.[^3] In addition to the main channel, they had a secondary channel, "TGOP Private Life," which filmed the members' daily lives and handled commercial collaborations.[^37]

In a Facebook post following the hiatus, Dong Zai detailed the various roles she had performed within the group: "Point of contact, group manager, producer, booking agent, handling company administrative affairs, and HR." She also wrote about what she found most difficult: "As the number of company staff gradually increased, many things became harder to manage. We had to start implementing systematic regulations, which I felt was the hardest part."[^13]

From the less-than-two-minute 〈Classic Quotes of a Waiter〉 in 2012 to the nearly twenty-minute 〈Classic Quotes About Flying〉 in August 2023, the length of their "classic quotes" videos increased about tenfold.[^27][^38] Their final project, [〈Classic Quotes About Convenience Stores〉](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KXYg0ojLsnw), lasted over nineteen minutes and featured guest appearances by Hsieh Ying-hsuan, Chao-chiang, Soya, and Li Chuan.[^39] In May 2024, while being interviewed about the new CTS drama _The Courage Family_, Yin-Sheng said: "We only produce two videos a month; the filming costs are high, and the expenses are large."[^15]

Yin-Sheng had already written on social media at the end of 2017 regarding this heavy burden: "Even if we are close to bankruptcy, we still take video production very seriously!" A few days prior, she had written, "The reach is so low I can't afford to eat."[^40] That year, they had just become Taiwan's first group of YouTubers to surpass two million subscribers.

During that 2024 interview, she also mentioned that the seven members never took any revenue from the channel: "The seven of us never take YouTube revenue; the money is used to support 20 company employees. Any remaining money is given as year-end bonuses, and one person gets at most around NT$100,000 to NT$200,000."[^15] When asked how it felt to stop uploading, her first response was, "I feel very happy." Looking back on these years, she said: "The seven of us making it this far already gives us a great sense of achievement."[^15] As the company grew larger, an additional layer of relationship formed between the seven members: they were friends of over a decade who also became colleagues delivering videos together every month.

## Friends Turned Colleagues: Even Scrolling Instagram Feels Like Working

In 2020, in a video titled "Why Hasn't This Group Disbanded Yet?", Nick said, "Actually, when a group goes through 10 years, it's inevitable that these thoughts will arise." He used the metaphor of "flavorless coffee" to describe how, after being together for so long, if people feel they are no longer that same original cup of coffee, the idea of splitting up begins to surface. [^41]

Reporters summarized the examples he provided, including profit distribution, talent agencies, starting companies, differing ideologies, personal grudges, and sacrifice, while also noting that they eventually persevered. Nick's perspective in the video was: "The sacred thing about everyone is that even if you have thought about it, you didn't actually do it, because you know that when you truly do, sometimes you break a gear within the group." [^41]

In the group, Yin-Sheng was often the one making requests to outsiders. During an interview in 2024, she spoke about the misunderstandings this caused within the group: "Zhan Rui used to think I was very selfish; they wondered why I had to make demands of people outside. Later, they realized I was fighting for everyone; my demands were never for myself." [^15]

After a seven-month hiatus, in September 2024, Nick looked back on this relationship on the channel he runs with his wife, Ashly, saying that the members had become so close: "I know every single hair on these people; I don't need to explore their private lives because I already know everything. When I scroll through Instagram, I see their updates, so my life is just them. At that time, I felt so exhausted—I couldn't even 'clock out' at times like this." [^42]

Nick said that everyone being too busy and having no time to create better scripts was indeed one of the reasons, but with seven people having different personalities, friction is inevitable during long-term interaction. When asked if the hiatus was due to members not getting along, he first spoke about himself: "Everyone, including me, has flaws. For example, I don't interact with people much; I look very fierce on set, but everyone has their own issues." [^42] SET News reported that he stated the hiatus was not caused by team discord. [^43]

For Nick, this level of intimacy meant seamless chemistry in ensemble acting before the camera, but once placed within a company that requires monthly video deliveries, it turned into seeing each other's daily lives even after work hours. By August 2023, the seven members had been discussing whether or not to stop for several months. [^11]

## Announcing Hiatus Next Year, Please Media Do Not Report Disbandment

In an interview published in late September 2023, Zhan Rong recalled a gathering of the group: Nick suddenly asked, "What state do you want TGOP to be in right now?" After thinking for a moment, he replied, "I hope we can take a break!" The reporter noted that the entire room fell into silence. Zhan Rong said, "I was just voicing the scene everyone else wanted to avoid; I just had more courage than the others to say it out loud."[^44]

On August 29, 2023, Zhan Rong posted the results of their discussion on his Facebook fan page: "We, TGOP, have decided it is time to take a break. Next year, we will find an appropriate time to stop uploading to the 'This Group Of People' channel." In the same post, he requested that the media "do not use [headlines like] 'TGOP announces hiatus,' 'TGOP stops filming,' or 'TGOP announces disbandment,' because we live in an era where it is very easy to be misled by headlines."[^11]

In late December 2023, Mu-xing played seven different members in the "TGOP Breakup" segment of the Quánmín Grand Theatre stage play 《Comedy Party Doesn't Live Here》[^45], bringing the journey of TGOP from its founding to its hiatus to the Taipei Water Source Theater.[^46] She mentioned that the final video had already been filmed. A CNA reporter wrote that she wasn't originally that sentimental, until on the day of filming, she heard an employee say, "Today is the last time I call you 'Boss'," which made her want to cry.[^46] During the stage play run, she said: "After all, the TGOP platform supported every one of us. We stood there and were seen; if it suddenly disappears one day, I actually don't know where the next place will be. Of course, I still have to work hard on my own."[^47]

On January 9, 2024, a weekly magazine cited an anonymous source claiming that TGOP had already disbanded.[^47] Three days later, Dong Zai denied this on Facebook: "TGOP has not disbanded; the main channel is just preparing to stop uploading!!!"[^48] At the end of the month, the final project, 〈Classic Quotes About Convenience Stores〉, was uploaded to the channel. The official description read: "Thank you to our partners for your companionship. TGOP's 13 years of filming has come to an end!"[^39] In the farewell video released on February 15, Yin-sheng joked, "We never intended to disband over these 13 years; I think we are already the oddest of the odd."[^1] Shi Tou said in the video that after deciding to stop uploading, the team no longer needed such a large staff and studio, so the company would also be closing.[^1]

## Life Has Been Exhausted, and Everyone Is Tired: The Reasons Each Member Gave for Stopping Updates

Shi Tou is the behind-the-scenes coordinator for the company. When he appeared on the podcast 《Producers》 in January 2024, he gave the reason most closely related to operations: he said, "The reason we aren't continuing is simply that we've grown tired of it; there are individual reasons and group reasons—everyone is tired." He also mentioned that stopping updates was "related to money" and that the company had "encountered operational issues." Regarding the short-form video market, he described the team as being in a state of "wanting to charge forward but being afraid to do so." Even if they were to reunite someday, he said they "would not do it in the same way we do now."[^49][^50]

In the announcement, Zhan Rong wrote that the change was to "return to that pure and close 'heart'." According to reporters relaying his words in the video, if it were purely a commercial consideration, it would have been impossible to stop this channel.[^11][^1] In the same interview, he said: "A lot of things feel stuck at work; it feels quite different from when I first wanted to do this!" He also added: "Basically, we don't have much life left; our talent is almost exhausted. I have put my entire life into this."[^44] Over thirteen years, they produced more than 300 long-form videos, with one video released on the 15th and another on the 30th of every month.[^6][^3]

In that same interview, Zhan Rui said: "Because there are two videos to upload every month, much of the creation comes from inspiration drawn from life; much of the resonance in our lives comes from my previous work experiences." He also said, "I want to experience more different kinds of life so that I can create in the future." He feels that their works reflect life, and his current life is not quite as dramatic.[^44]

Yin-sheng spoke about the pressure of maintaining a company: "There is pressure from having a fixed number of videos every month; there is pressure when we don't land any projects; and there is pressure when view counts drop."[^15] When Mu Xing was interviewed at a theater, reporters relayed her saying that stopping updates was a consensus among the members—they had reached a certain age, and the overall environment had changed.[^46]

After the announcement to stop updates, some netizens noted that their performance had plummeted. Zhan Rong felt it was fine, while Zhan Rui responded directly: "The view counts really have dropped; so what if you think they've dropped? I see no need to deny it." He also said: "All these things are reasons. If you constantly focus on the drop in views and blame yourself, you won't be happy."[^44] The change in subscriber numbers was much smaller: at the end of 2021, _Taipei Times_ listed them at 3.44 million[^51], reports before they stopped updating said 3.57 million[^1][^47], and by November 2026, it was approximately 3.4 million.[^6]

## Concerts, Theater, and Taiwanese Songs: Seven Paths After the Hiatus

After the hiatus of video uploads began, Dong Zai wrote on Facebook: "Stopping updates doesn't mean it's the end, because every one of us is still out here navigating this world!" [^13] A year and a half later, on July 18, 2025, Zhan Rong and Zhan Rui held their first large-scale concert, 《限時動態 派對演唱會》 (Stories Party Concert), as "Zhan Rong & Zhan Rui K.R. Bros" at Legacy TERA. [^19][^20] According to SET News, Yin Sheng and Dong Zai were the first guest performers on stage, and the four of them performed a ridiculous cover together. [^19]

During an interview in March 2026, when asked if they would reunite, Zhan Rong said: "Life is a circle; it's uncertain where it will lead." He described his current state as "a very comfortable and relaxed stage." The reporter noted that he currently has no plans for another group filming session; when he originally founded This Group Of People, he hoped the members would one day grow individually and move into different stages of life. Seeing everyone's individual development now, he said, "It is a wonderful thing to see dreams come true." [^5]

Zhan Rong remarked: "Spending money to create works sometimes requires courage." Zhan Rui added that he will "continue to develop different forms of content, driven by a love for performance and creation." [^5] Both brothers graduated from the _gezaixi_ (Taiwanese opera) department of a drama academy.
