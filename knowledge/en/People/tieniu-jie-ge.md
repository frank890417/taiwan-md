In July 2013, Wu Chia-wei spoke to ETtoday's camera about a character he had played: "Everyone, Jie-ge was played by me; I'm not that bad in real life..."[^1]

He was twenty-five that year, working in publishing as his day job, but because he liked acting he occasionally took gigs filming online shorts and commercials[^1]. His office colleagues didn't recognize him as Jie-ge[^2], but the building manager downstairs did — he would call out "Jie-ge" whenever he saw him. The offers that came knocking were all the same: they wanted him to play Jie-ge again. Intent on pursuing an acting career, he turned every one of them down[^1].

What made the manager recognize him was a gender-equality educational film produced by the National Academy for Educational Research (NAER), titled 《如果早知道：男生也會被性侵》 (_If I Had Known Earlier: Boys Can Be Sexually Assaulted Too_). In the film, a boy is lured by a man to his home and sexually assaulted; the official synopsis states "This unit is adapted from a true case"[^3]. Wu Chia-wei played the perpetrator Jie-ge, while Huang Yao-wei played the boy "A-wei." The line "Jie-ge, don't" (also commonly written online as "傑哥不要") that later went viral on the internet is A-wei's dialogue in the film[^4][^5].

> **30-second overview:** The NAER's 2012 gender-equality teaching film _If I Had Known Earlier: Boys Can Be Sexually Assaulted Too_ saw the victim boy's line "Jie-ge, don't" (also written "傑哥不要") explode online in 2013, then spawn massive derivative works on China's bilibili. Wu Chia-wei (Tie-niu), who played the perpetrator, was stopped on the street and called "Jie-ge" until it frightened him; he later turned Jie-ge into work: mobile-game ads, a bilibili channel, and a stage musical. Ministry of Health and Welfare statistics show one in four child and adolescent sexual-assault victims is male. In 2023 he said he still hadn't figured out how to explain to his son that Jie-ge was a character Daddy once played.

## A Classroom Teaching Video That Hit Nearly 800,000 Views in July

In 2012, the Ministry of Education commissioned the National Academy for Educational Research to produce this gender equality advocacy film[^3][^6]. Recalling the process, Wu Chia-wei said: "At the time, Chan Jung said the Ministry of Education wanted to film a promotional short and was casting actors; all of us submitted our resumes together, and that's how I was chosen to play 'Jie-ge'."[^7]

Netizens later reposted it to YouTube; as early as May 2013 there was a copy whose description still read "National Educational Resources Center Gender Equality Educational Video"[^8]. A video originally made for the classroom had thus left the classroom.

On July 3, 2013, Coolloud published a reader submission calling it "Taiwan's 101st academic year gender equality education resource," with YouTube views already surpassing 530,000[^5]. On July 13, NOWnews reported views had surged to nearly 800,000, describing it as a "rare record" for a government agency promotional video, and saying it "achieved the promotional effect of negative marketing"[^6]. Online, people quickly criticized the video as too facetious, worrying it would reinforce stereotypes; NAER's response was that the script had been reviewed by experts and scholars, and that students were still discussing it online after watching — which precisely proved the advocacy succeeded[^2][^9]. Wu Chia-wei managed to turn down every invitation to play Jie-ge again; the ones he couldn't turn down were the people who recognized him on the street.

## The Actor Who Played Jie-ge Was Shouted at on the Street; the Actor Who Played A-wei Got Off at the Next Stop

After going viral, Wu Chia-wei described what it was like going out in public: "No matter where I went, everyone called me Jie-ge, Jie-ge. Once I even ran into some gangster types who rode up beside me on a scooter and shouted 'Jie-ge, don't!' I was a bit scared and didn't know how to respond. Because if I answered it would be impolite, and if I didn't answer it would also be impolite." [^7]

In a 2025 interview with ETtoday Game Cloud, he looked back and put it bluntly: "I actually really regretted it at the time; for a while I rejected the role of Jie-ge." [^10] Speaking of those years of being shouted at, he also said in 2023: "Being laughed at takes courage, and back then I couldn't bear everyone's stares." [^7]

Huang Yao-wei, who played A-wei, had originally had a chance at a TV drama role before taking this part, but was replaced without warning, which he described as "falling from heaven into the abyss." Seeing a notice online from the Ministry of Education recruiting actors for a promotional short film, he decided to give it a shot: "If I'm going to start over, this is an opportunity. Even if it means playing a sexually assaulted character, that's fine — I just want to play the role well." [^4]

When Huang Yao-wei was recognized, he sometimes chose to avoid it: "When people stared at me on the MRT, I'd quietly get off at the next stop." [^4] In 2017, at the Kaohsiung Film Festival, he told reporters that whether walking on the street or eating _lurou fan_ (braised pork rice), people would come up and ask, "Excuse me, are you the one who got sexually assaulted?" Friends' jokes were the same; he said: "Good friends joked until I was about to lose my temper!" The reporter also wrote that he initially felt uncomfortable, but looking back now finds it rather funny. [^11]

By the time of a 2023 Mirror Weekly interview, Huang Yao-wei could say: "I've now completely accepted it; it's just a part of my life." Talking about being teased by friends back then, he said his personality is lively and everyone likes to joke with him, "but sometimes the closer people are, the less they know where to draw the line, so there's still a bit of hurt inside." He also spoke up for the jokers: "A lot of people actually have no ill intent; they talk about it because they like this character." [^4]

Looking back at that promotional film, Huang Yao-wei's takeaway was: "What I realized is that when heaven closes a window, it opens a door for you. Don't underestimate any acting opportunity, even if it's just a promotional short film." [^4] In November 2023, on his own YouTube channel "A-wei Super Brave der," he uploaded a 10th-anniversary compilation video of the "Jie-ge, Don't" character biography [^12].

## WANGAME's Audience Also Calls Tie-niu "Jie-ge"

In 2013, Wu Chia-wei formed the YouTube group WANGAME with Hua-sen, Sheng Jie-shi, and Nick[^13]. When their videos went viral that July, ETtoday's reporting already referred to him as a WANGAME member[^14]. The group name takes the "wan" from _wan-tong_ (mischievous youth)[^15]. In February 2014, WANGAME uploaded "[Things Little Brats Do?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YvLjdnUUsLI)" to their channel, and by 2017, the channel had surpassed 600,000 subscribers[^15].

The name he used on the channel was Tie-niu, a nickname given to him by his kindergarten principal, but early viewers called him Jie-ge, or Lao Wu[^15]. The character name in the videos changed by one character and followed him into his own channel. On February 9, 2021, Wu Chia-wei announced on Facebook: "WANGAME draws a close today, and we also announce our dissolution. Thank you to the partners who fought alongside us before!"[^16] The channel uploaded a farewell video "[Goodbye Everyone! WANGAME Has Graduated!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8A1fBtqQHC0)" the same day. Counting from its formation, the group had lasted eight years.

At the time of dissolution, he considered returning to a stable workplace, even thinking about getting an excavator license, because he always thought driving big vehicles was cool. He said: "But the more I thought about it, I felt there were many things I wanted to film that I hadn't filmed, and many things I wanted to document that I hadn't completed." He agreed with his wife Ting-ting to give himself two more years to run the channel; if it didn't succeed, he would go find a job[^10].

## "You Must Accept This Name," He Heard from the Side

In a 2023 interview, Wu Chia-wei recounted a moment that changed his perspective: by chance, he ran into Machi Dage (Huang Li-cheng) and Machi Didi chatting. Machi Didi felt that "Machi Didi" was his childhood name and wanted to break free from it to be himself. Machi Dage told his younger brother: "You must accept this name. It doesn't matter how others see you, but this is your past. You must embrace it, and only then will you become better." [^7]

Wu Chia-wei listened from the side, and his takeaway was: "I should also calmly accept that I am Jie-ge." Being recognized had another side to it, too: "Once I went to a film set as an extra, and all the extras came over to take photos with me. Even the big-name actors grew curious when they saw this, and it actually brought us closer together." [^7]

On December 15, 2021, the Taiwan official channel for the mobile game _Free Fire_ uploaded a ranked-match advertisement featuring Wu Chia-wei and Huang Yao-wei performing as Jie-ge and A-wei. Game media outlet Mofang reported that this was the first time the two had jointly endorsed a product as these two characters, nine years after the original filming [^17]. In the video, Jie-ge and A-wei appear together in the same gunfight. Wu Chia-wei said: "I never imagined I'd get to perform in an action piece; my chuunibyou spirit was ignited" [^17]. Speaking of the commercial collaborations Jie-ge brought, he said: "I never expected this meme could be commercialized to this extent." [^7] The collaboration didn't stop at one ad: for the 2022 Lunar New Year, Free Fire had the two film a New Year's greeting video [^18], and for the game's sixth anniversary in 2023, they released a Jie-ge voice pack voiced by Wu Chia-wei [^19].

In 2023, he collaborated with toy design team Infinity Toy to release a 30-centimeter-tall vinyl figure named _Jie-ge La_ [^20]. His account on the Chinese video platform bilibili, named "Iron Bull Jie-ge Official Channel" with the bio "Operated by Jie-ge himself," had approximately 1.58 million followers as of November 2026 [^21]. The Jie-ge character had already gone viral on bilibili before he even created his account [^22].

## First Musical Script Packs in Over a Hundred Memes

Around 2017, the clip was heavily remixed and reworked by netizens on China's bilibili[^22]. In 2021, the original was re-uploaded to bilibili, sparking another wave of popularity; that June a fan-uploaded high-definition restoration also appeared on YouTube[^23][^24]. On October 1 that year, music YouTuber Xiao Wei Ba turned the footage into a two-plus-minute "Jie-ge Bu Yao (THE Musical)"[^25], writing in the description: "Hope the video gets popular enough to get the man himself to sing it."

![Xiao Wei Ba (Tseng Yu-hsiang) wearing a mask, seated outdoors, performing with a Yamaha digital piano and a tablet](/article-images/people/tiest-xiaoweiba-jazz-square-2021.webp)

_January 2021, Xiao Wei Ba performs at the Jazz Plaza in Taipei's Zhongshan Metro Mall. That autumn, he adapted the Jie-ge clips into an internet musical. Photo: Solomon203 / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0_

A month later, Xiao Wei Ba actually brought the two protagonists on board. This version — co-adapted with Wu Chi-ju, with vocals by Wu Chia-wei and Huang Yao-wei — was titled "Let Jie-ge and A-wei Themselves Sing 'Jie-ge Bu Yao the Musical'"[^26]. Uploaded November 4, it had amassed roughly 2.13 million views by November 2026[^27]. Speaking of the netizens who create Jie-ge works, Wu Chia-wei said, "Netizens are truly talented," adding: "Some very talented people struggle to get their work seen, but once they make a 'Jie-ge Bu Yao' meme, it's easily discovered."[^28]

Director Gao Tian-heng, who later helmed the stage version, commented on this vocal cover: "It proved the 'Jie-ge Bu Yao' meme has enduring popularity, and also let everyone see Wu Chia-wei and Huang Yao-wei's singing chops." Huang Yao-wei had actually wanted to adapt Jie-ge into a musical back in 2018, but practical constraints like funding held him back[^26]. By the time the stage production's credits rolled, Huang was listed for "story conception," and Xiao Wei Ba appeared among the composers[^29].

The stage production 《Hou You~ Jie-ge Bu Yao La!!》 (吼呦～杰哥不要啦！！), produced by Spotlight Creative Workshop, cost roughly NT$4 million, partly funded through crowdfunding. Before adapting, Gao Tian-heng secured authorization from NAER: "The government agency was happy to let us adapt it, provided the work doesn't offend public morals." He planned to bring the netizens' flame wars, insults, and comments onto the stage through visuals and props[^26].

Playwright Wang Mu-tian delivered a first draft so meme-dense the director had to pick and choose: "I boldly threw in internet slang and anime gags — over a hundred memes in the first draft alone, for the director to select."[^26] The show premiered in December 2023 at the New Taipei City Arts Center, moved to Taichung Zhongshan Hall in February 2024, and returned for four performances at the Taipei Performing Arts Center's Globe Playhouse in April 2025[^30][^31].

Both Wu Chia-wei and Huang Yao-wei took the stage, playing the characters they know so well[^32]. Wu's take on the production: "People of this generation should all find resonance in it."[^33] Performing Arts Review Platform critic Chiu Yi-feng caught the Taichung run and wrote that "the overall performance was more than steady," also noting the audience's roaring, delighted response as proof of the work's resonance[^34]. In March 2024, Wu also uploaded a documentary of the musical to the "IronbullTingTing" channel he runs with his wife Ting-ting[^35].

Gao Tian-heng says the show's angle is: "The stories behind memes often carry sadness, but once they become memes they give others joy — that contrast is an interesting entry point."[^26] He has one more hope for the audience: "This show is definitely hilarious and great fun, but I also hope that after watching, people will look back and ask: what are the stories behind those who gave you joy on the internet?"[^36] Behind this show lies a 2012 educational film made for students.

## One in Four Child and Adolescent Sexual Assault Victims Is a Boy

The National Academy for Educational Research put the very lesson this film aims to teach right into its title: _If I Had Known Earlier: Boys Can Be Sexually Assaulted Too_. The official synopsis opens with a reminder: "Do not overlook the hidden worry of boys being sexually assaulted," followed by "Many parents ignore this aspect of the crisis"[^3].

A 2021 Ministry of Health and Welfare press release states, "One in four child and adolescent sexual assault victims is male"[^37]. Another press release in 2025 notes that nearly 60% of sexual assault victims are children and adolescents, and adds that "Many men choose silence due to gender stereotypes, fear of being questioned or ridiculed, leading to many cases going unreported"[^38].

On June 25, 2013, when ETtoday reported on this film, the journalist wrote, "Turns out sexual assault does not discriminate by gender"[^39]. By mid-July, with the meme at its peak, ETtoday consulted a veteran detective about whether such things happen in reality; the detective confirmed similar cases exist, citing another past case[^40].

Li Chia-lin, then a doctoral student in the Institute of Social and Cultural Studies at National Chiao Tung University, prefaced a contributed piece on Coolloud by stating no intention to join netizens' ridicule nor to underestimate the severity of sexual assault, then criticized the film's own narrative: the framework and moral perspective of such videos mean the manpower and funding the state invested fail to achieve their educational purpose, "instead deepening the stereotype that 'older gay men lure and assault boys'"[^5]. The piece concluded: "The author sincerely hopes that a single line — 'Jie-ge, don't' — sparks not just public kuso or jokes, but more meaningful change, such as profound government self-reflection…"[^5]

As of November 2026, this film on iLearn had accumulated 64,811 views, while the other four gender-equality educational films NAER produced the same year ranged between 8,000 and 17,000 views[^3][^41][^42][^43][^44]. Two netizen-uploaded copies on YouTube combined for approximately 6.42 million views[^8][^24].

In late 2021, Huang Yi-ching wrote in a Newtalk reader submission that this film "brought the issue that males can also be sexual assault victims into the public eye," then noted that due to derivative works, "Those who actually focus on the warning that males can be sexually assaulted are a minority." The submission quoted a YouTube comment under the 2021 restored version as an example: "Girl sexually assaulted — everyone: so sad qwq protect yourself; Boy sexually assaulted — everyone: Jie-ge, don't"[^23].

Huang contrasted it with NAER's _Superstition? Fortune-Changing?: Sexual Assault in the Name of Fake Religion_ produced the same year: also about sexual assault prevention, yet that film "was far less used as a meme." The submission worried: "Perhaps everyone just treats 'Jie-ge, don't' as a common meme, without realizing the implied social attitude of not taking male sexual assault seriously, or even conveying the mistaken notion that 'this is no big deal.'"[^23]

> 📝 **Curator's Note**
> In 2013, NAER took students still discussing the film online as proof of promotional success. That same month, Li Chia-lin criticized the script itself. In 2021, Huang Yi-ching looked at the comment section. All three observed a different face of this film: how many people are talking, how the film was made, what tone the talkers use. Whether viewers actually remembered "boys can be sexually assaulted too" — none of the three produced a measured answer.

This film remains on the National Academy for Educational Research's [iLearn](https://stv.naer.edu.tw/watch/289393) site, marked with a Creative Commons license. The video is divided into five segments, starting with parents chatting about social news while bringing kids to a park, and ending with a segment on "What Constitutes Sexual Assault and How to Face It"[^3].

## How to Tell His Son That Jie-ge Was a Role Dad Played

In 2013, he was able to say to ETtoday's camera, "Jie-ge is me acting." Ten years later, having become a father, in November 2023 he told Mirror Media: "This (Jie-ge) is a role Dad once played. I think he'll definitely get teased about it at school, but I haven't figured out how to explain it to my son yet — that's my biggest challenge right now."[^7]
