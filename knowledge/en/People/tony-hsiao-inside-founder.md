> **30-Second Overview:** Hsiao Shang-nung (online handle fOx) is the co-founder of INSIDE Hard-Stuffed Internet Trend Observation and iCook (愛料理). In 2009, he and five friends launched one of Taiwan's earliest opinion-driven tech media outlets; in 2011, they launched Taiwan's largest recipe platform. Both companies were sold to the same buyer—TNL Media Group (The News Lens Media Group)—in 2018 and 2022 respectively. He now serves as an internet observer, active on major podcasts and media programs, and is one of the most frequently invited voices to discuss trends in Taiwan's tech circle.

## The Collaborative Blog of 2009

On November 26, 2009—the year Happy Farm (開心農場) exploded in popularity on Facebook in Taiwan—six internet professionals from varied backgrounds decided to write a blog together. [^inside-wiki]

They named it "INSIDE Hard-Stuffed Internet Trend Observation." The six co-founders were: Hsiao Shang-nung, Li Chih-wei (Richard), Tao Yun-chih (Sting), Lin Yi-ju (Lawrence), Wang Yu-che, and Li Chuan-hsing. No office, no investors—just a collaborative writing tool and a shared conviction: **they didn't want to merely translate foreign tech news; articles had to have their own opinions and perspectives.** [^inside-article]

Hsiao Shang-nung (fOx) was one of them.

In October 2011, INSIDE was formally incorporated. Taiwan gained a tech media outlet grounded in a commentary perspective. [^inside-wiki]

> **📝 Curator's Note**
> Before 2009, most tech news in Taiwan was directly translated from foreign outlets. INSIDE's emergence was one of the few attempts at the time to expose Taiwanese readers to 'opinionated tech analysis'—something that seems obvious today, but was not back then.

## Game PM Background, Observer's Instinct

Hsiao Shang-nung's career starting point, like many early Taiwanese internet people, began in the game industry.

He first served as a game product manager at Songgang Technology, handling multiple Blizzard titles including StarCraft: Brood War (星海爭霸：怒火燎原). Later he moved to Yam (蕃薯藤), transitioning from games to an internet portal site, overseeing multiple channels including games, stock market finance, and fortune-telling/astrology. After that, he joined NHN Taiwan to manage dictionary services. [^inside-article]

This trajectory gave him a specific working style: cross-domain operation, but always observing "where people gather on the internet."

In INSIDE's 10th-anniversary retrospective, he said: "No need to deliberately curry favor or make enemies; life has its ups and downs, just do your own thing well. Most people can collaborate." [^inside-article] This reads like life philosophy, but in the context of his career, it also sounds like the insight of someone who has long been in the industry and navigated every transition without awkwardness.

## Meanwhile, He Was Building a Recipe Website

During the same period INSIDE was founded, Hsiao Shang-nung, Lin Yi-ju (Lawrence), and Li Chih-wei (Richard) took notice of Japan's Cookpad.

"Cookpad IPO'd in 2008, with annual revenue of NT$2 billion; maybe we could try too." [^gvm][^cookpad-correction]

This remark later became the starting point of iCook (愛料理).

In August 2010, the three founded Baoli Shi Company (寶利拾公司). In November 2011, iCook officially launched. [^gvm] The division of labor was clear: Lin Yi-ju and Li Chih-wei handled programming development, while Hsiao Shang-nung took charge of all non-technical aspects—finance, business, tax, and more. [^gvm]

He described the feeling of that period: "Since starting up, we've never worried about money." [^gvm]

> **✦** "Since starting up, we've never worried about money." — Hsiao Shang-nung (from a _Global Views Monthly_ interview)

This statement is considered unusual in Taiwan's startup circle. From 2011 into the 2020s, iCook became Taiwan's largest recipe platform—two people leveraging the technical foundation built during the NHN era, plus Hsiao Shang-nung managing the business side well, produced a solid scale.

## The Same Buyer, Bought Twice

| 2009 → 2018                                      | 2011 → 2022                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------- |
| INSIDE from blog to acquisition by The News Lens | iCook from launch to acquisition by The News Lens |

In January 2018, INSIDE Hard-Stuffed Internet Trend Observation was acquired by TNL Media Group (The News Lens Media Group). This was Taiwan's first recorded formal acquisition of an online media outlet. [^buzzorange]

In September 2022, TNL Media Group announced the acquisition of iCook. [^bnext]

Two companies, the same buyer, four years apart. Both Hsiao Shang-nung and Li Chih-wei participated in both transactions. At the time of the second sale, Hsiao wrote: "This is the second time, after more than four years, that we've merged a company we founded from scratch with another company." [^inside-icook]

> **📝 Curator's Note**
> Between 2018 and 2022, the exit path for Taiwan's independent online media and platforms accelerated toward 'integration into larger groups.' The two sales of INSIDE and iCook can be read as personal choices, or as the collective structural outcome of a generation of Taiwanese internet entrepreneurship—independent operational scale becoming increasingly unsustainable under advertising and subscription competition, only larger ecosystems can go further.

## Lizhang Bo (Village Chief)

The moniker "Lizhang Bo" (Village Chief) has circulated in Taiwan's internet circle for many years.

It describes fOx's role in the industry: a network so broad that "everyone knows him, and he knows everyone." When you need to meet someone or need an introduction, he might just be able to help. This role is uncommon in the internet circle—technical background but not an engineer, media perspective but not a pure journalist, business operational sense but also observing the industry panorama.

The Village Chief's job is connecting people. Hsiao Shang-nung's job is the same.

This network partly explains why he could operate INSIDE and iCook simultaneously, and remain active as an observer after the sales—in other words, "Taiwan's internet circle knows him" did not disappear because his companies were acquired.

## Facing AI: The One Who Says "Don't Panic"

In May 2023, half a year after ChatGPT's launch, iCook rolled out an AI recipe assistance feature. [^wazaiii] Around the same time, Hsiao Shang-nung used AI tools to build a business card scanning app, got it listed, and earns a stable NT$10,000+ per month from it. [^tnl]

He says: "I'm a tech optimist; of course I believe technology makes our lives more affluent and convenient." [^wazaiii]

He also says: "The control knobs are entirely out of our hands, so worrying is useless; I also tend not to worry." [^wazaiii]

This is his consistent stance toward every wave of technology: observe, experiment, then tell everyone not to panic too much. From the cryptocurrency era to the AI era, it's the same stance.

He currently appears regularly on BCC Pop Network's "Lan Xuan Time" "Reading Trends" segment, hosts the podcast "Seqa Side Chat," and continues to provide regular trend translations for Taiwan's tech circle.

Hsiao Shang-nung remains. The observation continues. He has turned "continuing to observe" itself into the next thing.
