> **30-Second Overview:** Tsai Chin-an (channel name "Tsai Hei-pi," early handle "Hei-pi") was born in 2002. In high school, feeling that "no one was doing transportation content," he began making videos. His first work was _10 Things You Didn't Know About the Taipei Metro_. Twelve years later, he stood on the stage of the 26th Golden Road Award—hailed as the "Oscars of Transportation"—and took home the Special Contribution Award. The core tension of this story lies in: in early 2025 he announced a hiatus, and the channel went silent for half a year; everyone thought he would disappear. Instead, his post-return "Taiwan 360" local series erupted with even deeper force. The Wuqiu feature hit the channel's homepage, views surpassing 100,000. A boy who grew up on MRT station names ultimately turned his lens toward the most forgotten corners of the map. This article is not about an influencer's rise, but about how a creator found his non-negotiable reason to keep going in the contradiction of "post more, no one watches; stop, and people wait."

## Why Read About This Person

In Taiwan's YouTube knowledge ecosystem, a few numbers are worth pausing for. One is "100": Tsai Chin-an spent roughly six years accumulating one hundred transportation infrastructure videos, each excavated page by page from government project proposals, engineering records, and historical-geographical materials. This content production cycle operates on a completely different time unit from an unboxing video or mukbang. Another is "0": from January to October 2025, he went nine months without posting—in algorithmic logic, a channel with zero uploads for nine months is essentially a death sentence. The third is "30": upon returning, he aimed his first local feature at Wuqiu, a place with a township population of only about thirty people[^9]. One hundred videos, nine months, thirty people—these three numbers happen to be the three keys to understanding this creator: how he makes videos, why he stopped, and why he came back.

There's an even quieter number hidden behind: as of December 2025, his channel had 189 videos, 56.61 million total views, and 260,000 subscribers[^14]; by July 2026, his videos were incorporated into high school geography textbooks[^15]. From YouTube to textbooks, he walked this road for thirteen years. This article traces back along that path: first returning to the little boy in the MRT station asking "where does this line go?", then watching how he turned that question into an entire Taiwan transportation knowledge base over twelve years.

## The Boy Who Memorized Station Names

On May 28, 2002, Tsai Chin-an was born[^1]. His childhood was much like many Taiwan kids—nothing special, except how he rode vehicles. Other kids on the MRT looked at phones or chatted with classmates; he looked out the window. In later interviews he described the origin of this habit: as a child who loved riding vehicles, loved the MRT, he liked watching the scenery outside, imagining the world, and would wonder in MRT stations "how big is this station really? And where does this line go?"[^2]

This curiosity became an almost savant-like skill: he could recite every station name[^3]. If you've ever ridden once with a railfan, you know how rare this is among Taiwan kids. Most memorize video game weapon codes; he memorized every station on the Wenhu Line from Nangang Exhibition Center to Taipei Zoo.

![Taipei MRT Tamsui Line platform, train heading to Beitou arriving, platform screen doors reflecting passengers inside car](/article-images/people/mrt-tamsui-line-train-2017.webp)

_Taipei MRT Tamsui Line. Tsai Chin-an's creative career began on a platform just like this. Photo: eugene_o, 2017-10-23, CC BY 2.0_

His creative career didn't start with transportation. In 2013, at age eleven, he launched his YouTube channel, initially as a Minecraft map creator, making mini-game maps like "Escape from Taiwan Junior High" and "Escape from Shin Kong Mitsukoshi," garnering tens to hundreds of thousands of views in the player community[^1]. During the same period, the channel also mixed knowledge, livestream, and entertainment videos. In 2016, the channel's cumulative views were only 10,000; in 2018, 2,500 subscribers, 250,000 total views[^1]. Placed in today's creator context, this equals nothing happening.

The real trajectory shift came in high school. He himself stated the reason for the pivot at the Golden Road Award: "In high school I felt no one was doing transportation topics, and I felt my knowledge exceeded my peers', so I started making videos. The first video topic was ten Taipei MRT trivia facts."[^3] A report by National Education Radio gave more specific coordinates: this transportation-themed debut was born in 2020[^13]. Thereafter the scope expanded from railways and MRT to highways, Taiwan geography and history; most data he searched himself, consulting industry experts when writing scripts[^3].

> **📝 Curator's Note:** Note the timing of this pivot. Around 2019–2020, Taiwan YouTube was saturated with unboxing, challenges, and mukbang; knowledge-based transportation content was a genuine vacuum. He didn't pick a hot track—he picked a station where no one stood—just like the lines he later loved to discuss: the terminus of this route hadn't been planned by anyone yet.

## "Railway Bar" and One Hundred Videos

"Railway Bar" (鐵道吧) is his best-known series, running steadily from 2020. Representative works include _Will National Freeway 6 Pierce the Central Mountain Range Straight to Hualien?_ (over 3.1 million views), the Xueshan Tunnel at 1.7 million, "Taiwan's Most Useless HSR Station: Tainan Station" at 1.6 million, and the Taoyuan Airport MRT at 1.8 million[^4].

![View from National Freeway 6 window, Central Mountain Range ridges disappearing into clouds and mist, green guardrails extending along mountainside](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/94/NationalFreeway6ofTaiwan20220326113440.jpg)

_National Freeway 6 along the route, Central Mountain Range shrouded in clouds and mist. Tsai Hei-pi's first video to break three million views covers this highway that never quite reached Hualien. Photo: OnionBulb, 2022-03-26, CC BY-SA 4.0_

![Taoyuan Airport MRT EMU traveling on elevated curve in Dayuan, blue-white body fully in frame under clear sky](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/14/Taoyuan_MRT_train_approaching_Dayuan_February_2026_1.jpg)

_Taoyuan Airport MRT EMU entering the curve before Dayuan Station. In his most popular videos, half discuss "infrastructure that took over a decade to complete." Photo: 4300streetcar, 2026-02, CC BY 4.0_

These numbers are top-tier among Taiwan's knowledge creators, but what's more worth examining is the topical orientation: his hit videos are almost all "projects Taiwan almost built but didn't": National Freeway 6 piercing the mountains, National Freeway 9 from Changhua to Kaohsiung, over 3,500 kilometers of planned national freeways[^4]. In other words, what he sells best is the "unfinished" in Taiwan's transportation history.

Another category is "built after a very long time": the Taoyuan Airport MRT took twenty-four years from planning to opening, one of the longest construction periods in Taiwan MRT history, and he used 1.8 million views to re-tell this "route two generations waited for" to a younger generation. This is precisely the most unique layer in his content: the timeline of transportation construction placed side-by-side with Taiwan's social memory history; viewers often finish watching not just understanding engineering, but a public history of "why we waited this long."

The railfan circle gave him a precise niche: viewers described "his early topics were almost all content you can't find on Wikipedia, lots of stuff only in project proposals"[^5]. And by late 2025 statistics laid out the scale of this niche: 189 videos, 56.61 million total views, averaging 300,000 per video—several orders of magnitude higher than most Taiwan small-to-medium media's single articles[^14]. In May 2021, the Chinese Institute of Transportation's "Transportation Society Lecture" invited him to speak, the introduction reading "Transportation Video Expert Tsai Chin-an (Hei-pi)"[^6]. A nineteen-year-old student, called an expert by Taiwan's most senior transportation society.

In the two years post-return, the channel's territory visibly expanded. Beyond the original railway/MRT-core "Railway Bar" and highway-core "Transportation Bar" (交通吧), he successively pushed "Taiwan 360" (臺灣三六零) from outlying islands to all counties and cities nationwide, and launched a pure history series "Hei-pi Tells Defunct Cities" (黑皮說故市), narrating a city's history through its defunct markets—currently over twenty episodes[^4]. Recent works include the continuing Wuqiu follow-up _Townships "Erased" from the Map?_ (101,000 views), _Taiwan Almost Had National Freeway 32? Over 3,500 km of Freeway Plans_ (118,000), and _Sanying Line Opening Unboxing_ (87,000)[^4]. Topics still maintain the two extremes of "unfinished" and "most remote."

He also began pushing this Taiwan narrative to the English-speaking world. Mid-2025, the channel released an English version _Introduction to Taiwan: The Formosa You've Never Discovered_, opening with the ancient name "Formosa," introducing the Taiwan face most international audiences overlook, garnering ~150,000 views—for a pure Taiwan-local-topic English video, this number proves his narrative framework holds up outside the Sinosphere[^4]. A creator who started by memorizing Taipei MRT station names is now translating "Taiwan's story" into a version the world can read.

But a creator's daily life has no awards stage. He once wrote on IG: "Many know me because of MRT, highways, railways, and often call me 'Transportation YouTuber' directly; but for me, what I'm truly fascinated by has never been the transportation vehicles themselves. What I want to know is, why would a highway be built _here_?"[^7]

His production process was fully documented in a late-2025 National Education Radio interview: after gathering materials and finishing scripts, "I ask these experts to review, to improve accuracy"[^13]. One person handles data search, map production, scripting, and editing[^5], plus a layer of professional gatekeeping. This is exactly the prototype of the Golden Road Award citation: the Railway Bureau of the MOTC thanked him for "introducing major government transportation projects in a relaxed, easy-to-understand way, narrowing the distance between government agencies and the public, and opening a chance for mutual understanding among all parties during transportation construction's dark period"[^3].

## Hiatus, Then Radio Silence

In January 2025, Tsai Chin-an announced a hiatus on his channel[^8].

For a knowledge channel, hiatus is a synonym for death sentence. Taiwan transportation-geography creators persisting is hard enough; peers "Railway Affairs Office" (鐵道事務所) and "Penguin Transportation Notes" (企鵝交通手札) also successively went on hiatus or fell into slumps[^5]. During that half-year, the most common comments on social media were "Knowledge YT Great Collapse?" "He disappeared half a year—what's he actually doing?"[^8].

He didn't explain for long. In October 2025, on the channel's twelfth anniversary, he returned, bringing a full restart plan: new name "Tsai Hei-pi" (original "Hei-pi" had too much name collision, search results confused with others), new email, new channel positioning, channel membership, LINE stickers, and a brand-new series: "Taiwan 360," aiming to cover the historical-geographical knowledge of every township, county/city, and island in Taiwan one by one[^8]. He hadn't actually vanished during the hiatus. The hiatus video _Knowledge YT Great Collapse? Tsai Hei-pi Disappeared Half a Year Turns Out Doing This..._ honestly displayed his life on the timeline: school courses, editing class, Korean class, collaborative channel projects with classmates—turns out those nine months, he used "rest" to reorganize his entire creation system[^16].

The name change reflects a creator's identity maturation: "Hei-pi" is a common gaming-circle nickname with high collision; "Tsai Hei-pi" locks his surname and nickname together, search results almost never collide. His later social media bio also changed to "I'm Tsai Hei-pi!" From "a nickname" to "a name," this step took him twelve years.

This restart's topics form a subtle echo with that boy from twelve years ago. Twelve years ago, he stood in an MRT station asking "where does this line go?"; twelve years later, he pointed his lens at the most marginal island on Taiwan's map—Wuqiu.

## Forgotten National Territory: Wuqiu and the Ocean Perspective

![Wuqiu Century Lighthouse standing on Daqiu Island hillside, camouflaged military facilities surrounding, foreground Marine Corps emblem sculpture and "Forever Protect Wuqiu" sign](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/96/%E7%83%8F%E5%9D%B5%E7%99%BE%E5%B9%B4%E7%87%88%E5%A1%941.jpg)

_Wuqiu Daqiu Island Century Lighthouse. Tsai Chin-an's outlying island feature pushed the lens into an island even most Taiwan people rarely set foot on. Photo: Kechinyuan, CC BY-SA 4.0_

_The Mysterious Restricted Zone at the Center of Kinma: Wuqiu_ hit the channel homepage, views exceeding 100,000[^4]. Wuqiu is Kinmen County's most remote township, population down to ~30, returning home requires jumping from a boat, no going out after dark—this "island among islands" has nearly vanished from ordinary Taiwan people's mental maps[^9]. He subsequently continued pushing "Taiwan 360" further: unlocking all of Taiwan's hundred-plus outlying islands, southernmost to northernmost spanning 1,800 kilometers[^4].

The Wuqiu feature's completion is simultaneously a transcript of his formal training. At the time, Tsai Chin-an was a student in Shih Hsin University's Department of Radio, Television and Film, "Broadcasting and Sound Design Group"[^14]. He collaborated with classmate Yang Yao-po to produce the program _Forgotten National Territory_, exploring Wuqiu's history and cultural preservation, winning the 22nd Golden Bell Awards (金聲獎) Social Concern Program Award from National Education Radio[^14]. And as early as the channel's tenth anniversary, his video _Zhongli Taoyuan, One Side One Country? Why Do Zhongli People Hate Taoyuan So Much! The Two-City Feud Has Lasted a Hundred Years!?_ won the 5th Golden Clock Awards (走鐘獎) "Golden Brain Award"[^14]. From Golden Clock to Golden Bell to Golden Road, Taiwan's digital content circle's three trophies sequentially landed in the hands of the same 23-year-old creator.

In his Golden Road Award acceptance speech, he added another important footnote: the award date, December 12, 2025, happened to be his channel's 12th anniversary: "To receive such a meaningful award on this day is a very wonderful fate for me"[^17]. In the same video, he also revealed a little-known promotion history: early in Taiwan's LRT construction, society generally didn't understand it, and he worked with Kaohsiung City on serious public promotion. "In my mind appeared an image of a grandma holding a child's hand, riding in the green tunnel of the Kaohsiung LRT—that image was very warm, and very moving"[^17]. He also mentioned his greatest pride as a knowledge communicator wasn't view counts, but "many viewers decided to study transportation, urban planning, geography-related majors because of my videos, even entering government service"[^17]. A YouTuber's influence is extending from screen into classrooms and the civil service.

In the Golden Road Award interview, he articulated the deepest foundation of his decade of creation: "From the continental empire perspective, Taiwan is on the periphery; but from the ocean perspective, Taiwan is the navigator of the ocean; therefore we must cherish this land, stop and listen to this land's stories."[^3]

This sentence deserves to be paused for. A creator of transportation construction videos ultimately arrived at the intersection of geopolitics and national territory identity: transportation construction is never just engineering—it's the question of "does this land deserve to be treated well?" The Railway Bureau's citation has a line easily overlooked: "opened a chance for mutual understanding among all parties during transportation construction's dark period"[^3]. His videos let ordinary people understand why a highway costs tens of billions and takes decades[^13], so one less "you don't understand" wall stands between government agencies and the public.

> **✦** "Because you never know what people you'll meet, what wonderful scenery you'll see, after taking that first step." —Tsai Chin-an[^2]

## Controversies Not Hidden Away

This article doesn't intend to write him as a perfect character. The railfan community has clear criticisms: some point out his "abnormal obsession" with Xizhi's transportation planning, suspecting he lives in Xizhi[^5]; others dug up controversial videos before his hiatus—two "fantasy route" proposals (Daxi Ke Station, Zhonghe Cross Line) questioned for feasibility, later taken down by him, with viewers bluntly stating "he presented fantasy routes as definitely getting built"[^5].

His response to criticism is also recorded in the award interview: "Very grateful for some critical comments, letting him know how he can be better, examine shortcomings and improve further."[^3]

> **📝 Curator's Note:** Here is a standard specimen of creator growth. Taking down problematic videos, acknowledging insufficiency, turning criticism into fuel for iteration—then answering two years later with the Golden Road Award and deeper topics (Wuqiu, Taiwan 360). In the traffic era, the most valuable credibility isn't "never making mistakes," it's "how you catch them when you do."

## After 260,000 Subscribers

As of December 2025, his channel had 189 videos, 56.61 million total views, subscribers exceeding 260,000[^14], channel stats showing 265K and still growing[^4]. His videos were even incorporated into high school geography textbooks. The publisher sent him the book; in late July 2026 he posted: "Opened it, and inside saw my own video; the moment I saw it, the feeling was very strange."[^10] He also called himself "I'm Tsai Hei-pi!" in the post, introducing this new identity to readers in a first-tone voice[^15]. In February 2026, "Railway Bar" sixth anniversary, he announced restarting the entire railway and transportation series[^11], and in the acceptance speech video "shouted out" to the Minister of Transportation and various bureaus: "In the future if there are projects hoping to be seen by more people... like Terminal 3, or the Danjiang Bridge, if you have any cases wanting to collaborate with me, welcome to reach out! I'd be very happy to help you unbox them~"[^17]. Behind the half-joking tone is his serious declaration of taking "bridge between government agencies and the public" as his self-expectation[^17]. And the timing of his shout-out was precise. The Danjiang Bridge opened in May 2026.

![Danjiang Bridge opening day: public walking on bridge, white single-tower asymmetric cable-stayed structure fully in frame](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/7b/Danjiang_Bridge_open_day_20260503_%28IMG_9559%29.jpg)

_May 3, 2026, Danjiang Bridge opening day, thousands of citizens walked onto this bridge before opening, the single-tower asymmetric cable-stayed structure fully unfolding at the Tamsui River estuary._ Photo: [Bigmorr](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/User:Bigmorr) / [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/)

If one bridge must summarize Taiwan transportation engineering's character, Danjiang Bridge might be the best example: proposed in 1980, included in the "Six-Year National Development Plan" in 1992, 2015 Zaha Hadid (札哈．哈蒂) team won international competition—"Silent Dancer" streamlined form, her last architectural design legacy before passing[^19]. Main bridge 2017–2018 failed tender seven consecutive times due to excessive technical threshold, work stopped over two years; 2019 officially broke ground; September 2025 bridge closure, total budget追加 to NT$23 billion[^19]. From April 2026, MOTC Highway Bureau held opening series events (road run, cycling, starry concert, bridge picnic), letting public walk on bridge before opening[^20], officially opened May 12, Tamsui and Bali shortened by 15 km drive, peak hours save ~25 minutes[^20]. World's longest single-tower asymmetric cable-stayed bridge (main span 450 m), design life 120 years[^19]. This is exactly the kind of construction Tsai Hei-pi's lens loves most: brewed for half a century, worthy of complete recording as public works. When he shouted "help you unbox," this bridge was just in opening season; and he, like every bridge he's filmed, stands between the bridge's history and the public's curiosity.

May 2026 brought another milestone: graduation. He posted on IG: "From 2020 enrollment to 2026, I studied a full 7 years of university (studying med school XD?) This long journey finally reaches the terminus, I'm graduating!!"[^18] The same post hid a very representative story: he never took a photo with his 100,000-subscriber play button: "When I received the button, I immediately put it deep in my room, then walked to my computer and kept editing," until seven years later, "thanks to AI's help, I got my first photo with this button"[^18]. Mid-May, he held graduation showcase at Songshan Cultural and Creative Park, hand-drew limited postcards for visitors, and admitted "administrative processes all handled by me alone, relying on one person's ability, really can't organize a formal meet-and-greet"[^18]. One person handling everything, from script to admin—this is the bedrock truth of the Tsai Hei-pi channel.[^18]

Worth noting is the channel's business model. Upon his October 2025 return, he simultaneously launched channel membership "Team Tsai," putting behind-the-scenes and bonus footage into member-only content, including _Forgotten National Territory_ Golden Bell Award behind-the-scenes—the award-winning video itself also came from member-area early reveal[^8]. This "public content builds trust, member content deepens connection" dual-track structure is his realistic choice to sustain the channel under one-person-team constraints: not relying on high-frequency updates to game the algorithm, but trading each video's depth for audience's long-term trust and member conversion.

From a boy memorizing MRT station names, to a 23-year-old creator on the "Oscars of Transportation" stage, to "textbook-level" influence with videos written into high school geography texts, Tsai Chin-an's story leaves Taiwan's digital creation circle a concrete demonstration: **deep topics don't die in the algorithm—they just need a longer compounding period**. On his award day he said a line that sounds like a summary: "One of my childhood wishes was to become a knowledge enlightener who could lead everyone to see this world; never thought I'd fulfill it in my twenties."[^2]

Next time you ride the MRT, take a look at the station name signs. For Tsai Hei-pi, those aren't just station signs—they're the starting point of his entire creative life.
