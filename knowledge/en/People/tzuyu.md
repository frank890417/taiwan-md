> **30-Second Overview:** Tzuyu Chou (周子瑜), born in Tainan in 1999, left home at age 13 to become a JYP trainee. Forty-seven days after her debut, she held up a Republic of China flag on a Korean variety show; those 8 seconds of footage were screenshotted by Chinese netizens. Two months later, a 90-second apology video was released on the eve of Taiwan's presidential election. Scholars estimate this video influenced the decisions of 1.34 million young voters[^8]. Since then, she has not uttered a single word regarding those 90 seconds; she simply continued dancing, releasing albums, and serving as a lead dancer and the _maknae_ (youngest member) of TWICE. Ten years later, she finally returned to Kaohsiung, reading a handwritten letter to 55,000 people: "I didn't leave because I was obedient or submissive, but because I truly wanted it and desired it." The entire audience wept with her.

At 10:00 PM on January 15, 2016, a 16-year-old girl from Tainan sat in a JYP Entertainment studio in South Korea, wearing a black turtleneck and holding a script. Looking into the camera, she spoke for 90 seconds with a trembling voice and hollow eyes: "There is only one China, and the two sides of the Strait are one. I have always been proud to be Chinese."[^3]

The next day was election day for the Taiwan presidential election. Tsai Ing-wen was elected as the first female president with 6.89 million votes[^7].

The _South China Morning Post_ later cited polling figures: those 90 seconds influenced the voting decisions of **1.34 million** young voters[^8]. Scholar Fan Shih-ping estimated that this "apology" contributed one to two percentage points to Tsai Ing-wen's margin of victory[^9].

Her name is Tzuyu Chou. Since that night, she has not said a single word about those 90 seconds.

But she did not disappear. Over the next decade, she continued to stand at the center of K-pop's brightest stages—dancing to 〈Cheer Up〉, 〈TT〉, and 〈LIKEY〉 with TWICE, being voted one of the most beautiful faces in the world, surpassing 10 million Instagram followers, releasing a solo EP, and earning a Master's degree from a European university. Every time she appears, she offers no explanation, yet every appearance reminds people of those 90 seconds.

Until ten years later, when she finally returned to Kaohsiung and brought out another handwritten script. This time, the content was entirely different.

## The Dancing Girl from Fuxing Elementary School

Tzuyu is a Tainan native, born in 1999. Her father, Chou Yi-cheng (周羿呈), has operated in the cosmetics industry for over 20 years, and her mother, Huang Yen-ling (黃燕玲), is the CEO of the "Sogelle" medical aesthetics clinic, with locations in Taichung, Kaohsiung, and Tainan[^1]. Since childhood, she was soft-spoken and loved singing and dancing. She attended [Fuxing Elementary School](https://news.ltn.com.tw/news/life/breakingnews/3736106) in the East District of Tainan City. During the school's 30th-anniversary celebration in 2024, while introducing distinguished alumni, the school unexpectedly revealed something she had never mentioned herself[^18].

In 2012, she participated in a performance at the Meis Performing Arts Center. A grainy video of her performance caught the eye of a JYP Entertainment talent scout. The scout called her home to ask if the girl would be willing to fly to South Korea for an audition.

She was 13 years old that year.

> **💡 Did You Know?**
> The average age of JYP trainees is 14–16, but 13 is an exceptionally early entry age. Dealing with language barriers, being away from family, training 8–10 hours a day, and the constant threat of elimination makes this one of the most brutal entry points in the K-pop industry. Tzuyu spent three full years as a trainee (November 2012 – October 2015)[^2], spending her entire adolescence in Seoul.

When she announced she was going to South Korea while at Fuxing Junior High School, classmates who liked her held a camping farewell party for her[^1]. On November 15, she landed at Gimpo Airport. From that day on, her daily routine became: attending Korean language classes by day, practicing dance by night, and being so exhausted upon returning to the dormitory that she could only sleep after intense stretching[^2].

## 47 Days After Debut

In 2015, JYP selected the initial nine members of TWICE through the Mnet survival show _SIXTEEN_. Tzuyu was not originally among those nine. However, the audience loved her so much that after the show ended, founder Park Jin-young decided to add Tzuyu and another Japanese member, MOMO, bringing the group from nine to 11 members[^1].

On October 20, TWICE released their debut album _THE STORY BEGINS_[^1]. Forty-seven days later, the story was interrupted by a flag.

On November 21, 2015, Tzuyu was filming for the Korean MBC variety show _My Little Television_. When the host asked members to introduce their home countries, she held up a Republic of China flag. The camera time lasted approximately 8 seconds[^3]. At the moment of filming, no one thought much of it, and the footage did not immediately go viral.

For two whole months, nothing happened.

> **📝 Curator's Note**
> Two months. From waving the flag on November 21, 2015, to Huang An's post on January 8, 2016—two full months passed without anyone paying attention. If Huang An hadn't stumbled upon that video that day; if the Chinese government hadn't been inviting TWICE to the Spring Festival Gala at that time; if this hadn't happened one week before the Taiwan presidential election, this "incident" might never have existed. But history does not accept "ifs."

## Huang An's Weibo Post

On January 8, 2016, a Taiwanese entertainer named **Huang An** (黃安) posted screenshots on Sina Weibo, accusing Tzuyu of "promoting Taiwan independence" and calling for netizens to boycott her[^4].

Who is Huang An? His real name is Huang Hung-ming. His career in Taiwan collapsed in the late 1990s due to controversies, friction with various hosts, and being blacklisted by the media; he eventually moved his family to China to develop his career[^5]. In China, he rebranded himself as a "whistleblower against Taiwan independence." In 2015, he filed a formal report against a Taiwanese netizen named Chung Yu-chen to the Taiwan Affairs Office of the State Council, receiving public "respect and gratitude" from the office.

That Weibo post regarding Tzuyu ignited anti-Taiwan independence sentiment among Chinese netizens. At the time, Chinese state media was inviting TWICE to perform at the Spring Festival Gala. Overnight, TWICE's endorsements were withdrawn, their Spring Festival performance was canceled, and all of Tzuyu's individual endorsements vanished[^4]. JYP Entertainment released its first statement on the evening of January 14, stating that Tzuyu "firmly supports and respects the One China principle"[^3]. Public opinion in Taiwan exploded.

At 10:00 PM on January 15, the second statement arrived. This time, it wasn't text; it was a video.

## Those 90 Seconds

> **✦** "There is only one China, and the two sides of the Strait are one. I have always been proud to be Chinese."

She wore a black turtleneck, wore no makeup, her eyes were hollow, and the script in her hands was shaking[^3]. This 90-second video became the biggest news story in the final 24 hours of Taiwan's 2016 election campaign.

That night, Taiwan's PTT, Facebook, and Twitter exploded. Young people said the footage looked like "terrorists forcing a hostage to read demands." Some began posting photos of their Republic of China ID cards on Facebook with the caption: "I am Taiwanese; this is my identity."

The next day, January 16, was election day. Tsai Ing-wen was elected as the first female president with 6.89 million votes[^7]. In her victory speech that evening, she uttered a sentence that would be repeatedly quoted later:

> "As long as I am President, I will work hard to ensure that not one of my citizens has to apologize for their identity."[^7]

## 1.34 Million People Who Changed Their Minds

Months after the election, the _South China Morning Post_ tracked a figure: **1.34 million**. This was the number of young voters estimated by polls to have "changed their voting decision because of the Tzuyu incident." Some had originally planned not to vote at all but went out the next day after watching those 90 seconds; some had intended to vote for the Kuomintang (KMT) but switched to the Democratic Progressive Party (DPP); others were neutral and were pushed by this event[^8].

Polls from Taiwanese think tanks indicated that the incident influenced approximately **ten percent** of the votes[^7]. Scholar Fan Shih-ping believed this apology had a "direct and massive impact on the 2016 election"[^9]. An academic journal later published an analysis titled 〈The politics of apology: The Tzuyu Scandal〉, discussing the event within the framework of K-pop's transnational dynamics[^10]. _The Economist_ featured it in a cover story at the time, writing: "This is the first time Chinese nationalism has backfired on K-pop. A 16-year-old girl has become the center of a three-way political struggle."

But the center of the story never spoke. For Tzuyu herself, her father, and her mother, the family's response to those 90 seconds in 2016 remained the same. In 2018, when Taiwanese actress Song Yun-hua (宋芸樺) faced a similar "Chinese person" controversy in China, reporters asked Tzuyu's mother, Huang Yen-ling, for her opinion. She said:

> "I'm sorry, I don't talk about politics."[^16]

This is the standard answer from this family to all political questions.

> **⚠️ Controversial Viewpoints**
> Was the Tzuyu incident a coincidence or a conspiracy? There is no definitive conclusion. One side believes it was a spontaneous action by Chinese nationalist netizens, with Huang An merely being the spark; another side believes the timing was too coincidental (occurring one week before the election), suggesting political manipulation. The Chinese side maintains that the incident was excessively politicized by Taiwanese media. Regardless of the origin, the **result** is clear: it changed an election.

## From 〈Cheer Up〉 to 〈Fancy〉: The One Chosen as the "Face"

In April 2016, TWICE released their second mini-album _PAGE TWO_. The title track 〈Cheer Up〉 swept the Gaon charts and won Song of the Year at the Mnet Asian Music Awards (MAMA) at the end of the year[^19]. Three months after the flag incident, not only was Tzuyu not reclaimed by the Chinese market, but she and TWICE actually became one of the most popular girl groups in all of South Korea thanks to this song.

In TWICE, she serves as **lead dancer, sub-vocalist, and _maknae_**. While "_maknae_" is Korean for "youngest," members noted that Tzuyu is calm, speaks formally, and uses Korean more precisely than the older members; conversely, they say she is "the one who feels most like the eldest sister in the group"[^1]. They also gave her a nickname: **Yoda**, because of her large round eyes and prominent ears.

In October 2016, TWICE released their third mini-album _TWICEcoaster: LANE 1_. The title track 〈TT〉 held the number one spot on Gaon for four consecutive weeks, and the "TT crying dance" became a viral dance trend among teenagers across Asia[^19]. Within a year, TWICE went from rookies to one of the top three girl groups in K-pop.

2017 was the year Tzuyu's popularity exploded externally as well. On January 30, during the ISAC (Idol Star Athletics Championships), TWICE entered the archery event. Tzuyu stepped to the line, drew her bow, and released; the bowstring caught her long hair and she missed the target, but her movements were so graceful that the video surpassed one million views within 24 hours[^20]. _Thor: Ragnarok_ director Taika Waititi and _Spy_ director Paul Feig both shared the GIF. Eight years later, the clip is still being circulated in Indian social media circles[^20].

> **📝 Curator's Note**
> Tzuyu's archery moment is a metaphor for her entire career: she didn't hit the target, but her posture was so beautiful that everyone remembered it. It took ten years for her to tell the world through a handwritten letter that she had actually been doubting whether she belonged there. But when the world looks at her, they only ever see that pose of drawing the bow.

In November 2017, TWICE appeared on Japan's NHK _Kohaku Uta Gassen_, becoming the first Korean group invited in six years[^19]. That same year, TWICE's first full-length album _Twicetagram_ and its title track 〈LIKEY〉 swept the charts again. With 〈What is Love?〉 in 2018 and 〈Fancy〉 in 2019, every song became a textbook representative of K-pop girl groups across Asia.

The American film website TC Candler releases an annual "Most Beautiful Faces" poll. Starting from 13th place in 2015, Tzuyu has appeared on the list every year, reaching **number 1** in 2019—making her the first Asian woman in history to take the top spot[^15].

She is famous, but she does not speak.

## The First Time Someone Heard Her Speak for Herself

In April 2019, Tzuyu posted a long message in English on her newly opened Instagram account. She said she had "been thinking a lot lately," telling fans, "You can't make everyone like you. There will always be people who don't like you for no reason, and that's normal," and urged fans to "remember who you are, remember the people who truly know you"[^21].

One comment was translated into Chinese countless times: "Even my family doesn't know how to comfort me."

That was three years after the flag incident, and it was the first time she used the first person to discuss "unhappiness" in public. She didn't mention any specific events, offered no explanations, made no complaints, and leveled no accusations. But she let everyone reading those words know: she was **having a hard time**.

In the following years, TWICE became even more famous, and Tzuyu became harder to find. In 2020, she released her personal photobook _Yes, I am Tzuyu._, making her the first member of TWICE to release one[^1]. In May 2022, all members of TWICE opened individual Instagram accounts; Tzuyu's handle is @thinkaboutzu. In March 2023, this account surpassed **10 million followers**, making her the second most-followed Taiwanese artist on Instagram, trailing only [Jay Chou](/en/people/jay-chou/)[^17].

> **💡 Did You Know?**
> In December 2024, during the 30th-anniversary celebration of Fuxing Elementary School in the East District of Tainan, the school unexpectedly revealed something Tzuyu had never mentioned: she had already obtained a **Master's degree in Applied Psychology from Universidad de La Sabiduría (Cervantes University) in Spain**[^18]. She has never attended a traditional university. The school allowed her extensive work experience to substitute for undergraduate credentials; during the pandemic, through a recommendation from a professor she met, she completed eight online courses and a thesis in just over a year[^11]. She held no press conference, posted no stories, and didn't even mention it on Instagram. The school posted it themselves.

On September 6, 2024, she finally released her solo EP _abouTZU_—a name combining "about" + "TZU," meaning "everything about Tzuyu." The music video for the title track 〈Run Away〉 surpassed 4 million views within 24 hours on YouTube and topped both the Korean Bugs and Taiwan iTunes charts[^12]. However, in interviews, she did not explain why the song was called "Run Away," nor did she explain what she was running from or where she was running to.

She simply released it, danced to it, and then fell silent again.

## Ten Years, and a Letter

TWICE debuted on October 20, 2015.

On November 22, 2025—exactly ten years and one month later—TWICE came to Taiwan for their first concert. The venue was the Kaohsiung World Games Stadium, with 55,000 people attending two shows, totaling 110,000 people[^13]. This was the first time in ten years since her debut that Tzuyu stood on a stage in her hometown.

Before the opening, she brought out a handwritten script—physically similar to the one she held on January 15, 2016, but with entirely different content. She read it to the 55,000 people, her voice choking with emotion throughout:

> **✦** "But sometimes, when everything seems to be going well, I actually feel empty and helpless. I try so hard to do my best, yet I slowly lose feeling, and I often miss home. That flame once became very small, so small that I began to doubt if I belonged here. But I didn't leave because I was obedient or submissive, but because I truly wanted it and desired it. I know that light has never gone out, so I am willing to continue watering my own seeds."[^22]

55,000 people wept along with her. Even the Korean leader, Dahyun, said in Chinese: "We are finally holding a concert in our pride Tzuyu's hometown!"[^13]

Nine years ago, during those 90 seconds, she read words written by someone else; these 90 seconds, she read words she wrote herself. Nine years ago, that script said, "I am Chinese"; this script says, "I didn't leave because I desired it." The same girl, the same action (standing and reading a script), but two completely different people.

Nine months later, from March 20 to 22, 2026, TWICE held three consecutive shows at the Taipei Dome. With 120,000 people over three days, the Taipei Metro saw a single-day ridership of **2.606 million** on March 21, breaking the monthly record[^14]. She stood in the center of the Taipei Dome and wept once more.

## Two Silences, One Desire

Tzuyu's life contains two kinds of silence.

The first was the 90 seconds on January 15, 2016, when she read words written by others—a silence of being gagged. She opened her mouth, but the words were not her own.

The second was the nine years of silence following those 90 seconds, where she no longer spoke of it—a silence of **choice**. She did not "clarify," she did not offer an "apology for the apology," she did not say "the real me is actually...", and she gave no interviews regarding her identity. She simply continued to dance, release albums, collect her distinguished alumna award from Fuxing Elementary School, and post photos of her dog on Instagram. Her entire family said, "I don't talk about politics."

But beneath these two silences, the handwritten letter in Kaohsiung on November 22, 2025, tells us there is a third layer: **desire**. She didn't leave because she was obedient or submissive; she left because she truly wanted it and desired it. She was never a passive recipient of circumstances, but someone who chose to keep watering her seeds.

> **📝 Curator's Note**
> The reason Tsai Ing-wen's line "no one needs to apologize for their identity" became one of the most quoted victory speeches of the decade is because it presupposes a painful reality: before that night, Taiwanese people took it for granted that "apologizing for identity" was something that would never happen. Until a 16-year-old girl proved that it could.
>
> And Tzuyu's ten years tell us something else: **some voices are not expressed through words**. Her silence is not a void; it is the accumulation of a Taiwanese girl growing from 16 to 26, waking up at 4:00 AM every morning to train, writing a Master's thesis in Korean, re-recording lines in the studio until they were perfect, and drawing that single bow at the Idol Star Athletics Championships.

The 1.34 million Taiwanese people who had no intention of voting went out on the morning of January 16, not because Tzuyu took a stand for them, but because when they saw a 16-year-old Taiwanese girl forced to take a stand for someone else, they could no longer pretend they hadn't seen it.

Her entire family says, "I don't talk about politics." But her existence, starting from those 8 seconds with the flag, is no longer just about "her." She is a Tainan girl who left home at 13, wrote a Master's thesis in Korean, and had her hair caught in a bowstring. She is also a political symbol that forced Tsai Ing-wen to say that night that "no one needs to apologize for their identity." She doesn't choose one or the other—she cannot choose. She simply continues to water her seeds.

And this island, after that night of January 15, learned how to finish the sentences for those who could not speak for themselves.
