# Yang Wei-che: Math Professor Opens Taiwanese-taught Class First, Controversy Follows

In the autumn of 1996, National Taiwan University (NTU) mathematics professor Yang Wei-che opened a general education course titled "Mathematical Methods and Reasoning." The syllabus explicitly stated that the course would be taught in _Tâi-gí_ (Taiwanese), noting that students were not required to speak Taiwanese to enroll. Beside this course on the university's official schedule, four characters were marked: "Taiwanese-taught." [^1]

According to later research by historian Chou Wan-yao, then-Academic Affairs Vice President Lee Si-zen critiqued the course with the remark, "It is inappropriate to use dialects in class." Yang Wei-che believed that using Taiwanese for teaching did not require approval from the Academic Affairs office; thus, during that academic year, the course proceeded exactly as planned until its conclusion. [^1]

Chou Wan-yao concluded: "This should be the first official course at NTU to explicitly label itself as being taught in Taiwanese!" [^1] Years later, in an interview, Yang Wei-che spoke plainly about why he insisted on offering this course: "As long as I actually do it, they can no longer use a mountain of excuses to claim that teaching in Taiwanese is impossible!" [^2]

In mathematics, to disprove an "impossibility," one only needs to find a single counterexample. Yang Wei-che's response to the linguistic controversies in Taiwan was also a counterexample—and it was a course scheduled into the curriculum and completed for a full academic year. The following autumn, a legislator stated in the legislature that this course was unconstitutional, even claiming to have personally called him to advise against it. By 2018, when the Legislative Yuan was reviewing the National Languages Development Act, both proponents and critics used his use of Taiwanese in class as an example. [^3][^4]

> **30-Second Overview:** Born in Fengyuan, Taichung, in 1939, Yang Wei-che is the second son of painter Yang Chi-tung. After being admitted to NTU's College of Medicine, he dropped out after three years to retake the entrance exams for the Department of Mathematics. He earned his PhD from Princeton University and returned to teach at NTU in 1971 before retiring in 2005. In 1996, he taught mathematics in Taiwanese during a general education course; the following year, New Party legislator Fu Kun-chih identified it as unconstitutional in the Legislative Yuan. During the 2018 Legislative Yuan deliberations on the National Languages Development Act, Deputy Minister of Education Lin Fu-lai and legislators Kuan Pi-ling and Ko Chih-en all cited him as an example; Article 9 was subsequently amended to state that schools shall use various national languages in education. He taught Chen Wen-cheng, served as a director of the Chen Wen-cheng Memorial Foundation for over thirty years, and at eighty-six, he still lectures on tidal forces at the NTU Department of Physics.

## A Single Phone Call to the Professor Himself

![An aerial view of the Legislative Yuan campus, with green-roofed buildings and a central courtyard lawn surrounded by red-brick buildings, and high-rise buildings of Taipei City in the distance](/article-images/people/yang-wei-che-legislative-yuan-2024.jpg)
_The Legislative Yuan campus and surrounding streetscape, 2024. Photo: Yu tptw / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0_

At 9:00 AM on Monday, October 6, 1997, in Meeting Room 7 of the Legislative Yuan, the Education Committee held its fourth meeting of the fourth session of the third term. Minister of Education Wu Chin was present to answer inquiries. The Chairperson, Lo Chuan-chin, called upon the first questioner, New Party legislator Fu Kun-chih. Fu first asked about road signs for the Li Mei-shu Memorial Hall in Sanxia, the term "turmoil" (動亂) in history textbooks, and graduate student subsidies; he then discussed the reduction of government-funded study abroad quotas from 150 to 50. Finally, he brought up National Taiwan University (NTU).[^3]

The verbatim transcript recorded in the Legislative Yuan Gazette stated: "Another issue is that some domestic schools, such as NTU Department of Mathematics Professor Yang Wei-che, who taught a general education course on Mathematical Principles last academic year, stipulated that teaching must be conducted using Taiwanese pronunciation. This is a completely unconstitutional practice. If anyone requires a specific dialect to be the restricted language for teaching and research at a national university, or even for a public matter, it is an unconstitutional form of discrimination against students whose mother tongue is not Taiwanese."[^3] The Gazette printed his name as "Yang Wei-che" (楊惟哲), and the course title did not match the official syllabus; the course was actually called "Mathematical Methods and Reasoning."[^1]

Fu Kun-chih continued, saying that he had already reported this matter in person to Vice Minister Yang and requested him to convey it to NTU: "This member also personally called the NTU President's office and Professor Yang Wei-che himself to persuade them, but it was of no effect." Having heard that a professor at National Chung Hsing University was also teaching in this manner, he requested the Minister to investigate all public and private colleges and universities nationwide to see which professors "restrict teaching to a dialect, such as Hakka or Taiwanese, regardless of whether the students understand it."[^3] Yang Wei-che later told Chou Wan-yao that the professor at Chung Hsing was Liu Hsing-yi from the Department of Law.[^1]

In his response, Wu Chin first clarified the incident involving National Cheng Kung University: before he became the President of NCKU, he had returned to Taiwan for an interview, and the selection committee asked him this question. "My answer at the time was that it is best to teach using a language everyone shares—Mandarin." Regarding NTU and Chung Hsing, he stated that if such situations as described by the legislator were true, the Ministry of Education would report them to the schools, "but for matters like this, it is most appropriate for the universities themselves to handle them. For a university to have autonomy, the university and its president must take responsibility; they should not throw trivial matters at the Ministry of Education. These are matters that universities should stand up and resolve themselves."[^3]

Fu Kun-chih insisted that this was a "constitutional-level issue" that the Ministry of Education should proactively handle. Wu Chin thus promised: "I pledge that the Ministry of Education will conduct a general investigation of all colleges and universities nationwide, though I suspect this phenomenon is not very common," while also warning that if an investigation were conducted, schools might feel the Ministry was interfering too much in administration. Fu Kun-chih concluded with an analogy: "Some things cannot be left to university autonomy; it's not as if the Ministry of Education stops caring about everything once universities are autonomous... For example: if a university said they wanted to arrange practical training courses involving firearms and ammunition, would the Ministry of Education agree?" He requested that the Minister provide an explanation after the investigation, to which Wu Chin replied, "Understood."[^3]

According to Chou Wan-yao's compilation, the headline in the _United Daily News_ that evening was "Legislator Criticizes Use of Taiwanese in Class as Unconstitutional," and both the _United Daily News_ and _China Times_ used the phrase "Legislator Criticizes Unconstitutionality" the following day.[^1] In his inquiry, Fu Kun-chih referred to a course taught "last academic year," but by October of that year, the course had already been completed for an entire year. The phone calls failed to persuade, and while the promise to conduct a nationwide investigation had just been made, the example was already standing right there.[^3]

The "Mandarin" (國語) mentioned by Wu Chin in the Gazette refers to Chinese, but Yang Wei-che had a different definition for those words. He had participated in the Ministry of Education's Mandarin Promotion Committee; in his 2002 article "Romanization and Mathematics," he wrote: "My definition of Mandarin is: Taiwanese, Hakka, and Indigenous languages are all Mandarin! Just like Huayu (Chinese)!"[^5] Even earlier, in 1998, he told a reporter from _Taiwan Guanghua_: "A nation is produced by the will of its people, not by some 'transcendental' existence; the same applies to 'Mandarin'."[^6] He compiled his own Taiwanese teaching materials, translated dictionaries, and self-studied several types of Taiwanese romanization systems. From 1989 to 1992, he also served as the second chairman of the Xitian Society Glove Puppetry Foundation.[^2][^7]

## Three Years in Medical School, and Only After Reading the Newspaper Did His Father Find Out

![The gate of Taichung First Senior High School, with a row of square pillars supporting a lintel inscribed with "National Taichung First Senior High School," as several students walk beneath it](/article-images/people/yang-wei-che-taichung-first-high-gate-2007.jpg)
_The gate of Taichung First Senior High School, 2007. Photo: H.T. Yu / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 2.0_

Yang Wei-che was born on November 21, 1939, in Fengyuan, Taichung. He was the second son of the painter Yang Chi-tung; his mother's native language was the _Minnan_ (Hokkien) dialect from the Haikou region of Quanzhou. [^8][^9] As he was nearing the end of fifth grade in elementary school, seeing that his son was gifted in mathematics, his father approached the principal to request that he skip a grade. He subsequently passed the entrance exam for the junior high division of Taichung First Senior High School, ranking first in the class. On the day the results were posted, his father rushed to Taichung First Senior High School to look for his son's name on the honors list, but failing to find it, he returned home dejectedly. Later, a friend informed him that the lists were arranged by examination numbers, and the top ten students were announced separately in the upper right corner—and his son was at the very top. [^8]

In high school, he developed a passion for physics, though his parents expected him to attend medical school. He first used his high scores to secure admission to National Taiwan University (NTU) Medicine; however, after studying there for three years, he withdrew to retake the entrance exams for the Department of Mathematics. [^8][^9] [Huang Wu-hsiung](/en/society/huang-wu-hsiung-community-college), who is four years younger than him, later wrote: "Yang Wei-che was four years my senior; after finishing three years of medical school and retaking the exams for the math department, he became our 'big brother'." [^10] According to Yang Chi-tung's biography, his father did not learn of this until he saw it in the newspaper. Yang Wei-che remembered that his father only gave him one solemn instruction: "This is your own choice; you must work extremely hard, otherwise people will be waiting to laugh at you." [^9] More than thirty years later, when he began teaching classes in _Tâi-gí_ (Taiwanese) at NTU, the Dean of Academic Affairs rejected his proposal, but he proceeded with the classes as originally planned. In one instance, he went against his parents' expectations; in the other, he went against the Dean's directive. Both times, he chose to act first.

He graduated from the Department of Mathematics in 1964 and later went abroad for further studies, earning a PhD from Princeton University in 1971, specializing in mathematical physics and probability theory. [^11] In August of that same year, he returned to NTU to take over as Chair of the Department of Mathematics, serving until July 1973. [^12] Huang Wu-hsiung recalls that Yang Wei-che returned to Taiwan one year earlier than him and became the department chair immediately upon his return. [^10] During those early years back at NTU, he taught his classes in Mandarin. In an interview in 2002, he remarked that he had not yet "awakened" at that time and felt deep shame when looking back. [^2]

## Teach Integration First, Then Differentiation

![NTU Coconut Grove Avenue, two rows of towering King Palms flanking a straight road, with a person riding a bicycle toward the camera](/article-images/people/yang-wei-che-ntu-royal-palm-boulevard-2014.jpg)
_NTU Coconut Grove Avenue, 2014. Photo: Daderot / Wikimedia Commons, CC0_

In September 1994, he wrote an article titled "Some Small Opinions on Calculus Teaching" in _Mathematics Communication_, published by the Institute of Mathematical Sciences at Academia Sinica. He stated that the essential task of mathematics is undoubtedly "the establishment of concepts," yet students often mistakenly believe that constructing concepts is a metaphysical, "empty-handed" endeavor. Consequently, they are unwilling to perform the "blue-collar" tasks of calculating numerical values or plotting points and graphs; as a result, they can never construct correct and useful concepts, leaving their only problem-solving strategy as "building a retrieval system for question banks."[^13]

In the article, he introduced the new calculus curriculum promoted by Harvard in the United States. Its guiding principle is "three-way," emphasizing equal importance to graphics, numerical values, and analysis; he believed this was "absolutely viable." He also intended to require students to bring calculators with trigonometric, logarithmic, and exponential functions to class. Regarding the order of instruction, he advocated for reversing it: "In my personal experience, teaching (definite) integration first, then differentiation, is probably a good idea!"[^13] Sixteen years later, while teaching Calculus B at NTU, he used his own textbook, _Calculus_, published by Sanmin Book Co. His syllabus began with limits, continuity, and sequences; finite differences, summation, and definite integrals were all placed before differentiation.[^14]

In the same article, discussing rigorous derivations such as stepping from Rolle's Theorem to the Mean Value Theorem, he admitted this might not please everyone: "Whether students will appreciate or comprehend it, I cannot guarantee; but as a university professor, I have this conviction."[^13] On the list of Outstanding and Distinguished Teachers for NTU's 92nd academic year (2003 to 2004), Yang Wei-che was listed under the Department of Mathematics, College of Science.[^15]

In October 2002, _New Taiwan News Weekly_ published an interview with him. He spoke about teaching mathematics in _Tâi-gí_ (Taiwanese) at Keelung Community College. He mentioned one student who was nearly visually impaired, so he used cardboard pieces to assemble regular polyhedra, allowing the student to feel them by hand. During the interview, he also pulled several honeycomb pieces from a drawer, noting that hexagonal cells are the most space-efficient. In that interview, he said: "I am absolutely confident in teaching in _Tâi-gí_; I treat teaching as a performing art, and every situation is different."[^2]

Many people remember him riding his bicycle around the NTU campus. A reporter from _Parenting Matters_ wrote that he "often wore shorts and sandals, sported a full beard, and could be seen roaming the campus on a bicycle"[^16], while Chou Wan-yao wrote more concisely: "A well-groomed beard, wearing shorts, and riding a bicycle—it is a classic NTU sight."[^17] A student who took his calculus class recalled on a BBS that he always carried a gray backpack to class and argued that there was no need to memorize formulas; one should simply derive them directly.[^18]

## The Basement Cleanup

On June 18, 2024, Normal University held a seminar themed "Adaptability and Transition Guidance for Gifted Math and Science Students Entering University." Eighty-four-year-old Yang Wei-che participated in the entire session. The records from the NTNU Mathematics Education Center noted: "Professor Yang is a pioneer of gifted education in Taiwan; he cares deeply about gifted education and has been a teacher to many gifted students." [^19] One of the speakers that day was Yeh Bing-cheng, who wrote in a social media post the following day: "From starting math classes in the NTU Department of Mathematics in high school, to later attending training camps for Mathematical Olympiad contestants, and eventually being recommended for admission to the math department—Professor Yang Wei-che watched me grow up." [^20]

According to Yeh Bing-cheng's account, after the lecture ended, he went to pay his respects to the professor. The professor remarked that it was a pity he hadn't been scheduled to speak; if he had, he would have told everyone that, in his view, giftedness is about personality, not problem-solving. The professor then recounted an old story: during the summer break of the year Yeh Bing-cheng served as president of the student association in his junior year, Yang Wei-che was teaching summer courses in calculus at the department building. Seeing Yeh writing on the classroom blackboard, Yang called on students to help clean up the department's basement, which had been messy for years, the following day. Early the next morning, Yang Wei-che made a special trip to check on them, only to see Yeh Bing-cheng sweeping all by himself. He joined in to help sweep until it was time to go to class. The professor said that true giftedness is this quality of being willing to do the things no one else wants to do for the sake of others; consequently, he later included this story in the recommendation letter he wrote for Yeh Bing-cheng. [^20]

The books he has written for middle and high school students include _Analytical Geometry for Junior High School Gifted Students_ (published by Sanmin Book Co.) as well as the series _Professor Yang Wei-che's Mathematics Lecture Hall_ and _Yang Wei-che's Gifted High School Mathematics Lecture Notes_ (published by Wunan). [^21] Even earlier, in 1981 and 1982, he published problems, solutions, and analyses from national high school mathematics competitions in _Mathematics Communication_. [^22]

When _Common Week_ ran a feature titled "How Do They Educate the Gifted Children in Their Families?" in 1999, the reporter wrote that he seemed quite reluctant to discuss the topic of "giftedness," believing that children have their own futures, paths, and lives, and that parents should not arrange them. His answer was interspersed with _Tâi-gí_ (Taiwanese): "In short, it's best to let nature take its course!" [^23] His son, Yang Po-yin, graduated early from the NTU Department of Physics at age eighteen in 1987 and later became a Distinguished Research Fellow at the Institute of Information Science, Academia Sinica [^24]. His daughter, Yang Han-ju, also studied mathematics in university before pursuing a career in _Kunqu_ opera, performing as a _xiaosheng_ (young male role) [^25].

## Television News Behind the Scenes

During the academic year he returned to teach at NTU in 1971, a senior student in the Mathematics Department named Chen Wen-cheng enrolled in his "Theoretical Mechanics" course. Chen graduated the following year; although the teacher and student only overlapped for one year in the classroom, their contact did not cease: during the summer of 1980, Yang Wei-che stayed at Chen Wen-cheng's home in Pittsburgh for a few days, and in 1981, when Chen returned to Taiwan to visit relatives, he visited the Yang family twice. [^12]

In July of that year, he entered the examination hall for the first time to serve as a mathematics item consultant at the invitation of Chien Kuo High School principal Huang Chien-pin. Once you enter the examination hall, there is no coming out. It was likely on the second night of his involvement that he saw on the television news that his student, Chen Wen-cheng, had been found dead on the NTU campus. [^12]

In his later oral history, he mentioned that newspapers reported Chen Wen-cheng had visited an elderly professor a few days before the incident; he knew immediately they were referring to him. When reporters and police arrived at his home, his family produced his proof of involvement in the examination process. Speaking of this passage, his conclusion was that fate will unfold as it must, and there is no use in being afraid. [^12]

After the incident, Chen Wen-cheng's parents and several friends frequently gathered at Huang Wu-hsiung's dormitory on Zhoushan Road; Huang Wu-hsiung remembers that shortly thereafter, both he and Yang Wei-che joined the Chen Wen-cheng Memorial Foundation. [^10] When the foundation held its first board meeting in 1987, it was still called the Taiwan-US Cultural Exchange Center, and Yang Wei-che was on the list of directors; he remained on the board for every subsequent term until 2021, when he was listed as an honorary director for the sixteenth term. [^26]

On July 2, 2012, NTU faculty and students held a memorial evening at the site of the incident to mark the 31st anniversary of Chen Wen-cheng's martyrdom. According to reports by _Sing Tao_, he said during the memorial that every year on July 2nd happened to coincide with the time he would be in the examination hall for university entrance exams; when the Chen Wen-cheng incident occurred, he was also in an examination hall, and it caused him great pain every time he thought of it. He also mentioned that when they were trying to establish the foundation back then, many NTU professors came to advise them not to make a fuss about the matter anymore; as a person as timid as himself, thinking about what worse things might happen, he ignored those dissuasions. [^27]

## A Line of Text on a Low Wall

The transcript of oral histories from the University Archives records him saying this about himself: "Although I am not that enthusiastic about politics and do not participate so actively, if you ask me to express my thoughts or opinions, then I will speak directly." [^12] On October 23, 1993, the NTU Administrative Council resolved to investigate the Philosophy Department incident. During the investigation team's first meeting, the President asked those present to elect a convener on the spot; he was elected immediately. On May 28, 1995, the team released the 《Investigation Report on the NTU Philosophy Department Incident》. [^28]

In early February 2021, the Chen Wen-cheng Memorial Plaza was completed on the NTU campus. According to Chou Wan-yao, the line "Commemorating a brave person who resolutely resisted state violence" on the plaza's low wall was text proposed by Yang Wei-che [^29], which was then written in regular script (_kaishu_) by Chang Bing-huang after discussion. The university administration had objections to the four words "state violence," but the foundation's view was to put it up temporarily, as the text had also been approved by the Board of Directors. [^30] That version of the temporary text was taken down on February 5. [^31] On June 19, the NTU Administrative Council voted on a proposal from student representatives and passed it with 74 votes to 64 to install this line of text on the "31" low wall; on September 28, the text was officially placed on the wall. [^31][^32] Much like that course in 1996, the thing was produced first, and institutional approval arrived one step too late.

## Twenty-One Years Later, the Legislative Yuan Mentions Him Again

On February 19, 2017, the Ministry of Education held an event for International Mother Language Day and invited him to speak on "The Inheritance and Reflection of Experiences in Teaching _Minnan_ (Hokkien) Classes." According to a report by _China Times_, he said on stage: "Students are not very proficient in Taiwanese; they asked me if they could take Calculus [using it]." Minister of Education Pan Wen-chung responded: "Universities can now offer courses in English, and of course, they can also use indigenous/local languages."[^33]

On May 7 of the following year, the Legislative Yuan's Education and Culture Committee reviewed the draft National Languages Development Act. When reviewing Article 9, Paragraph 3, which states "School education may use various national languages for this purpose," Legislator Ko Chih-en first raised a scenario: If a teacher insists on teaching in a certain language and parents or students cannot accept it, how should it be handled? Deputy Minister of Education Lin Fu-lai replied: "Actually, this has already happened; for example, Professor Yang at NTU uses _Tâi-gí_ (Taiwanese) to teach Calculus"[^4], then added that what Legislator Ko was concerned about was what would happen to a class if students could not understand it.

Legislator Kwan Bi-ling then suggested that everyone needn't worry too much. She cited Yang Wei-che and Liu Hsing-yi of the Department of Law at National Chung Hsing University's College of Law and Business (the predecessor of National Taipei University) as examples, noting that they both advocated for mother-tongue teaching: "Because they balance the students' right to learn, they only use it in elective courses."[^4] She argued for passing the version drafted by the Executive Yuan. Ko Chih-en finally returned to the subject of the article, stating that school education must have a set of plans and will not allow a teacher's personal ideas to force parents and children to use a specific language at a fixed time: "The advocacy of Professor Yang Wei-che or other professors belongs to exceptions."[^4]

Three different speakers used the same example: Lin Fu-lai used it to explain that this had already happened; Kwan Bi-ling used it to show there was no need for worry; and Ko Chih-en used it to demonstrate that it was merely an exception. In 1997, someone wanted the Ministry of Education to investigate his classes; by 2018, both the proponents and the skeptics reviewing the same legal clause used him as evidence, and the Gazette also got his name right this time.[^3][^4]

> 📝 **Curator's Note**
> In mathematics, "this is impossible" can be overturned by a single counterexample, but "this should be done" requires separate argumentation. Yang Wei-che's _Tâi-gí_ classes overturned the "impossible," proving that Taiwanese can be used to teach university mathematics, yet they did not answer whether every subject should be taught this way. Thus, both the proponents and skeptics in 2018 found it useful: the counterexample answered the question of "can we," leaving the question of "should we" to the Legislative Yuan.

The "teaching Calculus in _Tâi-gí_" mentioned by Lin Fu-lai is also the version that has long circulated on the NTU campus. When Chou Wan-yao compiled records in 2022, she specifically noted that the first classes he taught in Taiwanese at NTU were general education courses; Calculus came six years later: from the autumn of 2002 to the spring of the following year, he taught "Calculus A" in the Department of Civil Engineering. He taught as usual in the first semester and switched to _Tâi-gí_ in the second semester; the class next door during the same time slot was Professor Hu Ching-hsuan's, and students could choose for themselves. He did not offer that course again and retired in 2005.[^1][^11]

On January 9, 2019, the National Languages Development Act was promulgated. Article 9, Paragraph 3 is the exact sentence from the review: "School education may use various national languages for this purpose."[^34] Looking back on these events, Chou Wan-yao was not optimistic: "Ah, 26 years later, _Tâi-gí_ is on the brink of extinction, and the youth who are anti-'dialect' have only progressed to no longer calling it 'unconstitutional'!"[^1]

## Tidal Force at Eighty-Six

In November 2023, Chou Wan-yao launched the _Taiwanese Friday Lectures_, a series of lectures held on Fridays in homage to the "Sunday Lectures" (日曜日講座) of the Yixin Society in Wufeng during the 1930s. The first lecture in the preview was scheduled for December 1, featuring Yang Wei-che as the speaker on the topic "Discussing Politics and Systems from a Mathematical Perspective" at the NTU College of Liberal Arts Lecture Hall; the registration form was briefly closed due to reaching capacity. [^17][^35]

In June 2025, his 〈Three Lectures on Tidal Force〉 was published in _Mathematics Communication_, Vol. 49, No. 2. Tidal force is the force exerted by the Moon and the Sun that causes the ebb and flow of seawater on Earth. In the introduction, he explained that these lectures were inspired by reading an article on tides written by his dear friends, Mo Tsung-chien and his wife. Following their example, he dedicated the article to Shih Ke-kang, a high school physics teacher at Taichung First Senior High School: "Mr. Shih allowed me: in class, I could look down at extracurricular physics books, skip the exercises, and instead use problems from any extracurricular book to complete and submit." [^36] That student—who missed physics in high school but was later recruited into medical school—still remembered this teacher who permitted him to find his own problems nearly seventy years later. [^8]

At 2:20 PM on September 8, 2026, an academic lecture titled 〈Tidal Force and its Eigen-Problem〉 was held in Room 104 of the NTU Physics Department. The speaker listed on the announcement was Professor Yang Wei-che of the National Taiwan University Department of Mathematics, whose English name is written as Wui-Tet Yiunn. He was eighty-six years old that day. [^37]

Thirty years ago, regarding that course marked "Taught in Taiwanese," the Dean of Academic Affairs had disapproved it, and legislators had questioned it in the Legislative Yuan; more than twenty years later, a Deputy Minister used his Taiwanese-taught class as an example while reviewing legislation. Each time, the course existed first, and the controversy only followed. The announcement did not state how he lectured that afternoon. More than twenty years ago, when discussing teaching, he said that teaching is a performing art, and every situation is different. [^2]
