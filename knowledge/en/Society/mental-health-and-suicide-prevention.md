In 2024, the national number of suicide deaths was 4,062, with a standardized mortality rate of 13.4 per 100,000 people—the highest year since 2020. [^1]

In that same year, suicide returned to the top ten causes of death for citizens after a 14-year hiatus; in 2025, the number of deaths decreased to 3,951, but it remained at the 10th position. [^2][^3]

## Two Years, Four Numbers

First, let's look at 2024. With 4,062 deaths and a standardized mortality rate of 13.4 per 100,000, it marked the peak since 2020. [^1]

Next, 2025. Deaths fell to 3,951, a decrease of 111 people compared to 2024, or a 2.7% drop. The crude death rate was 16.9 per 100,000, while the standardized mortality rate was 12.9 per 100,000, representing a 3.6% decrease from the previous year. [^2]

These two numbers must be read together. Crude death rates rise alongside an aging population, whereas standardized mortality rates exclude the influence of age structure. A decline in the latter indicates that the actual risk of death is indeed slowing down; however, if the former remains high, it serves as a reminder that total numbers will not automatically decrease just because individual risk has dropped.

## The Line for Ages 15 to 24

Changes across age groups are not moving in the same direction. In 2024, there were 274 suicide deaths among those aged 15 to 24, with a crude death rate of 11.9 per 100,000—both figures being the highest since 2020. [^1]

The Legislative Yuan Budget Center noted in its evaluation report that while the Ministry of Health and Welfare (MOHW) has been promoting the "Mental Health Support Program for Young Adults Aged 15 to 45" since August 1, 2024, the crude death rate for the 15–24 age group in 2024 was still the highest since 2020, indicating that suicide prevention for this age group still requires strengthening. [^1]

A turning point appeared in 2025. When the MOHW released its cause of death statistics, it explained that deaths in the 15–24, 25–44, and 65+ age groups all decreased compared to 2024, while the 0–14 and 45–64 age groups saw slight increases. [^2]

In other words, while the overall numbers are falling, the risk is shifting between different age demographics. This is why looking at total numbers alone can cause one to miss critical signals.

## Debt and Fraud: Risks for Middle-Aged Adults

Statistics from the MOHW Mental Health Department provide another perspective. In historical reported data, the proportion of suicides caused by debt issues has climbed steadily from 3.04% in 2016, reaching a peak of 4.32% in 2024, before slightly dropping to 4.17% in 2025. [^2]

If looking specifically at the 45–64 age group, this proportion exceeds 5%, the highest among all age groups. Chen Po-hsi (陳柏熹), Director of the MOHW Mental Health Department, explained that "fraud" accounts for large amounts within debt losses; therefore, the department has collaborated with the Financial Supervisory Commission to enhance the sensitivity of financial industry professionals to act as "suicide prevention gatekeepers." [^2]

The logic behind this approach is worth noting: prevention does not rely solely on the medical sector, but rather on spreading the ability to identify risk to every window that comes into contact with the individual, including bank tellers.

## Three Hotlines and One Program

Current help channels are quite clear. The MOHW's "Anxin" (Relief) Hotline 1925, the Life Line Hotline 1995, and the Teacher Chang Hotline 1980; additionally, a specific "Men's Care Hotline" 0800-013-999 has been established for men, operating from 7:00 AM to 11:00 PM daily throughout the year. [^2]

The primary policy driver is the "Mental Health Support Program for Young Adults Aged 15 to 45." The MOHW noted that this program has cumulatively served 87,558 people with a user satisfaction rate of 96%, with participants reporting improvements in both quality of life and negative emotions; notably, 2025 also saw the first decrease in suicide deaths for the 15–64 age group. [^2]

It must be clarified that this is an official causal statement, not a rigorous efficacy evaluation. While the program implementation and death counts changed simultaneously in the same direction, there is no control group, so other factors cannot be ruled out. [^2]

## How to Read the Data

There are two things this article deliberately omits.

First, it does not describe any specific methods or on-site details. This piece focuses only on events and social context, avoiding descriptions of methods, scenes, or details that could potentially be imitated.

Second, it does not use the "Top Ten Causes of Death" as the sole framework for understanding this curve. Li Chiu-yen (李秋嬿), Director of the MOHW Statistics Department, explained that suicide's return to the top ten may be related to the success of liver disease prevention—which reduced deaths from that cause—allowing suicide to move up in ranking; the rank itself is not a direct indicator of risk. [^3]

The truly useful signals are three: the annual change in standardized mortality rates, the divergent trends between age groups, and the shifts in the structure of reported causes. All three provide more insight into where resources should be allocated next than the ranking alone. [^1][^2]

## If You Need Help

If you or someone around you is experiencing psychological distress, you can call the following hotlines directly; there is no need to prepare a formal explanation first:

- 1925 (MOHW Anxin Hotline)
- 1995 (Life Line Hotline)
- 1980 (Teacher Chang Hotline)
- 0800-013-999 (Men's Care Hotline, 07:00–23:00)
- In an emergency, please dial 119
