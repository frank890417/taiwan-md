# Tainan Railway Underground Project: The City is Sewn Together, but the Cost Falls on 340 Households

> **30-Second Overview:** On the evening of October 16, 2026, the last surface train passed through Tainan Station, marking the opening of the first phase of the Tainan Railway underground project. This project, planned in 1991 and approved by the Executive Yuan in 2009 to construct permanent tracks on the east side of existing rails, has a budget of NT$41.643 billion and spans 8.23 kilometers, requiring the demolition of 340 households. Residents from 323 households formed a self-help group to protest in 2012, facing successive forced demolitions between 2020 and 2021. At the opening ceremony, President Lai Ching-te apologized to the displaced residents.

---

## The Last Surface Train

On the evening of October 16, 2026, the last surface train passed through Tainan Station. The Railway Administration of the Ministry of Transportation and Communications, responsible for track construction, held a "Commencement and Housewarming" ceremony outside the station. The scene was crowded with onlookers; Tainan Mayor Huang Wei-che, various non-partisan legislators, and President Lai Ching-te were all in attendance. [^1]

In his speech, Lai Ching-te noted that Tainan had previously been split in two by the railway, hindering urban development. He stated that after undergrounding, not only will all level crossing accidents be eliminated, but a green corridor approximately 30 meters wide will be created on the surface, achieving "urban sewing." He also mentioned that Tainan Station has become a new case of coexistence between old and new: "the surface is a historical monument, while the underground is a new international-standard station." [^1]

During the same ceremony, he apologized to the displaced households: "I am grateful to the residents along the railway line for their sacrifice. I apologize once again; throughout this construction process, the city government and the Ministry of Transportation have done their best to readjust the engineering details to minimize impact, but we were still unable to 100% preserve every resident's beloved home." [^1]

Regarding the opening date, two versions exist in reports: _The Reporter_ records the first phase opening on October 17, while _Vision Urban Studies_ writes October 18. What is certain is that the first phase went into service in mid-October 2026, and the entire project is not yet complete. [^1][^2]

## 8.23 Kilometers and NT$41.2 Billion

The concept for this railway is not new. The Tainan Railway undergrounding was planned in 1991 and approved by the Executive Yuan in 2009. [^1]

This section of track spans 8.23 kilometers, crossing three administrative districts: North District, East District, and Rende District in Tainan City. [^1] The currently approved budget is NT$41.643 billion. [^2]

The undergrounding aims to address level crossings and the city being severed by the railway. The project is expected to eliminate 9 level crossings, 8 underpasses, 4 overpasses, and 2 railway culverts—a total of 23 traffic nodes. Additionally, two new underground commuter stations, Linsen Station and South Tainan Station, will be added. [^2]

Tainan Station itself consists of two levels: the old station is a historical monument preserved in situ, while the new station structure is located underground, including concourses and platforms, with signage provided in Chinese, English, Japanese, and Korean. [^2]

## The Eastward Shift Plan and 340 Households

The point of contention lies in "where the railway should be built."

The original plan was to only lease residential properties on the east side of the railway and return the land to the residents upon completion of the project. However, considering budget distribution and land development interests, the Executive Yuan approved the "Eastward Shift Version" in 2009: constructing permanent underground tracks on the east side of the existing rails and permanently expropriating the residential land on the east side. [^1]

This plan affected 340 households. In August 2012, 323 of these households formed the "Anti-Southern Railway Eastward Shift Self-Help Group" to oppose the expropriation. [^1]

The alternative proposal suggested by the self-help group was to place the permanent tracks beneath the existing tracks, using the land on the east side for temporary tracks during construction and returning the land once completed. The Tainan City Government argued that the plan approved by the Executive Yuan was intended to be constructed on the east side of the existing railway from the beginning, rather than a sudden "eastward shift" after approval. [^2]

In other words, the dispute was not about "whether to underground the railway," but rather "what method should be used to complete the undergrounding and how much private land is required for the public interest." [^2]

## Resettlement Housing and 70% Agreement

In 2013, the Tainan City Government planned to build specialized resettlement housing near South Tainan Station. The resettlement housing is located in the East District's sub-center redevelopment zone, about four kilometers from the city center, and was completed in 2018. [^1]

Following its completion, residents began moving in sequentially. In September 2018, approximately 70%, or over 200 households, agreed to the demolition and moved into the resettlement housing. [^1]

However, moving into resettlement housing did not equate to receiving a free home. One displaced resident, Chen Chuan-pei (陳秌沛), received NT$3.9 million in expropriation compensation but carried a NT$5.5 million loan when moving into the resettlement housing. [^1]

## Three Years of Forced Demolition

In April 2020, the Kaohsiung High Administrative Court ruled that the urban planning for the Southern Railway eastward shift met the criteria of public interest and necessity, ruling against the residents. The number of self-help group members dwindled from 50 to 9. [^1]

Following the legal defeat, forced demolitions began. In June 2020, after being cut off from water and electricity, the residence of Chang Wen-yao on Qianfeng Road was forcibly demolished by the Railway Administration in the early morning; this was the first case of forced demolition for the Southern Railway project. [^1]

In July and October of the same year, the residence of Huang Chun-hsiang on Kaiyuan Road faced forced demolition twice. The Huang residence was a "semi-demolished" house; the rear half adjacent to the railway was excavated, and the stairs from the second floor upward were destroyed, but because supporters and students blocked the demolition, it was only partially completed. Huang Chun-hsiang and her supporters traveled north to the Ministry of Transportation and the Executive Yuan six times to express their desire to preserve the stairs and maintain the living functionality of the remaining space. [^1]

In August 2021, the Huang residence faced its third forced demolition attempt. Approximately 60 supporters gathered to block the demolition the night before; the next morning, after 300 police officers surrounded the area and removed Huang Chun-hsiang and her supporters, the demolition of the final household was completed. [^1]

Another record states: between 2019 and 2020, the Railway Administration carried out successive forced demolitions of surface structures; on October 13, 2020, the last resident who had not yet completed the handover process was also forcibly demolished. [^2] The two timelines differ regarding the timing of the "last household," so this article presents both without making a judgment.

## Rulings and Aftermath

In 2022, the Supreme Administrative Court dismissed the residents' final appeal. The ruling held that the relevant urban planning had considered various options, public and private interests, and engineering objectives, and there was no flaw in the balancing of interests sufficient to be deemed illegal; the court also pointed out that judicial review examines whether urban planning is legal, rather than the court choosing which engineering scheme an administrative agency should adopt. [^2]

The ruling established the legal validity of the urban plan, but the loss of livelihood and emotional disputes caused by the demolition did not disappear with the verdict.

## After the Opening

Opening is not the end. Following the first phase of undergrounding, the engineering team must still demolish the existing surface tracks and proceed with the second phase and landscape engineering. Linsen Station is expected to open in 2029, South Tainan Station in 2030, and the overall project timeline listed by the Railway Administration extends to March 2031. [^2]

The surface corridor will be converted into a green corridor approximately 30 meters wide, providing space for walking, cycling, and recreation. For Tainan, this offers the chance to reconnect the East District and West-Central District, which were severed by the railway. [^2]

But for those who were displaced, the meaning of the opening is different. Two-thirds of Huang Chun-hsiang's house was demolished; over the past few years, she and a neighbor skilled in plumbing and electricity have slowly rebuilt the stairs, with the tiling and painting done by her own hands. Regarding the President's apology, she said: "What use is an apology? The homes of our entire row of residents were demolished!" [^1]

Another displaced resident, Chen Chuan-pei, was left with only 9.8 _ping_ (approx. 32 square meters) of buildable land after part of his family home was expropriated, making reconstruction difficult. His family moved into the resettlement housing carrying a NT$5.5 million loan, with NT$4.5 million remaining on the mortgage. He said: "The fact is, we cannot change anything." [^1]

The city has been sewn together. The cost fell on a few people, and those people still live in the same city.
