Over the past five years (from the 107th to the 112th academic year), the number of high school students choosing vocational tracks has decreased by approximately 115,000, a decline of 38.09%.[^1]

During the same period, the total number of high school and vocational students decreased by 146,000; the proportion of students in professional tracks fell from 41.7% to 38.7%.[^1]

## The 115,000-Student Gap

These three figures come from a 2024 Control Yuan investigation report (113 教調 0048). The premise of the report is stated bluntly: vocational education was once the cornerstone of Taiwan's economic growth, but with the recent severe labor shortage in Taiwan, the number of technical high school students has still plummeted by approximately 220,000 over the last decade. This figure of 220,000 appears alongside the 115,000 figure in the same report, though the report does not explain the difference between the two.[^1]

The report also does not clarify the specific methodology behind the 115,000 and 38.09% figures. Figure 2 shows that students in professional tracks dropped from 291,000 to 216,000.[^1]

Both sets of numbers point in the same direction. With the proportion of professional track students falling below 40%, it means that for every 10 high school and vocational students, fewer than four remain in vocational tracks.[^1]

## High School vs. Vocational Ratio: From 7:3 to 1:1

The report outlines the evolution of Taiwan's high school-to-vocational structural ratio: in the 1950s and 60s, it was 4:6; in the 1970s, it was 6:4; in the 1980s, it was 7:3; from the 1990s to the 2000s, it returned to 5:5; and during the 12-year compulsory education period, it was adjusted to 6:4.

The actual figures for the 112th academic year are 49.56% for academic high schools and 50.44% for vocational high schools—nearly a 1:1 ratio.[^1]

```tw-bars
Number of High School and Vocational Students in Academic Years 107 and 112 (Persons)
Academic Year 107 | 745,576 | Academic High School 327,758 / Vocational High School 417,818
Academic Year 112 | 599,920 | Academic High School 297,347 / Vocational High School 302,573
Source: Control Yuan 2024 Investigation Report (113 教調 0048), Table 2
```

Comparing the two years side-by-side: academic high school students decreased from 327,758 to 297,347, while vocational high school students decreased from 417,818 to 302,573. Vocational high schools lost 115,245 students, while academic high schools lost 30,411.[^1]

An analysis by the Audit Office also noted that for the first time in the 106th academic year, the number of full-time academic high school students exceeded that of vocational students.[^1]

## The Math of the Declining Birthrate

The drop in student numbers is not unique to vocational education. The report cites a June 2024 prediction from the Ministry of Education: tenth-grade students numbered 302,000 in the 102nd academic year and approximately 200,000 in the 112th academic year; it is estimated that only about 149,000 will remain in the 128th academic year.[^1]

From 302,000 to 149,000 represents a loss of 153,000 students, or a 50.8% decrease. Over the next 16 academic years, the number of tenth-grade students is expected to decrease by an average of 3,700 (2.1%) per year.[^1]

Another signal is the number of exam takers. Since the 102nd academic year, the number of students taking the Technical and Vocational Junior College Entrance Examination (統測) has been lower than those taking the General Scholastic Ability Test (學測) for the first time; the report states that "the gap is gradually widening."[^1]

In other words, while the denominator (total students) is shrinking, the proportion of that denominator choosing vocational education is also shrinking.

## Contraction in Technical Colleges and Universities

Vocational education at the higher education level is also shrinking. The number of students in technical colleges and universities was 589,496 in the 107th academic year and fell to 474,669 in the 112th academic year, a decrease of 19.48%.[^1]

The differences between disciplines are significant. The "Safety and Service" discipline saw the largest decline at 42.68%, though it only dropped from 970 to 556 students. "Food and Beverage and Hospitality Services" decreased by approximately 40,000 (-37.64%), representing the largest numerical gap. "Engineering and Industrial Engineering" remained relatively stable with a 9.01% decrease.[^1]

While the overall numbers are shrinking, the proportion of 17 disciplines has increased; for example, "Engineering and Industrial Engineering" rose from 19.30% to 21.81%, and "Agriculture" rose from 0.40% to 0.59%.[^1]

Looking at individual discipline numbers, "Business and Management" fell from 98,942 to 79,185 (-19.97%); "Information and Communication Technology" fell from 42,579 to 35,287 (-17.13%); "Architecture and Civil Engineering" remained relatively stable, moving from 13,412 to 12,804 (-4.53%). Nine disciplines saw increases, though none were large: "Education" grew from 2,182 to 2,421; "Social Welfare" grew from 19,889 to 20,511; "Agriculture" grew from 2,362 to 2,777; and the remaining six disciplines each saw increases ranging from a dozen to several hundred students.[^1]

## Academicization Over Employment

The other end of the vocational system is its output. The report points out that the higher education rate for professional track graduates reached 82.64%, an increase of approximately 3.1 percentage points over the last five years.[^1]

The report discusses this figure in the same section as "vocational academicization," noting that the trend of vocational students pursuing further education is increasing year by year.[^1]

The report also mentions that most special guidance schools are currently vocational institutions.[^1]

## Policy List

In response to these figures, the government has introduced a series of schemes over the past two decades.

The Vocational Education Act was promulgated and implemented on January 14, 2015, stipulating that the Executive Yuan should regularly convene the Vocational Education Deliberation Committee to set policy guidelines.[^1]

Earlier initiatives include the Industry-Academia Partnership Program in 2006, the first and second phases of the Vocational Education Rejuvenation Plan in 2010, and the Model University of Technology in 2013; more recently, the Expanded Industry-Academia Partnership Program 2.0 was launched in 2021. The Ministry of Labor has also been promoting "integration of certification and competency" since 2017.[^1]

## Conclusion of the Investigation Report

The report's conclusion does not endorse any single scheme, but instead lists a set of structural problems: the severe impact of the declining birthrate on vocational education; the need to reverse the perception held by students and parents; the need to improve learning motivation; the need to deepen career and vocational guidance; the need to increase graduates' willingness to work and their employment rates; the lack of effectiveness in related schemes; the ongoing difficulty in implementing the integration of certification and competency; and persistent challenges regarding teaching staff.[^1]

The report calls on relevant ministries to "collaborate on solutions" and to promptly adjust the overall development blueprint for vocational education.[^1]

This conclusion was not drawn solely from statistics. To compile the report, the Control Yuan visited 14 schools and two vocational testing centers, and on September 26, 2024, interviewed political vice ministers from the Ministry of Education, directors from the Vocational Education Department and the K-12 Education Administration, as well as officials from the Ministry of Labor, the Ministry of Economic Affairs, the Council of Indigenous Peoples, and the National Science and Technology Council.[^1]

To summarize this investigation in one sentence: the problem with vocational education is not just that student numbers are decreasing, but that while they decrease, 80% of the professional track graduates who remain choose to continue their studies rather than enter the workforce.
