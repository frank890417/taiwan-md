> **30-Second Overview:** In September 2011, six young graduates from the National Taiwan University College of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science founded Rayark Games on Beishan Road in Jinshan, Taipei, with a capital of NT$30 million and a team of 16. In January 2012, _Cytus_ was released on iOS, reaching number one on the charts in 14 countries within a month. Subsequent titles like _Deemo_ climbed the Oricon charts in Japan, _Cytus II_ won iOS Game of the Year in 2015, and _VOEZ_ became a launch title for the Nintendo Switch. Fourteen years later, Rayark has released over ten games with more than 130 million global downloads, but the keywords surrounding them have shifted from "genius" to "a pity." After struggling through eight years, _Sdorica_ will officially enter maintenance mode in February 2026; the _Cytus II_ animation, which began Kickstarter fundraising in 2015, has still not been delivered as of 2026; and following the 2020 ICE Morse code incident, Rayark modified its games to comply with the Chinese market. Aesthetics allowed them to be seen by the world, but also caused them to stumble in every field outside of music.

## What an Arcade Machine Taught Them

![Rayark Inc. official logo, the brand mark used since 2012, white text on a red background featuring the letter R](/article-images/technology/rayark-logo-2012.webp)
_Rayark Inc. official logo. Source: [English Wikipedia File:Logo of Rayark Inc.png](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Logo_of_Rayark_Inc.png). Fair use editorial commentary._

The story of Rayark begins with a failure.

In 2008, Ming-Yang You was a first-year master's student at National Taiwan University's Graduate Institute of Network Media and Information. He and two friends founded "Hypaa Studio" at the university to develop _THEIA_, a large-scale arcade touchscreen music game[^kopu]. That same year, South Korea's PENTAVISION released _DJ Max Technika_ on arcade platforms; while its gameplay was similar to _THEIA_, it completely overwhelmed them in terms of music quality, interface design, and overall polish[^pentavision-wiki].

"We thought that if the gameplay was fun, it would sell, but the market response taught us that visuals and music are vital," Ming-Yang You recalled later[^kopu].

This realization became the underlying logic for all of Rayark's subsequent products: gameplay is just the foundation; aesthetics are the moat.

After the failure of _THEIA_, Hypaa Studio did not shut down immediately. They spent two years trying again, releasing their second arcade machine, _Mozarc_, in 2010. It was a 6K falling-note music game with gameplay similar to PENTAVISION's _DJ Max_ at the time, but it utilized Hypaa's own music library[^mozarc-fandom][^bahamut-mozarc]. During its deployment period, _Mozarc_ accumulated a collection of tracks including "Bloody Purity," "Colorful Skies," "Saika," "Beyond," "Sanctity," and "Holy Knight"—song titles that would later appear in the tracklists of _Cytus_ and _Deemo_[^mozarc-fandom]. In other words, while Hypaa's two arcade machines were not commercial successes, they left behind a set of musical assets that allowed Rayark, founded two years later, to avoid building a music library from scratch.

> **💡 Did You Know?**
> A Rayark player recalled in a 2026 comment: "The art for _Mozarc_ was excellent. Unfortunately, the failure rate of the plastic button keys was too high, and when they were later changed to microswitch buttons, the tactile feel was poor, causing it to fade from the market. Hypaa actually operated this game for a very long time; it's hard to find the machines now, and you don't see anyone playing them anymore. The servers stayed up for quite a while before finally shutting down completely."[^pttweb-mozarc-comment] This is the real reason Rayark transitioned from arcades to mobile platforms: an independent team simply cannot sustain the maintenance costs of hardware.

Three years later, in September 2011, the team founded Rayark Games on Beishan Road, Jinshan District, Taipei. With a registered capital of NT$30 million and a team of 16, they shifted their focus entirely from arcades to mobile platforms[^rayark-wiki]. The six co-founders were Chih-Yuan Chung, Ming-Yang You, Shih-Chun Chang, Chang-Yen Hsieh, Shan-Yung Yang, and Yung-Ting Lee. Five came from NTU's Department of Computer Science and Information Engineering (Shan-Yung Yang B91; Ming-Yang You and Chang-Yen Hsieh B92), and one came from the NTU Graduate Institute of Telecommunications Engineering (Chih-Yuan Chung)[^csiecomm-2013][^rayark-wiki].

> **💡 Did You Know?**
> Ming-Yang You was the only one among the six whose undergraduate studies were not in the College of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science; he studied in NTU's Department of Forest Science (B92). "I originally wanted to study Computer Science, but I didn't do well on the exams, so I ended up in Forest Science," he said in a 2012 interview[^csiecomm-2013]. Moving from Forest Science to the Graduate Institute of Network Media and Information, and then founding a music mobile game company at age 26, this cross-disciplinary path became the source of Rayark's aesthetic sensibility and unexpectedly made him the company's public spokesperson.

Chih-Yuan Chung served as CTO; years later, he led the team through a complete transition from PaaS to a Kubernetes container architecture. Rayark operates nearly fifty K8s clusters on Google Cloud, allowing "three people to maintain games with millions of users"[^ithome-rayark]. Chang-Yen Hsieh later became the producer for _Sdorica_[^sdorica-wiki]. Yung-Ting Lee served as Game Director and was one of Rayark's early company spokespersons[^bnext-rayark].

The shared background of these six colleagues from NTU's College of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science eventually became the engineering foundation for Rayark's aesthetic formula: they could fine-tune the judgment precision of music games, build cross-platform engines, and keep servers stable on the launch day of the Nintendo Switch. However, this foundation faces entirely different challenges in the realms of long-term RPG operations, animation production, and political public relations.

## Cytus and Deemo: Two Pillars of a Musical Empire

On January 12, 2012, _Cytus_ was released on iOS[^cytus-wiki].

![Cytus II gameplay screenshot showing the dynamic scanning line mechanism for note detection — the signature gameplay of the Cytus series](/article-images/technology/cytus-ii-gameplay.webp)
_The *Cytus II* version featuring the signature "dynamic scanning line" gameplay of the *Cytus* series. Source: [English Wikipedia File:Cytus II gameplay.png](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Cytus_II_gameplay.png). Fair use editorial commentary._

The gameplay is simple and intuitive, involving tapping notes following a dynamic scanning line. However, what truly made it stand out was the quality of its art and music; the sci-fi visual design combined with high-level original electronic music gave _Cytus_ a distinct identity in a highly homogenized mobile rhythm game market. Within one month of launch, it hit number one on the App Store charts in 14 countries, held the top spot on Japan's music game charts for 29 consecutive days, and reached 1 million paid downloads by April 2015[^cytus-wiki].

On November 13, 2013, _Deemo_ was released[^deemo-wiki]. If _Cytus_ is sci-fi electronic music, _Deemo_ is classical piano. Players play the piano in a black-and-white world filled with a fairy-tale atmosphere; the music shifts from electronic to classical and lyrical, and the art style moves from cold sci-fi to warm hand-drawn aesthetics.

![DEEMO Last Recital PS Vita cover, featuring the signature scene of a young girl playing piano in a black-and-white fairy-tale style room](/article-images/technology/deemo-cover.webp)
_The *Deemo Last Recital* PS Vita cover; the black-and-white fairy-tale style is the signature art of the *Deemo* series. Source: [English Wikipedia File:DeemoLastRecitalVita.jpg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:DeemoLastRecitalVita.jpg). Fair use editorial commentary._

_Deemo_ became one of Rayark's biggest commercial successes. By October 2014, it had accumulated 7 million downloads, and by the time the animation was announced in 2021, it reached 28 million downloads[^deemo-wiki]. The soundtrack sold exceptionally well in the Japanese market, winning App Store Taiwan's 2013 Best of Year for Indie Developer and Google Play Taiwan's 2014 Best Game Music. The Nintendo Switch version holds a Metacritic score of 88[^deemo-wiki]. The sequel, _DEEMO II_, launched on January 13, 2022, with 1 million players participating in pre-registration globally; it surpassed one million downloads in its first week and served as Rayark's 10th-anniversary commemorative title[^deemo2-gnn].

> **📝 Curator's Note**
> _Cytus_ and _Deemo_ established Rayark's brand formula: rhythm games × high-quality art × narrative packaging. This formula gave Rayark a unique position in the global mobile game market, but it also boxed in their subsequent attempts. Looking back fourteen years later, all of Rayark's most successful works are rhythm games, while all of their most failed attempts were efforts to apply this formula to non-music genres.

## The Year of the Switch Launch: Five Projects at Once

Between 2015 and 2018, Rayark was simultaneously advancing five product lines.

_Implosion_ was released on iOS/Android on April 8, 2015, and arrived on Nintendo Switch on July 6, 2017[^implosion-wiki]. A 3D action game, its visual quality was close to console standards for mobile games at the time. Using a paid download model on the App Store with no in-app purchases, it earned a Metacritic score of 93, reached the #7 spot in the App Store Top Grossing charts within one week of launch, and maintained a 91.9% five-star rating[^implosion-wiki]. It won 2015 iOS Game of the Year Asia, the 2015 Bahamut ACG Creative Awards, and the 2014 Unity Awards Golden Cube[^implosion-wiki].

In June 2016, _VOEZ_ was released on mobile. The following year, on March 3, 2017, _VOEZ_ became one of the launch titles for Nintendo Switch; Rayark was one of the few non-Nintendo first-party rhythm game developers on the Switch launch lineup[^voez-4gamers]. By early 2017, global downloads had surpassed 10 million[^voez-4gamers]. _Nintendo Life_ gave it an 8/10, calling it "the Switch's secret weapon"[^voez-nintendo-life].

![Cover art for the Nintendo Switch version of VOEZ, featuring its signature watercolor visual style with a dual-person mirror composition](/article-images/technology/voez-switch-cover.webp)
_Cover art for *VOEZ* on Nintendo Switch (published by Flyhigh Works)—a March 3, 2017, Switch launch title. Source: [English Wikipedia File:SwitchVOEZ.jpg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:SwitchVOEZ.jpg). Fair use editorial commentary._

On January 18, 2018, _Cytus II_ was released, serving as the sequel to the original _Cytus_ six years later[^cytus2-gematsu]. The new system pushed rhythm gamers' precision to its limits, eventually accumulating over 18 million global downloads. In May 2026, Rayark announced that _Cytus II_ would be released for Nintendo Switch 2 / Switch in 2027, featuring over 500 tracks[^cytus2-gematsu][^cytus2-qooapp].

During these years, the _Cytus_ IP also expanded into derivative works. In November 2014, the multimedia musical _Code: Cytus_, commissioned by the Quanta Education Foundation, was performed for four shows in Taiwan[^cytus-wiki], bringing mobile game tracks to the theater. In February 2015, Rayark collaborated with Japan's Capcom to release an arcade version titled _Cytus: Omega_. It was unveiled at JAEPO 2015 and began location testing in July, but this collaborative version was ultimately canceled on March 30, 2018[^cytus-wiki]. A comment from data*pythoness in May 2026—"Cytus also thought about an arcade version. I was lucky enough to experience it live; back then, I was really looking forward to a large-scale rhythm game cabinet from Taiwan. It's a pity it later faded away silently"[^threads-cytus-arcade-comment]—provides the most direct player perspective on this derivative project. On April 25, 2019, \_Cytus α* was released on Nintendo Switch, adding two new chapters and an online Battle Score mode, earning a Metacritic score of 81[^cytus-wiki]. This marked the first time the _Cytus_ series officially arrived on a home console.

_Implosion_ proved that Rayark's technical capabilities were sufficient to cross genres. However, the business models for action games and rhythm games are completely different: rhythm games rely on continuous song pack updates to maintain revenue, whereas action games are typically one-time purchases.

Then came _Sdorica_ on April 19, 2018[^sdorica-wiki].

## Stepping Out of the Comfort Zone: Eight Years of Sdorica

![Sdorica game logo, aurora themed character illustration "aurora" subtitle version](/article-images/technology/sdorica-logo.webp)
_The \_Sdorica_ game logo (aurora subtitle version). Source: [English Wikipedia File:Sdorica logo.png](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Sdorica_logo.png). Fair use editorial commentary.\_

_Sdorica_ was Rayark's boldest experiment.

The project began in 2013, entered development in 2014, and was originally scheduled for a 2016 launch. It was delayed to 2017, then again to April 19, 2018, when the official open beta finally launched. Following a five-year development cycle, pre-registrations exceeded 1.5 million people; downloads surpassed 2.5 million on April 30, and broke 5 million on May 11[^sdorica-wiki]. After launch, daily active users exceeded 1 million.

It is a mobile RPG telling the story of heroes and "soul-infusing" in the world of Myriad. Chang-yen Hsieh served as the producer[^sdorica-wiki][^moegirl-sdorica]. Rayark applied the art budget from their rhythm games to create RPG character illustrations and applied their narrative expertise to the character progression system. However, they quickly discovered that the "muscles" required for a rhythm game's playlist are entirely different from those required for an RPG's content update cycle.

Problems erupted one after another following the launch. Issues included underestimating player counts on servers, gacha events not opening on schedule, Chinese character corruption in the Dragon Boat Festival version, and failure to distribute Remnant Powder[^sdorica-udn]. Between June and July 2018, Rayark's operational decisions faced intense backlash from players, including unannounced adjustments to character stats and controversies over high-priced bundles. Within four months, the company slid from its peak, marking a turning point for Rayark's reputation[^sdorica-bahamut-policy].

Among these, the one that left the deepest impression on the veteran player community was the **Black Pongji incident**. Black Pongji was an early popular character in Myriad; paired with Angelia SP, it was the strongest team composition at the time, and players had invested massive resources into this setup. After Rayark introduced a "zombie" mechanic (enemies can counterattack even after death) and limited Black Pongji's "taunt" to once per turn, this core team was effectively rendered useless[^sdorica-blackpongji]. Rayark claimed this was a bug fix rather than a nerf, but players did not accept it. The change occurred shortly after the release of a new skin for the character "Bai Pang," leading veteran players to view it as a predatory business tactic: **selling skins first and then nerfing old characters to force players to switch to new ones**[^sdorica-blackpongji][^pttweb-rayark]. "Quitting because of the Black Pongji nerf" became a common farewell among _Sdorica_ veteran player guilds. One PTT user commented in 2026: "In the early days of _Sdorica_, many players in top-tier guilds quit because of the Black Pongji nerf"[^pttweb-rayark-thread].

> **💡 Did you know?**
> Rayark has rarely missed the mark with rhythm or action games, but they stumbled with an RPG that required long-term live operations. The core competency of rhythm and buy-to-play games is "making a great product," whereas the core competency of RPG operations is "continuously serving a community." These require entirely different muscles.

Eight years later, on February 11, 2026, Rayark officially announced that _Sdorica_ would enter maintenance mode[^sdorica-gachago][^sdorica-bahamut-policy]:

> Main story chapters, special events, and limited-time bundles will all cease. The final two characters, Valdrir DR and Chiyuki DR, will be distributed via in-game mail to all players or obtainable through the 《Spring Water Intoxication》 event rerun. Special soul-infusing events will continue to rerun until July 2026; no new reruns will be available after the final one concludes. The servers will continue to operate for the foreseeable future.

"The active development lifecycle of _Sdorica_ has ended"—this was Rayark's own declaration that their battle in the RPG arena was over[^sdorica-gachago]. For the core players who had stayed for years, this announcement was more cruel than a sudden server shutdown: the servers remain open and old characters can still be leveled, but new stories will never come again. Rayark itself understood the cost of this decision: eight years of plot, eight years of character design, and an eight-year accumulated worldview would now enter a state of freeze, leaving players to decide for themselves how much longer they wish to stay.

For Rayark in early 2026, putting _Sdorica_ into maintenance mode was a necessary way to cut losses. For eight years, _Sdorica_ had continuously consumed Rayark's R&D resources and blurred the clarity of their brand positioning. A company founded on "making the most beautiful rhythm games" was being held back by an RPG it could no longer sustain; every year of delaying the shutdown made the company's overall narrative harder to justify. The side effect of the maintenance mode announcement may be that the _Cytus_ and _DEEMO_ series will once again become the clear pillars of the Rayark brand.

## Cracks Before the Morse Code: The 2014–2017 V.K. Incident

Rayark's earliest public relations crisis can be traced back to the V.K. plagiarism controversy, which occurred three years before the 2020 ICE incident.

In the summer of 2014, a composer named Waiting (using the pseudonym H&&D) received a demo from V.K. and discovered clearly plagiarized segments. Waiting wrote a letter to point out the issues, but V.K. refused to make revisions[^ettoday-vk-882323]. The work, titled "Crossing Through Time and Rain," had previously been rejected by Erotes Studio (the team behind 《雨港基隆》). Months later, V.K. renamed the piece "Crossing Through Time" and sold it to another client. In November 2014, it became the theme song for the mobile game 《女神聯盟》; netizens subsequently discovered that the track bore suspicious similarities to segments of the opening theme from the anime 《魔法少女小圓》[^ettoday-vk-882323][^cts-vk-201703131858055].

Upon discovering this, Rayark issued an official statement: "We have decided that until this controversy concludes, we will no longer include new music by V.K. in Rayark's games"[^cts-vk-201703131858055]. This was a measure taken at the level of music licensing; following the release of the _Deemo_ PS Vita version in 2015, no further tracks by V.K. were added.

The problem was that physical/offline activities did not stop in tandem. On May 14, 2016, Rayark continued to invite V.K. to participate in a concert in Shanghai; on February 4, 2017, they invited V.K. again to participate in a Rayark "Creation" lecture[^ettoday-vk-882323]. **Despite being aware of the plagiarism controversy, they continued various forms of public collaboration for three consecutive years**—this was the breaking point that finally led Waiting to go public.

On March 11, 2017, Waiting exposed the situation online, stating that "plagiarism in music and illustration should not be tolerated"[^ettoday-vk-882323]. The following day, March 12, Rayark issued a public apology on Facebook[^cts-vk-201703131858055]:

> Following the occurrence of said incident, Rayark did not comprehensively cease all physical/offline collaborations with V.K... In the future, we will adopt the same standard as our music licensing: until the controversy concludes, we will not collaborate in any form.

Waiting and Rayark founder Tony were middle school classmates[^plurk-waiting]. This personal friendship made the incident particularly painful. Waiting later publicly announced the termination of their collaboration and signed a "non-use of works" declaration[^disp-bbs-vk], ending the two-year aftermath of the incident.

> **💡 Did You Know?**
> In its 2017 apology statement, Rayark explicitly admitted that it "did not comprehensively cease all physical/offline collaborations with V.K." The value of this statement lies in how clearly it defined the shape of the problem: they knew there was a mistake, but because V.K. was a long-term collaborator and the business relationship was difficult to sever, they split the handling into two layers: "cease music licensing, continue physical activities." This was the first time a crack appeared in the logic of "brand purity"—when brand narrative conflicts with business relationships, Rayark chose to prioritize the business relationship for three years.

While the V.K. incident was not a political landmine, it represented the first conflict between the logic of "aesthetic commitment" and commercial interests. In the handling of the 2020 ICE Morse code incident, one can see the same decision-making structure, though the cost was higher this time because the opponent was the Chinese market.

## Morse Code 1344 7609 2575

In March 2020, Rayark's Music Director ICE (real name Wilson Lam, a Hong Kong musician who joined Rayark in 2014) released the album 《Consciousness》 under his own name, including the song 〈Telegraph : 1344 7609 2575〉 on his personal SoundCloud and YouTube[^silentblue-wilson-lam][^itechpost-cytus2]. The numbers "1344 7609 2575" in the song title are Chinese Morse code.

On July 17, 2020, Chinese netizens uncovered the track and discovered that the decoded Morse code was: **"Hong Kongers, add oil! Liberate Hong Kong, revolution [of our] era."**[^udn-ice][^newtalk-ice]

The following day, July 18, Ice announced his resignation from Rayark via a personal statement. The verbatim text reads:

> Regarding the controversy caused by the music I released in March 2020, which has led to much discussion among netizens. Since this was my private activity and does not fall within my scope of work nor is it related to Rayark Games, I have decided to resign and will be leaving Rayark Games effective immediately[^newtalk-ice].

On the same day, the Chinese distributor Longyuan Network and Rayark Games issued a joint announcement stating that 《Cytus II》 would be removed from shelves in China for rectification[^gnn-ice]. Taiwanese players expressed dissatisfaction on Rayark's fan page, questioning whether the company was conducting ideological censorship; as of July 20, Rayark had still not issued an official explanation on its Taiwanese platforms[^techorange-ice]. In May 2021, Longyuan and Rayark officially announced that following rectification, six tracks composed by ICE would be deleted. They promised compensation to players who had already paid, and the Chinese server of 《Cytus II》 was restored to sale[^4gamers-cytus2-ice-removal].

After leaving Rayark, ICE continued to create as an individual. In July 2022, he publicly revealed his true identity; he is currently active under the name **Ice@Spaceport Productions**, producing trance and hardcore tracks, and continuously releasing new works on Facebook and X[^namuwiki-ice][^facebook-iceloki][^x-iceloki]. In a comment section from May 2026, a reader of Rayark noted: "Ice is still continuing to release songs; it's just that his name is a difficult keyword to use for recognition" [^pttweb-rayark-thread]. Two facts hold true simultaneously: he has not disappeared, and Rayark cannot erase him from history.

This is the Rayark version of a "political landmine" incident, and it marks the second time the logic of "aesthetic commitment" has clashed with commercial interests. During the VK克 incident three years ago, Rayark chose to prioritize commercial relationships; this time, they faced an even larger market, but the decision-making structure remained exactly the same.

One year prior, in 2019, [Red Candle Games](/en/technology/red-candle-games)'s 《Devotion》 was removed from the Steam China region on February 23 due to a talisman incident; three days later, Red Candle announced its complete removal from all of Steam[^cedargames-wiki]. Subsequently, the publisher, Shanghai Jumiao Information Technology, was investigated by the Yangpu District Market Supervision Administration, and its business license was revoked on June 17[^cedargames-wiki]. **The party that truly paid the price was the distribution end**; Red Candle protected the original content without yielding, but the publishing company was eliminated. In the 2020 ICE incident, Rayark chose a different path: letting the individual leave, modifying the game to comply, and preserving the Chinese distribution channel. Prioritizing commercial relationships remains the same logic seen in the VK克 incident.

> **⚠️ Controversial Perspectives**
> This represents two starkly different choices made by two iconic Taiwanese indie teams when facing pressure from the Chinese market. Red Candle did not yield on content, and the publishing company paid the price of being eliminated; Rayark yielded on content to preserve its Chinese distribution channels. The English media outlet PocketGamer.biz reported this with the headline "China pulls Cytus II from the App Store for secret pro-democracy Morse code message"[^pocketgamer-cytus2], while discussions in the Chinese-speaking world focused on whether "Rayark knelt / whether the separation was correct." One event, two interpretations. Rayark's choice this time is an extension of the same brand logic applied to a different source of pressure as their 2014–2017 choice regarding VK克.

## Implosion: Eleven Years of Zero Day

![Implosion Never Lose Hope game poster, mecha action scene with App Store / Google Play availability info](/article-images/technology/implosion-cover.webp)
_Promotional poster for 《Implosion: Never Lose Hope》— a 3D mecha action game that won 2015 iOS Game of the Year Asia. Source: [English Wikipedia File:Implosion- Never Lose Hope - cover.png](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Implosion-_Never_Lose_Hope_-_cover.png). Fair use editorial commentary._

Some promises are uglier than silence.

On December 5, 2015, Rayark launched a Kickstarter campaign for the animated film 《Implosion: Zero Day》, aiming to raise US$400,000 for a 90-minute animation[^inside-zero-day]. Within 48 hours of launch, it reached 18% of its goal, and after 10 days, it surpassed NT$5 million[^implosion-zero-day-wiki]. Completion was originally scheduled for August 2018.

In 2018, nothing was delivered.

On January 24, 2019, following a third delay announcement, Rayark admitted that "the IZD animated film cannot be completed in 2018 as expected" and provided refund options: backers could choose to receive a refund while still receiving the rewards corresponding to their pledge tier[^disp-bbs-implosion]. Refunds were tallied in February 2019, and the first batch of goods (hoodies, T-shirts, collector's edition movie scripts, game soundtracks, manga chapters, and movie wallpapers) was sent out in March. The full set of rewards was only to be sent after the film's completion.

From 2020 to 2026, there were five years of silence. Rayark's official 《Implosion》 page and the English Wikipedia entry for _Implosion_ still maintain a status of "animation in progress," with no release date provided[^implosion-wiki]. Kickstarter backers worldwide have endured an eleven-year wait, yet as of 2026, the animation has still not been seen.

"They don't even dare to speak about the film's progress; they can't even provide half a schedule," one backer wrote under the 2019 delay announcement[^disp-bbs-implosion].

This is the only project where Rayark truly "failed to deliver" on a promise. More serious than the operational cracks in _Sdorica_ is this long-term silence toward global Kickstarter backers. However, media attention for this matter in the Chinese-speaking world has been far lower than that of the ICE incident, the _Sdorica_ maintenance mode, or the AI art controversy. This may be because the Chinese-speaking indie game industry is more tolerant of such delays, or perhaps because the consequences of the delay are ultimately borne individually by all the backers from that 2015 Kickstarter funding.

## Those Still Present

Rayark Video Games Co., Ltd. as of May 2026: approximately 270 employees, capital of NT$64 million, headquartered at No. 47 Dongxing Road, Xinyi District, Taipei City. Since 2012, they have released over ten games with cumulative global downloads exceeding 130 million[^rayark-104]. They have a branch, Rayark Japan, in Tokyo; the company is not publicly listed.

Their fourteen-year portfolio is far more extensive than what was mentioned earlier in this article. Beyond the five main lines—the _Cytus_ series, _Deemo_ series, _Implosion_, _VOEZ_, and _Sdorica_—Rayark has also released: _Mandora_ (2012, casual puzzle) [^rayark-wiki], _DEEMO ~Final Finale~_ (2015, PS Vita music rhythm) [^deemo-wiki], _DEEMO -Reborn-_ (2019–2020, PS4/Switch/Steam music adventure, "Very Positive" on Steam reviews) [^steamcharts-deemo-reborn], _MO:Astray_ (2019–2020, Steam/Switch/Mobile action puzzle) [^udn-moastray], _Soul of Eden_ (August 26, 2020, card strategy, developed for over 4 years, once noted for having mechanics similar to _Clash Royale_[^bnext-59103]), _Chaos Academy_ (2021, tactical card game) [^rayark-wiki], and _DEEMO II_ (January 13, 2022, reached 1 million pre-registrations globally, one million downloads in the first week) [^deemo2-gnn]. Over fourteen years, they have produced 10+ works spanning five platforms: mobile, Switch, PS4, PC, and PS Vita.

> **💡 Did you know?**
> In Rayark's portfolio, only the _Cytus_ series and _Deemo_ series maintain "Cytus-level brand recognition" as music game mainstays. While every other independent work (_Implosion_, _VOEZ_, _MO:Astray_) is of high quality, it is difficult for readers to identify them as "a Rayark game" in a single breath—when your brand becomes synonymous with "aesthetic rhythm games," expanding into other genres can actually dilute brand recognition. Looking back over fourteen years, Rayark's strongest curatorial ability lies in the IP extension of _Cytus_ and _Deemo_ (musicals, soundtracks, arcade collaborations, animated films), rather than the development of new genres.

Timeline of the past three years:

- May 2023: Rayark AI art controversy. Rumors circulated online regarding "full AI creation + firing artists"; on May 27, Rayark issued a clarification via Twitter stating they "did not use AI to create the vast amount of imagery required for games, nor did they fire their art team because of it" [^rayark-twitter-ai][^gnn-rayark-ai].
- November 30, 2023: Hao Lei Coffee ceased operations.
- October 14, 2024: Rayark Concept online store ended operations[^rayark-concept-fb].
- February 11, 2026: _Sdorica_ entered maintenance mode.
- May 22, 2026: Announced that _Cytus II_ will be released for Nintendo Switch 2 / Switch in 2027[^cytus2-gematsu].

In comparison to rhythm game companies of the same era, PENTAVISION—the company that caused Hypaa Studio's crushing defeat in 2008—took a completely different path. Due to financial difficulties, it liquidated and closed in 2012, being fully absorbed by its parent company, Neowiz[^pentavision-wiki]. In 2017, Neowiz revived the series with _DJMax Respect_; by December 2024, _DJMax Respect V_ surpassed 6 million downloads, and in 2025, Neowiz's revenue hit an all-time high[^digitaltoday-neowiz].

PENTAVISION survived by "being absorbed by a large corporation and then being revived." Rayark chose the opposite path: remaining independent, expanding genres, and absorbing the cost of every mistake themselves.

Rayark's situation is the most typical dilemma faced by successful independent game developers in Taiwan: while the first product explodes through passion and talent, subsequent products require organizational capability and operational discipline. The market ceiling for rhythm games dictates that Rayark must expand into other genres, but every expansion dilutes its original brand identity. The players of _Cytus_ and the players of _Sdorica_ are entirely different demographics; serving both groups under the same brand name may result in both groups feeling that "Rayark has changed."

Yet, Rayark remains one of the few brands in Taiwan's gaming industry with global name recognition. With 18 million downloads for _Cytus II_, 28 million downloads for the _Deemo_ series, _Implosion_ winning iOS Game of the Year, _VOEZ_ launching on Switch, and the _DEEMO_ animated film produced by Production I.G and Signal.MD featuring a theme song by Yuki Kajiura—these achievements remain unmatched by any other company in Taiwan's mobile gaming market as of 2026[^deemo-movie-rayark].

In 2008, Yu Ming-yang learned from an unsold arcade machine that "aesthetics are important." Eighteen years later, this sentence remains Rayark's greatest asset, and perhaps its greatest shackle: when you define yourself as "the person who makes the most beautiful games," the market's tolerance for error drops to zero.

✦ Aesthetics allowed Rayark to be seen by the world, but they also caused it to stumble in every non-musical arena.

The eight-year operational rift in _Sdorica_ stems from the fundamental conflict between the formula of "Art × Music × Story" and the nature of long-term RPG service. Every event update requires high-quality new character illustrations, and every story expansion requires original music; while this was manageable for a 16-person team with NT$30 million in capital, it became a systemic time black hole within the rhythm of a game with hundreds of thousands of daily active users. The quality standard Rayark built through rhythm games—"polished until recognized at a glance"—clashed fundamentally with the content speed of mobile RPGs, where "players consume a major update in an average of three days."

The core issue behind the eleven-year delay of the _Implosion_ animation is the collision between the standard of "making the most beautiful animation" and the execution limits of an independent company. Animation production at the level of Production I.G requires organized production pipelines and a stable, long-term writing team. When Rayark promised a 90-minute animation on Kickstarter, they used their "game development muscles" to make a promise to the animation industry. While Rayark has the technical threshold to produce _Implosion_ animation, the difficulty lies in the total cost of "producing it and maintaining it until release"—a scale an independent company of this size cannot afford.

The ICE incident demonstrates the inevitable result when the logic of "brand purity" is extended to employees. Rayark's entire brand is built on a narrative of "aesthetic-driven, politically neutral, and globally compatible." When an employee released a song supporting Hong Kong outside of work, the internal consistency of that brand narrative was challenged. Rayark's choice—allowing the employee to resign, modifying the game to comply with the Chinese market, and retaining Chinese distribution channels—was the optimal solution for this brand logic, serving as a counterpoint to another indie path (that of Red Candle Games).

What will the next Rayark look like? Perhaps there won't be one; perhaps it will require different muscles. When aesthetics are everything, every non-aesthetic choice becomes a crack, including politics, operations, and time. But Rayark's fourteen-year trajectory also proves one thing: Taiwanese indie game teams have the ability to secure a scarce brand position in the global rhythm game market—a feat achieved in Asia only by Japan's BEMANI system and Korea's _DJ Max_ series. Rayark's story is not over; the day _Cytus II_ launches on Nintendo Switch 2 in 2027 will be the next milestone to test whether an "aesthetic empire" can continue to define "made-by-Taiwan" in the age of AI.
